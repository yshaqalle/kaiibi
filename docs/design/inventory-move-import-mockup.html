Missing categories, a second store's import, and moving stock by spreadsheet

Three reported bugs and one new job. The bugs are not what they look like from the outside — each traces to a specific line, and the diagnosis changes what the fix should be. The new job, moving stock by spreadsheet, is the one that needs a design decision from you.

  3. Typing a quantity when moving stock — the stepper is +1 forty times

1 · Categories stop at ten

It isn't a cap. There is no limit anywhere in the code.

listCategories() reads the whole categories table with no .limit(), and the POS row renders every name it gets. The nine chips in your screenshot are simply all nine rows that exist.

The categories table is only ever written from two places: the Add button in Settings, and the product form when someone types a new category by hand. The CSV importer writes products.category as free text and never registers the name. So every category that arrived by import is real on the products, filters correctly in Inventory search — and has no chip in POS, because no row was ever created for it.

Two halves to the fix.

Going forward: runProductsImport upserts each distinct accepted category (and brand — brands.ts has the identical gap) through the existing createCategory, which is already an ignore-duplicates upsert and safe to call redundantly.

For the shops already affected: a migration that inserts, per shop, every distinct non-empty products.category with no matching categories row. Your imported categories reappear as chips without anyone re-typing them.

2 · An import for the second store — send it to Move stock instead

The rejection is deliberate. The advice it gives is the bug.

runProductsImport(shop.id, …) takes a shop and no store, loads every product in the shop, and rejects any row whose name or SKU already exists — "already exists — edit it in Inventory instead of importing." The rule is right: an import must never silently overwrite product data. The advice is what fails — it sends someone stocking a second store to open 214 products by hand, when the operation they want isn't an edit at all.

Product import doesn't change. It says what it's for, and points at the right tool.

Importing that catalogue a second time was never the right operation anyway: 214 rows of "Torriden Balanceful Serum, stock 40" imported as products means 40 more units now exist — when really they're the same units, at a different store. It would have inflated the shop's stock and its stock value. A move is the honest operation, and it leaves a stock_transfers record saying what went where.

So: no store picker, no new "stocked" outcome, no change to the import rules. The modal states its job in one line, and the duplicate rejection routes to Move stock.

Why no store picker is also the correct answer, not just the cheap one.

The opening-stock trigger (20260810000000_stock_by_location.sql:137) already places a newly created product's stock at the primary location, chosen by order by is_primary desc, created_at asc. Stock entering the shop lands at the primary store; moves distribute it from there. That is the database's model, and a store picker on import would have been a second, competing answer to the same question.

The cost, stated plainly: a delivery that arrives physically at Jaalala 2 is imported to Jaalala Skincare and then moved — recording a movement that didn't happen on a van. That's an accuracy cost in the transfer history, and it's the price of this scope. It's worth paying now; if it bites, the fix is a store on the opening stock, not on the importer.

4 · Move stock — by hand, or by sheet

Shops are already using Import to shuffle stock between stores, because it's the only bulk tool on the screen. So Move stock becomes the bulk tool, with two tabs doing one job: fill it in the app, or fill it in a spreadsheet. Same columns, same checks, same result. Someone who learns one already understands the other.

By handFrom a sheet

By hand for a handful of items, or when you're packing the box and want to scan them in. From a sheet for a whole store's worth. Neither is the "advanced" one.

4a · By hand — search and fill

Today the by-hand modal makes you search, tap a suggestion, watch the search clear, then tap + until the number is right — then start over for the next item. For three items that's tolerable; for fifteen it's why people reached for Import. The rebuild keeps the search but stops clearing it, and turns every result into a row you type a number into.

It's used by the inventory header, the product form, and the POS open-register sheet. Move stock should not invent a third pattern.

Two things to settle before reusing it

1. It needs an exclude prop. StoreDropdown lists every active location with no way to omit one, and TO must not offer the source — transfer_stock refuses a move to itself. Small addition, and it belongs on the component rather than in a copy of it.

It already ships in the POS open-register sheet (open-register-sheet.tsx:164 renders it at :180), so there is precedent — but precedent is not proof, and this one gets checked on a device rather than reasoned about.

Moving more than the app says is there

Worth its own answer, because the honest one is "no, and here's the thing you actually want".

The database forbids it, and not by accident.

product_location_stock.stock is declared integer not null default 0 check (stock >= 0) (20260810000000_stock_by_location.sql:50). Moving 10 out of a store holding 4 needs that store to reach −6, and the row won't accept it. Allowing over-moves means dropping that constraint — and it isn't scoped to transfers: the same column backs POS, so negative stock would become sellable, low-stock alerts would go strange, and stock value in Accounting could go below zero. That's a shop-wide change to buy a transfer-screen convenience.

But the situation behind the question is real, and it has a better answer.

Nobody wants negative stock. What they want is: "I'm holding 10 of these in my hand. The app says 4. Let me get on with it." The app's number is wrong — an unrecorded delivery, a return that never got keyed, a miscount. Blocking the move is correct; making them leave the screen to fix the count is not.

So the over-quantity row stops being a dead end and becomes a two-option question, right where it happens.

Only if you want it: an explicit stock-take import

I'd hold it back until you tell me the counts are drifting, rather than build it on spec.

Same job, when the answer is two hundred rows rather than three. Export what you have, fill in where it's going and how much, import it back.

How it commits

Rows are grouped by (From, To) pair, and each pair goes through one transfer_stock call. That is the existing RPC: both sides in one transaction, one stock_transfers record, the same movement history the by-hand modal produces. A sheet moving stock in two directions produces two transfer records, which is correct — they are two different movements and a shop reading its history should see them as such.

If a pair fails at commit time — someone sold the last unit between the check and the button — that pair fails whole and is reported by name; the other pair still goes through. The alternative, rolling back everything because one pair raced, throws away good work for a problem the user can fix by re-uploading the one sheet section. The result screen names exactly which pair didn't land.

Where it lives, and how the two tabs meet

Both tabs live inside the existing Move stock modal — the sheet is not an entry in the Import menu. Import means "bring products in", and moving stock is not that; a shop that finds moving under Import will keep expecting product import to move things, which is the confusion this whole document is fixing.

The tabs share one selection. Switching from By hand to From a sheet keeps what you filled in, and Download move sheet hands you a file with those rows already filled — so a shop that starts by hand, realises it's a bigger job than they thought, and finishes in Excel doesn't retype anything. The reverse holds too: a checked sheet lands in the by-hand list where you can adjust a number before committing.

The one rule that keeps them honest

Neither tab ever moves anything until the same button is pressed. The sheet tab doesn't commit on upload, and the by-hand tab doesn't commit as you type. Both end at Move stock, both run the same validation, and both produce the same transfer_stock call and the same movement record.

5 · What I need from you

Settled: product import doesn't change.

No store picker, no "stocked" outcome, no new import rules. The modal says what it's for, and the duplicate rejection routes to Move stock — which means the move sheet is now the only way to stock a second store in bulk, and has to be good enough to carry that on its own. The questions below are all about the sheet.

b — One sheet per download, or one row per store?

The mockup gives a product held in two stores two rows, one per store, each with its own count. It's longer, but it's the only shape where "move 5 out of Jaalala Skincare" is unambiguous. The alternative — one row with a From column you fill yourself — is shorter and more error-prone. I recommend the two-row shape shown.

b2 — "Scan to add": answered by the device, not by us.

It was built, and taken back out. The wedge-scanner path does exist, but it cannot reach inside a sheet: HardwareKeyboardModule.startCapture wraps currentActivity.window, and a React Native Modal on Android is a Dialog with its own window. Keys typed while this sheet is up are neither reported to JS nor swallowed — which is why POS and Inventory both stand their scanner down while a sheet is open. Scanning inside a modal has never worked in this app.

Shipping it anyway would have been worse than leaving it out: with nothing swallowing the scan, the trailing Enter presses whichever control holds focus. On the Pixel it closed the sheet and threw away the basket. The search box says "Search a product" again.

What it would take: teaching the module about the Dialog's window — a native change, in the code that has already cost this app three keyboard bugs, and one to make deliberately rather than in passing.

c — Should the move sheet include products with zero stock?

The mockup includes only products that have stock somewhere, since you can't move what isn't there. Including everything makes the sheet a full catalogue you could also use as a picking list. I recommend stock-holding only, and keeping Export CSV as the full-catalogue route.

d — Does the category backfill run for every shop, or only on request?

A migration that recovers missing categories shop-wide is the fix that doesn't need anyone to do anything. It's also a write to every affected shop's data at once. It only ever inserts names already present on that shop's own products, so I think it's safe to run for everyone — but it's your call.
