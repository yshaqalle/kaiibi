Task 1Done

The moving average

receive_stock replaced the cost with the newest line's price. The original migration's own comment called it “latest wins”. Buy 200 bags at $14.10 and 10 at $14.90, and all 210 are valued at $14.90 — every subsequent sale's COGS is the most recent price paid, whatever the units actually cost. That is replacement cost, and IAS 2.25 permits exactly two formulas for interchangeable goods: FIFO and weighted average. It was neither.

Before
One line 20260902000000:173
-- "latest wins" update public.products set cost_cents = v_cost, updated_at = now() where id = v_product.id;
After
Weighted average 20260907000000
-- read BEFORE the upsert, summed SHOP-WIDE select greatest(coalesce(sum(stock),0),0) into v_prior_qty from public.product_location_stock where product_id = v_product.id; -- ...upsert happens here... if v_prior_qty <= 0 or v_product.cost_cents is null then v_new_cost := v_cost; else v_new_cost := round( (v_prior_qty::numeric * v_product.cost_cents + v_qty::numeric * v_cost) / (v_prior_qty + v_qty)); end if;

The two ways to get it subtly wrong

The stock is already updated when the cost is computed.

receive_stock upserts stock at line 164 and writes the cost at line 173 — so product_location_stock.stock already includes this delivery. Averaging against it counts the received quantity twice and lands between the two costs: wrong in a way nobody would spot. The prior quantity is therefore read before the upsert, not subtracted back afterwards, which is a second place to get the sign wrong.

The cost is shop‑wide; the stock is not.

products.cost_cents is one figure per product, but stock lives per location. So the average sums stock over every location. Averaging against one branch would let the same delivery produce a different cost depending on where it landed.

All four candidate answers, separated on purpose

300 units at $10.00, then a delivery of 100 at $20.00:

VerdictCostWhat would produce it
Correct1250(300×1000 + 100×2000) / 400
Wrong1200Averaged against post‑upsert stock — delivery counted twice
Wrong2000“Latest wins” — the bug being fixed
Wrong1500A plain mean of the two costs, ignoring quantity

This took two attempts. The plan's first draft used 200 @ 1410 then 10 @ 1490 — where the correct answer and the post‑upsert bug both give 1414, so the likeliest implementation error would have passed a green test.

A product with 40 units on the shelf and no cost recorded takes a delivery of 5 at $8.00. Correct is 800. A coalesce(cost_cents, 0) implementation gives 89 — damningly different, rather than a near miss that reads as rounding.

Task 2Done

The costs that already exist

Before

No stated policy. Every products.cost_cents is the price of whatever arrived last, and nothing in the repo says what happens to those figures when the formula changes — so the question gets asked again by whoever next reads it, and answered differently.

After

Prospective, deliberately, and the migration header says why. The existing figure becomes the opening average and the formula runs from the next delivery forward.

Why there is no backfill, and cannot be.

Restating an existing cost as a weighted average needs the quantity and price of every delivery behind the current stock. stock_receipt_items only reaches back as far as receipts have been recorded — which for most shops is not far, and for stock predating the feature is nowhere at all. A backfill would therefore have to invent the missing history.

This is the ordinary treatment for a change in accounting estimate: applied prospectively, and disclosed rather than discovered — which is what task 3 puts on the screen.

It converges quickly. Each delivery pulls the average toward the true figure in proportion to its share of stock on hand, so a shop turning its stock every few weeks is on a real average within a month or two.

The disclosure

IAS 2.36(a) requires disclosing the cost formula used. With one formula that is a constant string, not a column or a toggle. Inventory is the only screen in the app that shows a stock value — and its old words were not merely undisclosed, they were accurate about an impermissible basis, which made them wrong the moment the arithmetic changed.

Named twice, on purpose.

The formula is in the tile hint and in the caveat, so dismissing the caveat does not lose the disclosure. The caveat is dismissible; the hint is not.

Tone stays context, still with no action.

House rule: wrong means the number is wrong until the reader fixes something and must carry an action. Nothing a shop can do about the costing basis from Inventory — and a wrong with no fix would train people to skip the whole family, including the uncosted warning above it, which does have one.

The shop‑wide clause survived, and changed meaning.

It was an admission before; it is a consequence of the design now. The average is taken shop‑wide precisely so the same delivery cannot produce a different cost depending on where it landed. A single‑store shop gets the sentence without that clause — verified on screen.

Check 2b lost its question, and got a better one

2b used to pin which of two lines for one product was “latest” — the ordinality tiebreaker decided it. Under an average that question has no meaning: averaging is commutative up to rounding, so order no longer changes the answer. What is still worth pinning is that both lines land.

The Restock preview started lying

The Restock sheet previews the cost write before committing it — “so the preview can say 4.50 → 4.80 before anything is written”. That arrow was true while receive_stock replaced the cost. The moment it averaged, the preview promised a figure the commit does not produce.

Stock is valued at weighted average cost, so each delivery moves a product's cost part of the way toward what you paid — by how much depends on how much you already hold. A product with no cost recorded, or none in stock, takes the delivery's price exactly.

Why “toward”, and not the computed average.

The true result needs the shop‑wide quantity at commit time, and reimplementing the weighted average in TypeScript would be a second copy of the formula — free to drift a cent from the one in the migration, on the one screen whose whole job is to promise what is about to happen. An honest direction beats a precise number that can be wrong.

The exception is stated rather than hidden: with no cost recorded, or none in stock, there is nothing to average against and the delivery's price lands exactly — so that row still renders a flat no cost → $9.00.

Unplanned CNot in the plan

The clash warning

A sheet that prices one product two ways still deserves a warning — it is usually a mistake in the sheet. But what it warned about stopped being true.

Before

…Only one cost is kept per product, so the last store to go through is the one that sticks — fix the sheet if that isn't what you meant.

True under “latest wins”: the commit order silently decided the answer.

After

…There is one cost per product, so both are averaged into it and the product ends up on a blend of the two rather than on either — fix the sheet if that isn't what you meant.

Store order no longer decides anything, so the warning describes a blend, not a race.

What did not change

No past margin moves.

costOfGoodsSold() and every item‑margin figure read sale_items.unit_cost_cents, frozen at sale time, never products.cost_cents. This migration writes only the latter. Historical margins cannot move; only sales made after it lands freeze the averaged cost.

Nothing else in the database.

No new table, no cost layers, no basis toggle, and no change to complete_sale, save_stock_count, refund_sale_items or transfer_stock. The migration defines exactly one function.

FIFO stays available, and is now an upgrade rather than a prerequisite.

The two are equals under IAS 2.25. Weighted average was chosen because under inflation FIFO draws COGS from the oldest and cheapest stock, raising reported profit and so raising tax. The FIFO design and plan are merged (#65, #68); cost layers would replace this arithmetic rather than build on it.

One thing still outstanding.

The migration exists locally only. Until it is deployed, the live app still does “latest wins”. Two migrations are pending on the remote project — this one and 20260905000000, a complete_sale deadlock fix that belongs to different work — and a push applies both.

What this unblocks

Phase 2b — posting to the ledger becomes the next work, and it can now post a COGS figure computed on a formula IAS 2 permits. That was the real blocker; it was never FIFO specifically.
