Kaiibi · design mockup · 9 Aug 2026

Help & support

One sheet, reachable from the ☰ on every screen, that turns "something is wrong and I don't know who to tell" into a ticket the platform can act on — with the shop, the person, the plan and the device already attached so nobody has to ask "which store are you?" first.

The form asks five things, not fifteen

Who you are, what plan you're on, what device you're holding and where you were standing are all already known — they get attached, not asked. The person types a category, a subject, the story, and optionally drags in a screenshot.

It is ungated

Settings in the ☰ is hidden from anyone without settings.access. Help is not. A cashier who can't ring up a sale is exactly the person who most needs to reach us, and today their ☰ contains one item: Sign out.

The open question is what happens after Send

A form that goes nowhere visible is a black hole — people send once, hear nothing, and never send again. Section 5 proposes the smallest thing that isn't a black hole. It is also the part that costs the most to build, so it is the scope decision to make before anything is planned.

Why under the ☰ and not in Settings' sidebar. Settings is a destination you go to on purpose, and it is permission-gated; support is something you reach for at the moment something breaks, from whatever screen you were on when it broke. Putting it in the ☰ means it is two taps from the POS mid-sale, which is where the bug reports that matter actually happen. A Settings row would still be worth adding later as a signpost, but it can't be the only door.

Send is off until a category, a subject and details are all there. Three required things, and the disabled button is the only thing telling you so — no red text on an untouched form. The validation message appears under whichever field is empty the first time Send is pressed.

No priority picker. Everyone chooses "urgent" — a self-declared priority field measures frustration, not impact. Urgency is inferred instead: Billing or payment and Something's broken from a shop whose POS is down are what a triage view sorts to the top (§6).

"Reply to me on" is a real choice here, not a nicety. WhatsApp is how these shops already talk to their customers — the codebase ships WhatsApp receipt sharing — and an emailed reply to a shop owner in Hargeisa may simply never be read. Defaulting to WhatsApp when a phone number is on the profile, and hiding that option when it isn't, is the difference between a reply that lands and one that doesn't.

Scope call to make

A — Form only. Submit, confirm, done; replies happen entirely over WhatsApp or email. Smallest build, no list, no thread, no unread chip. B — Form + list + read-only replies. You can see status and read what we said, but you answer over WhatsApp. C — Everything drawn above, including replying in the app. Recommendation: B — it kills the black hole for roughly a third of C's cost, and the reply box is an additive change later.

8 · Open questions

  1. How far does v1 go — A, B or C? See §5. The recommendation is B: form, list, read-only replies; answering happens over WhatsApp.
  2. Is WhatsApp really the reply channel? The whole "Reply to me on" row rests on it. If replies will only ever be email, that row shrinks to a single confirmation line and the phone number stops mattering.
  3. Do we notify, and how? The unread chip on the ☰ needs a poll or a realtime subscription. Push would need the notification infrastructure that docs/backlog/2026-08-01-notification-delivery.md says doesn't exist yet.
  4. Five categories, or fewer? Billing earns its place because payments are matched by hand. "Something else" may be swallowing what should be its own category — worth revisiting after a month of real traffic.
  5. Video attachments? A short clip is the single most useful bug attachment and the single most expensive one on a metered Somaliland connection. Proposal: allow it, cap at 10 MB, and warn before an upload over 5 MB.
