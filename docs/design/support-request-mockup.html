Kaiibi · design mockup · 9 Aug 2026

Help & support

A two-way line between a store and the people who run Kaiibi. A shop opens it from the ☰ on any screen; an operator opens it from the console and can start the conversation too. One thread either way, with the shop, the person, the plan and the device already attached so nobody has to ask "which store are you?" first.

One conversation, either end can start it

A store reporting a broken scanner and an operator saying "your ZAAD payment cleared, you're back on Growth" are the same object — a thread with messages in it. Building outbound as a separate announcement system would double the work and split the store's history across two places.

The form asks five things, not fifteen

Who you are, what plan you're on, what device you're holding and where you were standing are all already known — they get attached, not asked. The person types a category, a subject, the story, and optionally drags in a screenshot.

It is ungated

Settings in the ☰ is hidden from anyone without settings.access. Help is not. A cashier who can't ring up a sale is exactly the person who most needs to reach us, and today their ☰ contains one item: Sign out.

Now the unread count is load-bearing

When only the store could start a conversation, an unseen reply was an annoyance. Once the platform can open one, an unseen message is a message that never happened — so the chip on the ☰ stops being a nicety. Section 10 is honest about what that costs given no push infrastructure exists yet.

The count is unread messages from us — a reply, or a conversation we started. Nothing unread, no chip.

Why under the ☰ and not in Settings' sidebar. Settings is a destination you go to on purpose, and it is permission-gated; support is something you reach for at the moment something breaks, from whatever screen you were on when it broke. Putting it in the ☰ means it is two taps from the POS mid-sale, which is where the bug reports that matter actually happen. A Settings row would still be worth adding later as a signpost, but it can't be the only door.

Send is off until a category, a subject and details are all there. Three required things, and the disabled button is the only thing telling you so — no red text on an untouched form. The validation message appears under whichever field is empty the first time Send is pressed.

No priority picker. Everyone chooses "urgent" — a self-declared priority field measures frustration, not impact. Urgency is inferred instead: Billing or payment and Something's broken from a shop whose POS is down are what a triage view sorts to the top (§6).

"Reply to me on" is a real choice here, not a nicety. WhatsApp is how these shops already talk to their customers — the codebase ships WhatsApp receipt sharing — and an emailed reply to a shop owner in Hargeisa may simply never be read. Defaulting to WhatsApp when a phone number is on the profile, and hiding that option when it isn't, is the difference between a reply that lands and one that doesn't.

The second dropdown is optional in every case. It sharpens triage and it is genuinely useful — "broken, POS" and "broken, payroll" go to different places — but a person whose till is frozen must never be blocked by a field about taxonomy. Category, subject and details stay the only three required things.

Why the app list mirrors the nav. POS, Inventory, People, Accounting, Settings are the words already on the sidebar, so nobody has to translate what they were looking at into our vocabulary. "Signing in" and "Receipts & printing" are added because both are outside the nav and both generate support.

Identical to a thread they started — because it is one. The only difference is the From Kaiibi mark and which message sits at the top.

One store at a time, not a broadcast

"Message every store on Starter" is a different feature with different failure modes — an announcement nobody can reply to, sent 200 times, that turns the unread chip into noise people learn to dismiss. The recipient row above takes multiple stores because the field naturally does, but v1 caps it low and there is no plan filter. If a real broadcast is wanted, it should be designed as one.

Reachable from Stores too

An operator looking at a store in ShopDrawer and noticing something wrong shouldn't have to go to another tab and search for the store they're already looking at. A Message this store button in the drawer opens the same composer with the recipient filled in.

PieceShapeNotes
Menu rowThree edits — admin-sidebar.tsx, admin-tabs.web.tsx, admin-tabs.tsx Ungated, unlike the Settings row beside it. Carries the unread count.
The sheetsrc/components/support/support-sheet.tsx over AppModalBento surfaces: bentoSurface card at BENTO_RADIUS, bentoSoft identity strip, bentoAccentWash for the chosen category and for anything we said. No hexes.
Tablessupport_threads + support_messages A thread carries store, category, subject, status, reference, contact preference, who opened it (shop or platform), who it's addressed to, and a JSON context blob for version/platform/screen. A message carries author kind, body, and read timestamps at each end.
The taxonomycategory, area, and one area_other text column Two enum-ish columns plus the free-text capture, defined in one place (src/lib/support-taxonomy.ts) that both the store's sheet and the operator's filters read — so adding a category is one edit, not four. area is nullable; the second dropdown is never required.
Who can read whatRow-level security on both tables Store-opened: the author and operators only — a cashier's message must not be readable by their manager. Platform-opened: whoever it was addressed to, or settings.access holders when it's addressed to the store. Operators read everything.
AttachmentsA new private bucket, not product-images product-images is public and its insert policy demands inventory.edit, settings.access or staff.manage (0024, 20260820000300) — the exact permissions a stuck cashier lacks. A screenshot may also show customer names and totals, so public read is wrong on its own. Signed URLs, and operators need read access to a bucket stores can write to.
ReferenceKB-####, a sequence Short enough to read down a phone line, which is how half of these will be followed up.
Operator tabNew Support tab in src/app/platform/index.tsx + src/components/platform/support-tab.tsx Mirrors requests-tab.tsx's shape and the console's pill row. The thread panel reuses ShopDrawer's two-column idea.
Operator writescallPlatformAdmin — new actions reply_support, open_support, close_support Same path as plan approvals, so every reply and every outbound message lands in the audit log automatically.
Unread countA count query on mount plus a poll or Supabase realtime subscription The cheapest honest version. See §10 — this is the one piece with no obviously right answer.

Deliberately not in v1: broadcasts to a whole plan tier (§8), a help-article search box above the form (nothing to search yet), assignment between operators (there is one operator), CSAT ratings, canned replies, and live chat. Each is additive later; none earns its build now.

10 · Open questions

  1. How does a store find out a message arrived? The biggest one now that we can start conversations. Cheapest is a count fetched when the app opens plus a Supabase realtime subscription while it's running — no new infrastructure, but a tablet sitting on a POS all day will see it and a phone in a pocket won't. Real push would need the delivery infrastructure docs/backlog/2026-08-01-notification-delivery.md says doesn't exist yet. Proposal: realtime plus a one-line banner on the next app open, and accept that anything genuinely urgent still goes out over WhatsApp.
  2. Is WhatsApp really the reply channel? The "Reply to me on" row and the operator's "they asked for WhatsApp" caveat both rest on it. Worth confirming: does Kaiibi actually send from a support number today, or is that aspirational? If it's aspirational the row still works — it just records a preference nobody acts on yet, which is worse than not asking.
  3. Can a store reply to an announcement? As drawn, yes — every thread has a reply box. That's right for "your payment cleared" and wrong for "we're down for maintenance tonight" sent to fifty stores. If broadcast ever lands (§8), it needs a reply-disabled thread type, and that decision is easier to make now than to retrofit.
  4. Eight categories — too many for a phone? They wrap to two rows on a narrow screen, which is fine, but the list is a guess. The free-text capture on Something else is the correction mechanism; the question is whether anyone will actually read those answers monthly. If not, the capture is theatre.
  5. Video attachments? A short clip is the single most useful bug attachment and the single most expensive one on a metered Somaliland connection. Proposal: allow it, cap at 10 MB, and warn before an upload over 5 MB.
  6. Does a lapsed store keep this? It must. Billing is a category, and a shop locked out for non-payment is the one most likely to need it — so Help & support has to survive the upgrade wall in (admin)/_layout.tsx the same way the billing screen deliberately does.
