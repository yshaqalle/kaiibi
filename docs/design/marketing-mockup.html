Marketing: the reach the promotions never had

Kaiibi already stores promotions and already applies them in the till — but nothing tells a customer they exist. This adds a fifth People tab that turns a promotion into a message, picks who gets it out of the segments Customers already computes, and walks the owner through sending it on WhatsApp one chat at a time. No Meta account, no approval, no per-message fee. Flip through the four views; the honest limits are at the foot.

The four decisions this mockup makes

Promotions move, they don't get rebuilt

The editor lives in Settings → Sales & promotions today, three taps from anywhere and nowhere near the customers it targets. Marketing becomes its home; Settings keeps a read-only summary that deep-links here. Same table, same promotions module gate, no migration.

"Reachable" is a first-class number, not a footnote

An audience of 96 with 12 unusable phone numbers is an audience of 84, and the KPI strip says so before a single message is written. whatsappLink() already decides this — it returns null for anything under 9 digits — so the count is the same rule the button uses, not a second guess at it.

Every number is something Kaiibi watched happen

WhatsApp tells a deep-linking app nothing — not sent, not delivered, not read, ever. So the campaign counts only two kinds of thing: a tap the owner made, and a sale rung up under a customer's name. "Marked sent" is the owner's own answer to a one-tap question on returning from each chat. "Bought within 7 days" comes from sales.customer_id and never touches WhatsApp at all. Nothing here is inferred from how long the app spent in the background.

Facebook, X and TikTok get a poster, not an integration

Posting on a shop's behalf needs an OAuth flow per network, a Meta app review, a paid X tier, and TikTok's approval-gated content API — weeks of work and per-shop onboarding, to save a tap. A rendered poster the owner saves and posts themselves lands the same content today. The integrations stay on the table; they are not the first thing built.

What each view is made of

Marketing tabFifth entry in PeopleTab, gated on the existing promotions module plus a customers read permission — an owner who has one but not the other should not see a half-working tab.
Stat stripThe one place Marketing glances: BentoGrid + four StatTile variant="bento". Everything below it is read down a column and stays out of the grid.
Campaign list + detailTwoPaneListDetail, the same component Customers and Team already use — list on the left at 34%, sheet over the list on a phone.
AudiencesegmentForCustomer() unchanged, plus a "no purchase in N days" filter derived from sales the customer detail pane already loads. No new segment concept, no schema field for one.
MessageStored per campaign with {name}, {shop}, {offer}, {ends} filled per recipient at open time. Somali and English drafts are two fields on the campaign, not two campaigns.
Send queueOne row per recipient with a state. Opening a chat calls the existing openWhatsApp() — no new native surface, and it already works on web, iOS and Android.
New tablesTwo: campaigns (shop, promotion, audience filter, message, status) and campaign_recipients (campaign, customer, state, timestamps). Both shop-scoped, both under the same RLS shape as customers.

Where every number comes from

The tempting metric on this screen is "opened", and it is unavailable. A wa.me link is a one-way door: no callback, no return value, no scheme that reports back. Anything Kaiibi displayed as a read receipt would be fabricated. So each recipient carries a state, and the state names its own source.

ReachablewhatsappLink() returns a URL. A pure function of the stored phone number — the same rule the button uses, so the count can never disagree with what the button does.
Chat openedKaiibi called openWhatsApp(). A record of our own tap. True, and deliberately weaker than "sent" — it is what a row falls back to when the owner ignores the question.
Marked sentThe owner answered "Yes, sent" when the app returned to the foreground. An AppState listener notices the return and asks; nothing is inferred from how long the app was away.
Not sent / SkippedThe owner said so, either on return or from the queue row. Both are correctable afterwards.
Bought within 7 daysA sale exists with that customer_id, dated after the recipient's sent timestamp. Entirely our own data — the strongest number on the screen, and the only one that measures the outcome rather than the errand.
Delivered · Read · FailedNot available, and not shown. These arrive only as Cloud API webhooks. When a shop connects one, three new states appear and "Marked sent" stops being asked for — the schema already has room, since state is a text column with a check.

One consequence worth naming: "Bought within 7 days" is a correlation. The customer may have been coming in anyway, and walk-ins get the same discount. The tile says so rather than letting the owner read it as proof the campaign worked.

The limits, stated plainly

  • It is one tap per person. Roughly five seconds each. Eighty-four customers is about seven minutes of tapping, and the pacing banner makes that expectation explicit rather than letting the owner discover it at recipient thirty.
  • Bulk sending from a personal number risks a WhatsApp block. Numbers that fan out to dozens of non-contacts in a burst get rate-limited or banned. The queue paces deliberately and suggests a break every twenty; it is a real constraint, not a UI flourish.
  • Delivery is unknowable on this path. Read receipts, replies, and failures never come back to Kaiibi. Only the WhatsApp Business Cloud API returns those, and it costs per conversation and needs template approval before a single message goes out.
  • The best number depends on the till. "Bought within 7 days" only counts customers the cashier attached to the sale. A shop that rings most baskets up anonymously will see a low figure that says more about checkout habits than about the campaign.

Everything above survives the upgrade: when a shop connects the Cloud API, the campaign, audience and message stay exactly as they are and only the sender changes — the queue becomes a progress bar instead of a to-do list.
