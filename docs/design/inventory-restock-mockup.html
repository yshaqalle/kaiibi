Restock, and one door called Stock

Inventory can create products, move them between stores, and correct a count one product at a time. What it cannot do is the most ordinary thing a shop does all week: take in a delivery of things it already sells. This is that job — by hand and by spreadsheet — plus the header change that stops the four stock jobs competing for the same button.

1 · Four jobs, three doors

Every change to a stock number a shop makes is one of four things. Three of them have a tool. The fourth — the one that happens every time a supplier's van pulls up — has nothing, so people improvise, and every improvisation is wrong in a way the app cannot see.

What the shop is doingToday's toolWhat actually happens
“I've started carrying something new”
40 lines of a new brand		 Import products Works. Creates the rows, stock lands at the primary store.
“A delivery came in”
60 units across 22 products I already sell		 Nothing Three bad ways out, all seen in the code comments already:
— open 22 products and retype 22 counts;
— re-import the catalogue → every row rejected as a duplicate, nothing imported;
— re-import under tweaked names → it works, and now the shop owns two of every product.
“Send some to the other branch” Move stock (hand + sheet) Works. One transaction, a stock_transfers record.
“The shelf says 8, the app says 11” Inline edit on the row Works — one product at a time. A stock-take of 300 lines is 300 taps.
The gap is not “Move can't upload products” — it's that receiving has no home at all

Move is correct to reject a product it doesn't recognise: moving stock that isn't there is not a thing you can do, and planStockMoves is right to say so. Teaching Move to create products would make the same mistake Import already made in reverse — stock-move-import.ts exists precisely because people were reaching for the nearest bulk tool and inflating their counts.

The answer isn't a fourth column on the Move sheet. It's a fourth door — and then all four behind one button, each one labelled with what it does to the number.

2 · Add, replace, relocate, create

This is the spine of the whole design. Four operations, four verbs, and each sheet has exactly one blank column named after its verb. If a shop can read the column header, it cannot pick the wrong tool.

Why Count is in scope even though it half-exists

Because it's the sibling that makes Restock legible. “Received” and “counted” are the two numbers a shop writes on paper, and if only one of them has a bulk tool, people will use it for both — typing the shelf total into Quantity received and doubling their stock. One shared frame, two column headers, and a running +6 / −3 in the footer that spells out which arithmetic is happening.

If you'd rather ship Restock alone first, Count stays greyed in the door sheet with “coming next” — the door still teaches the distinction even when one room is locked.

Stock opens the door sheet below — Restock, Count, Move, Import products. + Add product stays out on its own because it is the only one that makes a row rather than changing a number, and it's the single most-tapped control on the screen.
Why Import products sits inside Stock and not next to + Add product

Both readings are defensible — import does create rows. It goes inside because of who reaches for it and when: nobody opens Import having decided “I will create catalogue rows”. They open it having decided “I have a spreadsheet and I want these numbers in the app”, which is the exact sentence that leads to Restock, Count and Move too. Putting all four spreadsheet-shaped jobs behind one door is what lets the door disambiguate them. Filing Import elsewhere leaves the one tool that caused the double-counting sitting alone with no sibling to be distinguished from.

4 · The Stock sheet

One tap, four rows. The hint line under each is not decoration — it is the whole reason the sheet exists, and each one names the arithmetic. This screen is the fix for the double-counting problem, more than any validation rule downstream of it.

Each row leads to a modal with the same two tabs — By hand and By sheet — so the choice of which job and the choice of how many rows never get tangled. Today they are: Move has the two tabs, Import has only the sheet, and receiving has neither.

Single-store shops

Move hides itself, exactly as the header button does today (showLocationFilter). A one-store shop sees three rows, and Restock — the one it needs — is the first and default-highlighted.

Difference 1 — the results list is the whole catalogue, not just what's in stock here

Move can only offer products the source store holds (listProducts(shopId, fromId), and rightly). Restock is the opposite case: the product you're receiving is very often the one that hit zero, and a picker that hides zero-stock rows would hide exactly what the van just delivered. Rows show “Has 0 here” rather than disappearing.

Difference 2 — there is a cost column, and it is the point

Inventory already carries a tone="wrong" caveat for uncosted products, because they make stock at cost understate and gross profit overstate. A delivery is the one moment the true cost is on the desk, on the invoice, in the shop's hand. Asking for it anywhere else is asking someone to remember; asking here is asking someone to read. Every restock is a chance to retire that caveat one product at a time.

Typed, not stepped

The quantity is a text field, per the decision already made for Move: a stepper is “+1” forty times. Deliveries are larger than transfers, not smaller.

7 · Where the two halves meet

“Seamless” is not a feeling, it's a structural claim, and it has to be true in the code or it isn't true on screen. Both tabs build the same object, pass it through the same checks, and end at the same button:

By handSearch / scan a product, type a quantity
↘
PlannedReceipt[]store, product, qty, unit cost, note — pure, testable, writes nothing
↙
By sheetUpload, parse, match by SKU → barcode → name

The plan module mirrors stock-move-import.ts line for line — a pure planRestock() that turns a parsed sheet plus what the shop currently holds into receipts and rejections, and a caller that commits it. Every rule below is then testable without a database, which is how the Move importer got its rules right:

  • Product matched SKU → barcode → name, in that order, for the same reason: identifiers survive someone tidying a name in Excel, and a name is the only one of the three two products can share.
  • Store matched on code or name, trimmed and case-insensitive, so a store rename doesn't orphan a sheet downloaded last week.
  • Two rows receiving the same product into the same store → rejected, not summed. Almost always a copy-paste slip, and silently adding them receives twice what the shop read on screen.
  • Both columns blank → skipped. Only one filled → rejected, naming the empty one.
The one asymmetry: Restock has no ceiling

Move's hardest rule is “you cannot send more than the source holds”, checked against a running total so five rows each moving 8 out of a store holding 10 are all caught at once. Restock has no equivalent — a delivery can be any size, and there is nothing to run out of. That removes the trickiest part of the Move importer and is why this is a smaller build than it looks.

What replaces it is softer and belongs in the preview, not in rejections: a receipt of 10× or more what a store has ever held of that product gets a warning pill. It's the shape of a misplaced decimal or a case-vs-unit mix-up, and it is a warning rather than an error because sometimes the pallet really did arrive.

8 · When the row is the wrong job

Four doors means four chances to walk through the wrong one. Every rejection therefore names the right door, and the modal offers to hand over — the pattern already live between Import and Move (“Move stock instead”, useStagedSheet so iOS doesn't drop the second sheet).

Movea product that doesn’t existToday: “check the spelling, or fill in the SKU”. Add: “…or, if it’s new, Import products.”

This is the actual answer to “Move doesn't let people upload products”

Move stays exactly as strict as it is — it will never create a product, because a product that doesn't exist has no units to relocate. What changes is that the dead end becomes a doorway: the rejection stops being “no” and starts being “not here — there, and here's the button”.

9 · Unit cost — the decision I need from you

A product has one costCents (nullable — and isUncosted is careful that null ≠ zero, because a free sample really does cost nothing). A restock arrives at a price that may differ from the last one. Three options, and this changes what stock at cost and gross profit mean everywhere in Accounting:

A — Latest cost wins — my recommendation

A filled unit cost overwrites costCents; blank leaves it alone. The preview says so out loud: “2 costs updated — Torriden 4.50 → 4.80”, and it's a line the shop can see before committing.

Matches the single field that already exists, needs no migration, and is what a small shop means by “what it costs me”. It does mean the 11 units already on the shelf are retroactively valued at the new price — acceptable, and worth one tone="context" caveat on the Accounting side.

B — Weighted average

(11×4.50 + 6×4.80) / 17 = 4.61. Correct accounting, and the honest answer to “what did the stock I'm holding cost me?” Needs no new column either. But it produces a cost the shop never typed and cannot find on any invoice, which is a support conversation every time. Right for a shop with a bookkeeper; heavy for one without.

C — Cost is display-only

Show it in the preview, write nothing. Cheapest, and wrong: it wastes the one moment the true cost is in the room, and leaves the uncosted-products caveat un-retireable in bulk. Only worth it if you want Restock shipped this week and costing as a follow-up.

The related one: does a restock create an expense?

expense-reporting.ts already has an inventory_purchase category labelled “Inventory restock”, and it's excluded from profit — the Accounting tile literally reads “Stock & owner draws — excluded from profit”. So the concept exists and is waiting for this feature.

My recommendation is a single unticked checkbox at the foot of the modal — “Also log 158.40 as an inventory purchase” — visible only when every line has a cost, so the amount shown is the real amount and not a partial one. Unticked because a shop that enters supplier invoices in Accounting separately would otherwise double-count its spending, silently, forever. Opt-in is recoverable; opt-out is not.

Sheet-from-a-sheet is the one real iOS risk here

More → Stock → Restock is three stacked modals, and iOS silently drops the third. Whatever ships, the compact path must go through useStagedSheet — the same mechanism the Import→Move handover already uses — or the Restock button will simply do nothing on a phone and look like a dead control.

The cheaper alternative, if you'd rather not stage three deep: on compact, the Stock row replaces the contents of the More sheet in place rather than opening a new one. One sheet, two panes, a back chevron. Worth deciding before code.

11 · Scanning, on web only

Scanning was built into the Move sheet and reverted the next day (f31d9aa). The right way to read that revert is not “scanning in a sheet is impossible” — it is to read what it actually names, because the cause it names is one platform's window system:

From the revert message

“HardwareKeyboardModule.startCapture wraps currentActivity.window. A React Native Modal on Android is a Dialog with a window of its own, so keys typed while any sheet is up never reach that wrapper — it neither reports them to JS nor swallows them.”

Two separate failures, both downstream of that one fact: no scan registered (keys never arrived), and the trailing Enter closed the sheet and discarded the basket (with no capture to swallow it, Enter pressed whatever had focus).

Neither failure can occur in a browser, and not by luck — by construction. React Native Web renders Modal as an ordinary DOM node in the same document. There is no Dialog and no second window, so:

The Android failureWhy the browser cannot have it
Keys never reach the capture wrapper The web path listens on document (use-barcode-wedge.ts:169). A sheet is a node inside that document, so its keystrokes were always arriving.
The trailing Enter presses whatever has focus The listener is attached in the capture phase and calls preventDefault() + stopPropagation() on a terminator that completed a scan (:150-155) — it runs before React's synthetic handlers, so the Enter is swallowed before any control sees it.

The one real hazard, and the mechanism that already solves it

The global listener deliberately stands aside when focus is inside a field (:142), so the field can handle its own scan. Inside this sheet that matters more than usual: a barcode is all digits, and a scan fired while the cursor sits in a quantity box would set the quantity to 8809611860018 — a wrong number that looks like a typed one.

This app already has the answer, built for exactly this: stepFieldBurst() / fieldBurstScan() in barcode-wedge.ts tell a scan from typing inside a field, by the same speed discriminator (3–20ms per character from a scanner; 90–150ms from a fast typist). So:

Focus in SearchA detected burst resolves the code and adds the row. What Inventory's own search row already does.
Focus in Received / Unit costA detected burst is discarded from the field and routed to the same handler. The box keeps the number a person typed.
Focus nowhereThe document listener catches it, and swallows the Enter.
And this is the platform where it matters most

A delivery is checked in at a back-office desk with a USB scanner, not by holding a phone over 22 boxes. The web build is where that desk is. The mobile builds keep a plain search field and promise nothing they cannot do — which is the sentence the revert asked for.

Two things must stay true or one scan gets read twice

Inventory's own wedge must stand down while the sheet is up — it already does this for the Move sheet, and the Restock sheet has to join that same condition. And the camera Scan button renders on web only: BarcodeScannerModal works there (only torch and haptics are native-gated), and a modal over a modal is fine in a browser.
