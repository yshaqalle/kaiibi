What the tabs are doing differently

Four of the seven are ledgers — Transactions, Bills, Expenses, Payroll. Their job is scanning a long list and reconciling it against something else. Three are analytical — Overview, Cash & Budgets, Reports. Their job is glancing. Bento was designed for the second job.

Works: Overview, Cash & Budgets, Reports

These genuinely gain. Reports in particular stops being a 466-line vertical scroll and becomes something you can take in — P&L, labour and tax sit side by side instead of one under the other.

Neutral: Bills, Payroll

Short lists with a stat row above them. Bento neither helps nor hurts — they look tidier and read the same.

Fights back: Transactions, Expenses

Look at Transactions above. The table wants every pixel of width and a single uninterrupted column of dates and totals. In a bento cell it gets 26px of rounding and 18px of padding on each side, then still spans all 12 columns — so the grid buys nothing and costs horizontal room. On the phone frame the table scrolls sideways inside a card that is itself inside a scrolling page, which is two nested scrolls in the same direction.

That last one is the real cost of B, and it is not a styling problem — it is the card fighting the content. Your own code already makes this argument: StatementRow's comment says money that reconciles is "READ DOWN A COLUMN, not scanned as cards."

What I'd do instead: keep bento for the three analytical tabs, and give the four ledgers the same visual language — new palette, type scale, 26px outer card, restyled table headers and status chips — in a full-bleed table layout with no inner grid. Same design, two layouts, each matched to its job. That is option A from earlier, and seeing B is what makes the case for it.

If you still want B everywhere after looking, say so and I'll plan it — it is entirely buildable, and the cost is the horizontal room and the nested scroll above, not correctness.
