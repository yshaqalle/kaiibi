People, in the bento language

Dashboard and Accounting are converted; theme.ts names People as still-cream "until converted on purpose." This is that proposal — the bento palette, chrome, card shape and controls, with the list-and-detail layout kept. The grid appears only where People actually glances. Flip through the four tabs; my read is at the foot.

Where the grid stops, and why

The Accounting mockup found that bento serves glancing and fights scanning: a table in a bento cell spans all twelve columns anyway, loses 36px of padding for nothing, and on a phone puts a sideways scroll inside a vertical one. People is mostly scanning — two of its four tabs are a roster you read down.

So the grid is used in exactly two places here: the stat strip at the top of Customers and Team, and the tile row in a detail pane. Both are glancing. Everything else keeps the shape it already has and simply changes what it is made of.

Real gains: the detail panes

Today one 400-line card holds every section, separated by grey uppercase labels. Split into bento cards, purchase history and points history sit side by side on desktop instead of a metre apart, and the tiles stop being cream-bordered boxes floating on white.

Real gain: the stat strips are new

Customers and Team currently open with no summary at all — you land on a list and count. The four figures on each strip come from data both tabs already fetch (segmentCounts, getCustomersStatsBatch, onLeaveMemberIds), so this is layout, not new queries.

Neutral: Schedule and Me

A week grid and a self-service form. Both take the palette and the 26px card and look like they belong; neither is any easier to use than before. That is the correct outcome — consistency is the whole reason to convert them.

The cost, named: the phone sheet

Look at the phone frame. The detail arrives as a sheet over the list, so a long customer record scrolls inside the sheet while the list scrolls behind it. That is the app's existing behaviour, not something bento introduces — but bento's taller cards make each record longer, so it gets slightly worse. Schedule keeps one genuine sideways scroll at 380px, because a seven-day grid cannot be anything else.

What this needs from the shared components: StatTile, Badge and CategoryChip each need a bento variant — they currently hardcode the cream palette, which is why Dashboard and Accounting already show cream-bordered tiles on white bento cards. Fixing them for People fixes those two as a side effect.

The alternative, so it is a choice: push the full card grid onto the roster too — list and detail as sibling bento cells rather than panes. It reads fine at 1280px and collapses to the same stack below, but it costs the list its sticky, always-there column and gives the detail a lid. Say the word and I will mock that instead.
