Design review · Accounting

Apple blue, read against the app as built

The plan asks for three things: repoint every primary action to #0071e3, add an Accounting sub-tab with nine pages, and add a Reports sub-tab with twenty-three reports.

Both tabs shipped. Accounting has eleven views, not nine; Reports has a hub of eleven cards over seven built reports. So this mockup is not "here is the new thing" — it is the proposal drawn on top of what exists, so the two real questions are visible: does the blue land, and which of the twenty-three reports are actually missing.

2 · The blue, measured

The plan states #0071e3 on white is "~4.6:1 … and white on #0071e3 is ~4.6:1 as well". Those are the same check — contrast is symmetric, so one number was reported twice as if it were two passes. The real number is 4.70:1, and it only holds on white.

Where it fails: the grounds kaiibi cards actually sit on

The tinted pill is the one that matters most: it is the pattern the plan proposes for the most repeated control in the app, and 11.5px semibold blue on its own tint is 4.15:1. This is the exact failure theme.ts already documents for the muted ramp — a token solved against white, then shipped onto soft grey.

Where it collides: kaiibi already has this blue, and it means data

theme.ts states the rule this breaks in its own words, on chartAccent: "Deliberately not accent: that orange is reserved for actionable UI (buttons, selected states), so data ink and call-to-action never share a hue." At ΔE00 2.7 the proposed button fill and the line on the revenue chart are not two hues that are close. They are one hue.

This is a live surface, not a hypothetical: the Accounting Overview draws a payment-method mix in bentoSeries1–4 and a revenue trend in chartAccent, on the same screen as the buttons the plan wants blue.

The disabled state

The plan specifies background:#b6b6ba; color:#f0f0f0. That label is 1.77:1 on its own fill, and the fill is 2.02:1 on white. WCAG exempts disabled controls, so this is legal and still unreadable — a disabled Charge button whose amount cannot be read is worse than no amount. If a disabled variant is wanted, the label needs to stay legible.

Dashboard is where it actually breaks

Reports is mostly tables. Dashboard is almost entirely charts — and the plan explicitly restyles its Money in / Money out buttons. bentoSeries1 or chartAccent is read by the revenue legend, the cash legend, the sales-pace gauge, the top-mover sparkline, the open-hours area, the leaderboard avatars, the goal meter, the budget bar and the waterfall. If the blue ships, that screen has a button the same colour as nine of its own marks.

The adaptation — the proposal's proportions, kaiibi's tokens

The proposal's card is genuinely roomier than the shipped one, and that part is worth taking. The shipped card is tight: a 13.5px title over an 11.5px blurb at line-height 1.38, in 18px of padding. The proposal gives the same content a 36px icon tile, a 15px title, a 12.5px blurb at 1.5, and 20px of padding — and that is geometry and type scale, which carry no colour. It can be lifted wholesale.

What must not come with it is the proposal's grey ramp. Its footer meta #9a9a9e is 2.80:1 on white — it fails AA by a wide margin, and it is the colour on "86 accounts". Its blurb grey #6e6e73 passes at 5.07:1 but is still a step lighter than bentoMuted. Both have exact bento equivalents that are already solved: bentoMuted 6.50:1 and bentoMuted2 4.89:1.

7 · What I would do

  • Drop the blue. Not because black is better in the abstract — because this specific blue is chartAccent at ΔE00 2.7, and the app draws charts and buttons on the same screen. If a hue for actions is genuinely wanted, the constraint is that it must clear ΔE00 15 against #2a78d6, #2f6bff and #1b47b8 under deutan and protan — which rules out the whole blue axis and points at the orange accent the cream palette already owns.
  • If the blue is adopted anyway, the tint has to go. The solid fill is fine at 4.70:1. The tinted pill is 4.15:1 at 11.5px semibold, and it is the most repeated control in the hub. Keep bentoSoft for quiet actions and use blue only where it fills.
  • Take the proposal's card proportions. This is the part of it worth keeping, and it is separable from the colour: 20px padding, a 36px icon tile, a 15px title, a 12.5px blurb at line-height 1.5, a roomier pill — all rendered in bento tokens on the borderless 26px card. The shipped card is tight, and the plan is right that it should breathe. Its grey ramp does not come with it: #9a9a9e on the footer meta is 2.80:1. Section 4 has the property-by-property spec.
  • Drop sections 3 and 4 of the plan as written. Both tabs shipped, with more views than were drawn. What is worth keeping from them is the backend notes — but only the two that are still open: aging buckets, and a vendor roll-up.
  • Build the four that share two functions. Aging Receivables, Aging Payables, Customer Balance Summary, Vendor Balance Summary — one bucketing function parameterized by direction, one roll-up parameterized by counterparty. Four cards, two pieces of logic.
  • Ask about purchase orders separately. "Purchase Order Report" on a reports list is a product decision wearing a report's clothes.
  • Keep "Owed to you". The proposal has no equivalent, and dropping it to match would remove the only place a shopkeeper sees who owes them money.
