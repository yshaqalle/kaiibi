Storefront · kaiibi branding

Putting kaiibi on a page that belongs to the shop

There is no branding on the storefront today — the footer prints the shop's name, its place, a WhatsApp button and two terms, and stops. Adding kaiibi is straightforward, but only once you separate the two things people mean by "branding", because they want opposite placements and one of them can quietly cost the shop money.

1 · Two different asks wearing one word

Worth splitting before choosing placements, because the answer differs.

The rule that falls out of this: attribution is a permanent, quiet mark on the shop's page. Acquisition is a single, well-timed ask on the confirmation screen, once the customer's own job is done. Putting "Create your own shop" above the goods monetises the shop's traffic against the shop — and the merchant is the customer here.

Note what it is not. The button is outlined, not accent-filled — the accent belongs to the shop and to the act of buying, and an acquisition CTA wearing it would be the loudest thing on a screen whose job is to confirm an order. It sits below the order link, so the thing the customer came for stays first.

The order link is the highest-value branding surface on the whole product and the easiest to overlook: it is a URL the customer saves and reopens to check their order. Whatever lives on public-order-view.tsx gets seen repeatedly, by someone in a good mood, at a moment kaiibi visibly worked.

5 · The version the merchant actually wants

Branding is usually a tax on the merchant. It does not have to be — if it carries trust rather than just a name.

Hargeisa Men's Clothing Store
Main stree, Hargeisa · Collection only
Open now
kVerified shop on kaiibi

A stranger deciding whether to walk across Hargeisa to a shop they found on a link is making a trust judgement with very little to go on. "Verified shop on kaiibi" answers that — which means the merchant wants the badge, and kaiibi gets its name on the page as a side effect rather than as a levy.

It has to be true, though. A badge that every shop gets the moment it signs up is decoration, and customers learn to ignore it. It needs to mean something checkable — a confirmed phone number, a real address, a first completed order. Otherwise ship the plain "Powered by" and skip this.

  • Never the shop's accent. That colour means "press this to buy". Branding is not pressable in that sense.
  • Never kaiibi's own blue on the shop page. Save it for the directory and the marketing site, which are kaiibi's surfaces.
  • Use the white mark on ink, the black mark on light. Both assets already exist — kaiibi-mark-white.png and kaiibi-mark-black.png.
  • It is a link, so it needs a visible focus state — the footer is keyboard-reachable on web.

7 · Open questions

✓ Decided — branding is removed on Pro

Which settles the architecture: the mark must be one conditional in one place, and the footer is therefore the only sensible home. Three constraints came out of the code, and all three point the same way — the decision belongs on the server, not the client.

  • It cannot use the entitlements path. The storefront renders for a stranger with no session; my_shop_entitlements() is authed. The flag has to travel on get_public_storefront, the security definer RPC that already decides what an anonymous visitor may see — so it becomes one more column in that explicit column list.
  • Do not test planKey === 'pro'. Plans get retired and hopped to a successor (20260824000100_resolve_retired_plans.sql), and platform_settings.post_trial_plan_key can move. A hardcoded key breaks the first time Pro is renamed or superseded. Model it as a capability on the plan row, the way plans.modules already is — "an unknown entry is ignored by the reader, so a plan row can outlive a catalog change".
  • The default must be "show branding". entitlements.ts sets the rule: over- restricting costs a retry, over-permitting "is the shape a monetization bypass takes". Here the perk is hiding the mark, so absence must mean show. Boolean(row.hide_branding) turns a missing column into false — branding visible — which is the same shipped-ahead-of-the-database guard offersDelivery already uses.

One question this opens: a shop in grace is fully usable by design — payment is mobile money confirmed by hand — so branding should stay hidden through grace and return only on expired. That is another reason to compute it in the RPC rather than in the client, which has no business knowing about billing states at all.

Where does the link go?

A marketing page that explains kaiibi, or a sign-up? "Powered by kaiibi" clicked by a customer is curiosity; clicked by a shopkeeper browsing a rival's shop it is intent. The second is the valuable one, which argues for a page that sells rather than a form.

Does "kaiibi" need a fixed wordmark treatment?

Right now it would be set in the page's own font, which changes across the three themes. If the name is meant to be recognisable, it wants to be an image or a pinned face — otherwise it is just a word, styled differently on every shop.

What is "verified" if we ship the badge?

Needs a definition that a customer would agree is meaningful and that a shop can actually reach. Confirmed phone plus a real collection address is the cheapest honest bar.

8 · Suggested order

  1. Footer attribution — one row in shop-footer.tsx, zero added height, works on all six palettes and all three themes at once.
  2. Order status page — the same mark on public-order-view.tsx. Highest repeat exposure in the product, smallest change.
  3. Confirmation acquisition line — after deciding the link target and whether plans remove it.
  4. Directory branding — kaiibi's own page, so this can be as strong as the marketing site and needs no restraint.
  5. Verified badge — only once "verified" means something checkable.
