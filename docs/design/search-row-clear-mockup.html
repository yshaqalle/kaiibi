Inventory & POS · search row

A scanned barcode goes into the search box and cannot get out

On Inventory a scan deliberately fills the search box — finding the product is the whole job of a scan on that screen. But nothing empties it again. The cashier is left looking at a one-product list with 5901234123457 in the box and no visible way back to the full inventory.

The proposal, in one line

A × button inside the field, left of the scan button, present only while there is text. It clears the value and nothing else.

Today

Both modes of SearchRow, after scanning one product on Inventory. Neither offers a way out that does not involve deleting thirteen digits by hand.

the whole point
An empty list gives the reader nothing to tap. Before this change the only affordance on screen was the one that got them here.

What it does, and what it deliberately does not

QuestionAnswer
What does × clear? The search value, via the onChange('') the row already takes. Nothing else. The screen's own filters — category, stock filter, sort — are separate controls and keep their state.
Does it close the keypad? No. Clearing is usually the start of typing something else, and closing a dock the user is mid-way through using would cost more taps than it saves.
Does it clear the POS scan banner? No. scanFeedback and the + Add a product with barcode … prompt are their own state with their own lifetimes (the banner self-clears after 3.5s). One control, one job.
Why not auto-clear after a scan? Because on Inventory the filled box is the result — it is what narrows the list to the scanned product. Auto-clearing would undo the scan.
Accessibility accessibilityLabel="Clear search", accessibilityRole="button". The glyph is × (U+00D7), not a lowercase x, and it is never the only signal — the label carries it.

Alternative considered

× replaces the scan button while there is text. Tempting — no new padding, no crowding. Rejected: it removes the scan button exactly when the next scan is most likely, which is right after the last one. A cashier working through a delivery scans the next item without clearing the previous one first, and the whole row is designed around that not being interrupted.
