What is actually being proposed

New: a stat strip that replaces a sentence

Today the screen says "148 products · 12 need attention" as grey subtitle text under the title, and what the stock is worth is nowhere at all. Four tiles from data the screen already loads — the product list is in memory, so both value figures are sums, not queries.

New: cost and retail, as a pair

Stock at cost is what you paid for what's on the shelf. Stock at retail is what it would bring in at current prices. They only mean something together — one alone invites the reader to supply the other from imagination. The gap between them is the margin sitting in the stockroom.

The standard retail-accounting names, deliberately: "stock value" is ambiguous about which of the two it is, and that ambiguity is exactly what gets a shop's stock figure argued about.

New: the uncosted warning becomes a caveat — and now it bites

The Dashboard already carries a Caveat about products with no cost and deep-links here with ?filter=nocost, but Inventory itself says nothing. It matters more now: those 6 count as $0 in stock at cost, so a figure on this very screen is wrong until they're fixed. That is a textbook wrong caveat — a number with a cause the reader can remove.

Unchanged: the table

Seven columns, sortable headers, the stock stepper, the phone's tile fallback. It goes in one full-width card with reduced side padding and nothing else changes. Third screen running, same conclusion: a table in a bento cell spans all twelve columns anyway.

The one real problem: six header actions

Store dropdown, Export CSV, Export PDF, Move stock, Import, + Add product. On desktop it fits. On a phone that is two full rows of pills — the exact clutter the Schedule tab just had. The phone frame above shows the fix: + Add and More, with the rest behind a sheet, matching what Schedule now does.

Empty states are kept verbatim. This screen already writes the best empty copy in the app — it names which filter emptied the list, and a store that carries nothing gets told how to change that. The mockup's "No cost" state shows one. None of that wording changes; it just moves onto a card.

Why "No cost" lost its tile. The count already appears twice — in the caveat and on the filter chip. A third copy as a tile was the weakest of the three, and it was occupying the slot that stock at retail needed. Nothing is lost: the caveat states it in a sentence that says what it breaks, which the bare number never did.

What this needs that People didn't: nothing new in the UI. It reuses StatTile variant="bento", Caveat, CategoryChip variant="bento" and the existing pill buttons. The only new work is two sums over the product list — one at costCents, one at priceCents, both multiplied by stock on hand. Neither needs a query.

Pagination: there isn't any, anywhere. inventory.tsx maps every filtered product into a plain ScrollView — no pages, no FlatList, no virtualisation, so all 148 rows mount at once. The same is true of Accounting's Transactions list. It holds up at a few hundred rows and stops holding up somewhere after that, on the phones this app is actually used on.

This conversion does not change that either way, and it shouldn't — it is a performance decision, not a styling one. But it is worth deciding separately, and my recommendation is virtualise, don't paginate: a shopkeeper finds a product by searching or scanning, not by walking through pages, and the filter chips already do the narrowing that pages would. The catch is that a FlatList inside the screen's outer ScrollView nests two scrollers, so the strip and filters would have to become the list's header rather than siblings above it.

The open question for you: the stock stepper. It is the one control here that is genuinely interactive, and on a white card the current grey pill is quiet to the point of looking disabled. The mockup keeps it as-is so you can judge it — if it reads as too quiet, it is a one-line change to give it a border.
