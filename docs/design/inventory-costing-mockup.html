What stock costs, and in what order to fix it

Restock shipped with latest cost wins on a single shop‑wide field. Closed months are safe — that was solved in 2026 and still holds — but three things are wrong today, and one of them is wrong rather than merely approximate. This is what each fix looks like, and why the order matters more than the choice.

1 · What is already safe

Before proposing anything: a restock cannot change a closed month. Migration 20260804000000_sale_item_cost_snapshot.sql freezes each sold line's unit cost onto sale_items, and costOfGoodsSold() reads that frozen value, never the product. Its header states the problem it was written for:

“Without this, cost of goods sold has to read products.cost_cents live, which means raising a product's cost retroactively rewrites every past period's gross profit — a closed month's numbers change depending on when you look at them.”

It also refuses to backfill, and reports pre‑migration rows as uncosted rather than valuing them at zero or at today's cost. That instinct — a precise‑looking number that is simply wrong is worse than an admitted gap — is the one this whole document follows.

What the snapshot does not cover, and this is the part that was under‑stated when Restock was designed: the freeze protects history perfectly, but what it freezes is cost_cents at that instant — which is the replacement cost, not the cost of the unit that actually left the shelf. Restock at $4.80 while still holding units bought at $4.20, and the next sale of an old unit is permanently costed at $4.80. Correctly frozen, and wrong.

2 · The three that are wrong

One shop, one product, two branches. Jaalala 1 holds 10 bought at $4.50; Jaalala 2 holds 20 bought at $4.20 months ago. A delivery of 6 arrives at Jaalala 1 at $4.80.

1 — Stock at cost values everything at the newest price

Approximate, and it moves both ways: it overstates when prices rise and understates when they fall. Every shop holding mixed‑cost stock has some of this. Annoying; not a defect.

2 — A delivery at one branch re‑values another branch's stock

cost_cents lives on products. product_location_stock has no cost column at all. So the $12.00 error on the highlighted row was caused by a delivery Jaalala 2 never saw, and nothing on that store's screen says so. This is not imprecision — it is one branch's number changing because of an event somewhere else. This is the one to fix.

3 — Future sales freeze the replacement cost

The mixed‑cost problem, made permanent one sale at a time. Fixing 1 fixes this too: whatever cost_cents holds at sale time is what gets frozen, so making that number honest makes the snapshot honest.

Why context and not wrong

The house rule: wrong means the number is wrong until the reader fixes something, and must carry an action. context means the number is right and here is why it looks surprising. There is nothing a shop can do about this from Inventory — the basis is the app's choice, not their mistake — so a wrong with no fix would train people to ignore the whole family. The hint under the tile changes too: “at the latest price paid” rather than “what you paid for it”, so the caveat is a second telling, not the only one.

Rounding is not free and should be stated. $4.6125 stored as $4.61 loses 4½ cents across 16 units. Over many receipts the drift is one‑directional if it always truncates, so it rounds half‑up, and the receipt ledger stays exact — stock_receipt_items.unit_cost_cents is never rewritten, so the true history survives whatever the rolling figure does.

6 · Why this order, and not any other

Step 1 · hoursSay what it meansOne caveat, one hint reword. True under every later decision, and the feature is live now.
Step 2 · daysCost per storeMigration, one field through the scoped read, one lookup in complete_sale. Fixes the only actual error.
Step 3 · daysWeighted averageArithmetic inside receive_stock, plus the preview showing its working.
Doing 3 before 2 is worse than doing nothing

A weighted average across the shop would carefully blend Jaalala 1's $4.80 delivery with Jaalala 2's unrelated $4.20 purchase and produce $4.41 — a precise figure describing no store's stock. Today's blunt latest‑wins is at least obviously a single number; a considered average is more believable and no more true. That is exactly the failure the 2026 snapshot migration refused to commit when it declined to backfill.

Step 2 alone is a coherent stopping point

If Step 3 never happens, per‑store latest‑wins is still a defensible position: each store's stock is valued at the last price that store paid, no branch's number moves because of another's delivery, and the caveat from Step 1 says so accurately. Worth knowing, because it means Step 3 can wait for evidence that shops care rather than being carried by the same push.

7 · FIFO, and why not

Recorded here so it is decided once. The standard comparison has four rows; two of them land differently in this app.

RowThe general caseIn kaiibi
Physical match FIFO wins — old stock sells first True, and more so than usual. Products carry expiryDate and expiry tracking, so for skincare the shelf enforces it. But this is a claim about which units left, not about which number the owner needs — and those come apart.
Taxes Usually decides it Inert. taxRatePercent applies server‑side to each sale's post‑discount subtotal — a revenue tax. COGS never enters it, so the tax this app computes is byte‑identical either way.
Profit under inflation FIFO reports higher Argues against FIFO here. FIFO reports higher precisely by matching old cheap stock against today's prices — phantom profit. For a shop holding SLSH that is not a neutral preference: it says money was made that cannot be spent, exactly when currency movement is largest.
Cost to build FIFO is harder Lopsided. Average is arithmetic in receive_stock. FIFO needs sales to consume layers — changing complete_sale, which already carries tax, promotions, loyalty, credit and the stock decrement. The worst place in this codebase to be wrong.
Weighted average, and it is not close

The row that usually decides — tax — is inert here. The row that favours FIFO argues about physical flow rather than about the figure. The profit row actively favours average given the currency. And the build row is a rounding error against the risk of touching checkout.

The door is not shut: stock_receipt_items keeps every delivery's true unit cost, unrewritten, so the layer ledger FIFO would need already exists from today. If a shop ever needs true FIFO, the data will be there to build it from — it does not need deciding now, which is the whole reason that column was frozen rather than derived.
