Kaiibi · scanner till · fix pass

The keypad docks, and the notice leaves the caveat family

The branch review found two places where the build drifted from the approved design. The keypad — drawn as a bottom dock in the approved mockup — renders inline in the scroll flow, directly under the search row, so on a phone its lower rows can fall below the fold. And the till notice wears Caveat tone="context" with an action, which the Caveat contract explicitly forbids. This page shows both fixes.

1 · The keypad becomes a real dock

Not an overlay, and not a scroll-into-view patch: the keypad moves out of the scroll flow entirely and becomes a flex sibling below the ScrollView, inside the same SafeAreaView. The list shrinks to make room — exactly what the system keyboard does — so nothing is ever hidden behind the dock and no content inset maths is needed. This is the shape the approved mockup drew in its frame 3.

The dock pins to the bottom of the screen, full-bleed with its top border, above the home indicator. The ScrollView shrinks above it — the grid stays scrollable to its last row with the keypad open. Opening the keypad also scrolls the search row into view, so what you type is always visible while you type it.

Why a sibling, not an overlay or a Modal

An absolutely-positioned overlay hides the last rows of the grid and needs inset maths to undo it; a Modal would steal every tap, and the whole design promise is that the grid, the scanner and the keypad all work at once. A flex sibling gets the system-keyboard behaviour for free: list shrinks, everything stays reachable.

2 · On a wide till, the dock spans — the keys don't

POS on a tablet is the most likely scanner till. The dock's surface runs the full width under both panes, but the keys cap at a phone-ish width and centre — stretched to tablet width they become a piano, and the hand loses the row shape it just learned on the phone.

The cart stays visible and tappable with the keypad open — a cashier can scan the next item, tap a tile, or take payment without closing anything. The dock sits under both panes because it belongs to the screen, not to the search column.

3 · The notice stops borrowing the caveat

Caveat is the sentence that travels with a number, and its contract says a context caveat must never carry an action. The till notice is about a cable, not a figure, and its action is its whole point — so it gets its own small component, DeviceNotice, rather than a fourth tone that would dilute what the tones mean.

today — a context caveat wearing an action it must not have
ⓘ A keyboard or barcode scanner is connected to this device. If it’s a scanner, turn on scanning for this store to use it. Open scanning settings ✕
fixed — DeviceNotice: white card, soft glyph well, pill action
⌨ A keyboard or barcode scanner is connected to this device. If it’s a scanner, turn on scanning for this store to use it. Set up scanning for Hargeisa Main ✕
Same gates, same copy, different clothes
Confirmed keyboard + setting off + can act on settings + not dismissed — none of that changes. The hedged copy stays word for word: detection still cannot tell a scanner from a keyboard case, and the reader can see the cable.
The action now lands where the fix lives
Today the button opens the Locations panel and leaves you to find the right store. It now deep-links into the active store’s editor — /settings?nav=locations&location=<id> — with the scanning toggle on screen, and the label names the store so the reader knows where they’ll land.
Visually a cousin, not a sibling
No 4px tone rule and no tint — that pair is the caveat family’s uniform and it stays theirs. A plain white card with a soft glyph well reads as “the device is telling you something”, one step quieter than a data warning.
