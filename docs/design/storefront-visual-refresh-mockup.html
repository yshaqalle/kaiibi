Kaiibi · Storefront · 2026-08-30

The storefront’s visual pass, against the storefront we actually have

The brief that started this describes a single-brand page — juniper and parchment, Fraunces and IBM Plex Mono, five named designs, photo category tiles. Kaiibi’s storefront is not that shape: it is three layouts × six contrast-checked palettes, rendered in React Native, coloured per shop. This page translates the brief into that system, keeps every idea in it that survives the translation, and names the four that don’t.

The one thing that cannot be adopted as written: a fixed brand palette. The storefront’s colours belong to the shop — six palettes, each contrast-checked against WCAG in storefront-catalog.test.ts, with muted and danger derived rather than picked. Baking juniper/parchment into the page deletes that feature. Everything the brief wants from colour is reachable by deriving more tokens the same way, which is what this proposes.

2 · Colour — two more derived tokens, no new literals

The palette already derives what it can’t store. mutedInk blends ink toward ground by 0.34; dangerInk anchors on a conventional red, tints 12% toward the palette’s own ink, then walks it until it clears 4.5:1. Two colours never got that treatment.

The live defect — two colour collisions, both verifiable by grep. #1f7a4d and #8a5a05 are typed directly into product-tile.tsx:56 and theme-counter.tsx:132 — a fixed green and a fixed amber on all six palettes.

1 · Out-of-stock amber is Saffron’s accent. #8a5a05 is byte-for-byte storefront-catalog.ts:58’s accent for that palette. On a Saffron shop, “Out of stock — ask us” is the same colour as the Add button and the section rule. The codebase already knows this: storefront-catalog.test.ts:68 asserts danger !== '#8a5a05' precisely to avoid the clash — the guard was put on danger and never on the stock literal that caused it.

2 · In-stock green is the WhatsApp button. #1f7a4d is byte-for-byte WHATSAPP_BUTTON_GREEN (storefront-catalog.ts:139) — a colour the catalogue calls out as deliberately not themeable, an affordance rather than a brand colour. Today the words “In stock” wear the affordance colour on every palette, one line above an Ask button wearing the same green.

Proposed: stockOk and stockOut, derived per palette

Same formula as dangerInk: anchor, blend 0.12 toward the palette’s ink so the token is genuinely computed from that palette, then stepUntilContrast(…, ground, 4.5). The swatches below are computed live on this page by the real maths ported out of contrast.ts — they are not hand-picked.

Read the ratios, not the colours. Every cell prints its own contrast against that palette’s ground. Nothing lands below 5.9:1 — and the amber/accent collision on Saffron is gone, because the token is now a function of Saffron rather than a constant that happens to equal it.

What this does not fix, said plainly. A 0.12 blend barely moves a green that is already close to the palette’s ink, so stockOk lands within ~4% of WHATSAPP_BUTTON_GREEN on every palette — #1d6f47 against #1f7a4d. A stockOk !== WHATSAPP_BUTTON_GREEN test would pass and prove nothing, so it is deliberately not proposed. What separates the two is shape and weight, not hue: a light wash pill carrying dark green type, against a saturated green button carrying white text. Real hue separation would mean choosing a different anchor — a decision, not a derivation.
Open question, worth settling before build: does “In stock” need a colour at all? Most products, most of the time, are in stock — so a green pill on nearly every tile is decoration carrying no information. The alternative is to mark only the exception: out-of-stock gets the pill, in-stock gets plain muted text or nothing. That removes the WhatsApp-green resemblance by removing the green, and leaves colour on the one state a customer actually has to notice. Recommended — but flagged rather than decided, because it changes what a customer sees, which is a step past a visual pass.

3 · Type — three roles, one family

The brief’s real complaint is right: there is no role assignment today, just weight 800 at six different sizes. That is fixable without shipping a single font file.

Display
Xamdi Electronics
800 · −0.9 tracking · 19–32px

Shop wordmark, hero headline, category and section heads. Tight negative tracking is what makes a system face read as set rather than typed.

Body
Solar panels, inverters and batteries, with fitting included.
400/600 · 0 tracking · 12.5–14px

Product names, the about paragraph, checkout copy. The only role that ever wraps to a second line, so it is the only one with a line-height worth tuning.

Meta
11 products · In stock
800 · +0.09em · uppercase · 9.5–11px · tabular-nums

Prices, stock pills, category counts, eyebrows. fontVariant: ['tabular-nums'] is the part that matters — a column of prices that doesn’t align is the tell.

What a serif would cost, if it’s wanted later. Bundle the file into assets/fonts, add a useFonts gate in _layout.tsx, and accept that every hero and section head reflows on native at a different metric than the system face — which means re-verifying Window’s hero and Counter’s section rules on device, not just on web. Worth doing deliberately; not worth smuggling into a colour-and-spacing pass.

7 · Responsive

The brief proposes a 900px breakpoint and one source of truth. Kaiibi already has the second half of that and a different first half.

RuleWhere it lives
Grid columns gridColumnsForWidth() in theme-shared.tsx — 2 below 640, 3 below 1024, 4 above. Already one source of truth for Market and Window, already the key={numColumns} remount trick RN requires. The category band uses the same function rather than introducing a second breakpoint set.
Editor preview Already switches to a stacked single column below the wide breakpoint (wideColumn / previewColumn). No mobile-only stylesheet exists to collapse.
900px specifically Not adopted. Adding a fourth number to a set that already has 640 and 1024 would mean two answers to “how many columns at 900px”.

8 · What this needs that isn’t CSS

The category band is the only item here with a backend cost. Everything else is component-local.

ItemCost
Public category read A new function beside get_public_storefront_products returning name, image_url and a live product count, joining categories to products.category by name (they are not FK-linked — see 0004_categories_tags.sql). Must be anon-callable, and must respect the same “published only” gate the products function does. Add it to the narrowed anon surface in 20261009000100_narrow_the_anon_rpc_surface.sql deliberately, not by accident.
Preview parity The editor reads products admin-side via getStorefrontPreviewProducts precisely because the public RPC returns nothing while unpublished. Categories need the same admin-side twin or the preview shows an empty band on the one run the shop is looking at it.
Two derived tokens Pure addition to storefront-catalog.ts plus cases in storefront-catalog.test.ts asserting ≥4.5:1 on all six palettes, and one asserting stockOut ≠ accent — the Saffron collision, pinned so it cannot come back.
Counter’s flyer rule Untouched. Counter renders no flyers by design, pinned in storefront-flyer-placement.test.tsx. The category band follows the same logic — Counter already groups by category, so it gets no band either.

9 · Past the visual pass — what would make it feel built

Sections 2–7 fix how the storefront looks. These six fix how it feels, and three of them are worth more than everything above. Ordered by felt difference per line of code.

The subject, stated, because it should drive every call below. This page arrives as a forwarded WhatsApp link and opens in an in-app browser, on a phone, often on a slow connection. The customer usually already knows the shop — many have stood in it. They want three things: what is in, what it costs, and how to get it. They are not here to be told a brand story, and they will pay a person, not the page. That rules out most of what a generic e-commerce template would add, and it makes latency and touch response matter more than ornament.
GapWhy it is worth doing
1Nothing responds to touch Not one Pressable in the whole storefront has a pressed state — no style callback, no android_ripple, anywhere. Tap Add and the page does nothing until a number changes in the corner. This is the single loudest “cheap” tell on a phone, and it is one shared helper away.
2The shop’s own words are thrown away products.description is selected by the public RPC, mapped in storefront.ts:148, and rendered by no theme at all. Shopkeepers type it; no customer has ever seen it. Dead data, already paid for on every page load.
3First paint is a white screen and a spinner store/[slug].tsx:67-73 — a bare ActivityIndicator on #ffffff. That is the first second of every visit, over the slowest connection in the flow.
4No way to find anything Counter exists for “a long catalogue with no photos” — a 200-line pharmacy — and there is no search field on the public page at all. The one theme built for scale has the least navigation.
5Three themes, three unrelated scales Shop name is 19px in Market, 15px in Window, 18px in Counter; page padding 14 / 16 / 14. Nothing is wrong individually; together there is no system, which is why it reads unfinished.
6The empty state is a full stop “Nothing listed yet.” — on a page whose whole purpose is to start a conversation, next to a WhatsApp number it declines to offer.

Not a look change — a consistency change, and the reason the set reads unfinished today. Sizes below are the existing values reconciled to one ramp, not new inventions.

If only three of these ship, make it 1, 2 and 3. Touch feedback changes how every screen feels for about fifteen lines. The product sheet turns data the shop already typed into the reason a customer stays. And the loading state is the first second of every single visit — the one moment no amount of polish further down the page can reach.

10 · Acceptance

  • Stock reads off-palette nowhere. No #1f7a4d / #8a5a05 literal survives in product-tile.tsx or theme-counter.tsx; both come from paletteColors().
  • Every palette clears 4.5:1 on both new tokens, asserted in storefront-catalog.test.ts, and stockOut ≠ accent on Saffron.
  • Stock state survives greyscale. Pill shape and wording carry it; colour is the second signal, never the only one.
  • Window’s hero is legible over a dark photo — verified on device with a real upload, not in a jest snapshot.
  • Window’s hero without a photo looks intentional, which is the test every theme in this set had to pass.
  • The category band renders only at 2+ categories, and tapping one drives the existing filterByCategory state with CategoryFilterBar as the way out.
  • A shop with no category photos gets gradient tiles that read as designed, not as missing images.
  • Column counts still come from gridColumnsForWidth — band and grid agree at every width.
  • Picker rows are still derived from THEMES / PALETTES.
  • Editable/read-only unchanged: name, headline, about, hero photo, WhatsApp, layout and palette stay per-shop and editable; products stay read-only and backend-synced.
  • Verified on web, iOS and Android — this pass touches RN layout, and /testing-kaiibi exists for exactly this.
  • Not in scope: a display serif, a low-stock third state, a 900px breakpoint, renaming or adding a layout, any change to what a shop can edit.
