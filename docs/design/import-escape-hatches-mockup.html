Import's second door

Shipping Restock gave Import's rejection somewhere useful to point. But CsvImportModal's escape hatch is one slot, and pointing it at Restock silently took Move's away — so a multi‑store shop now gets Move named in prose and nothing to press. Two ways to give it back.

1 · Why one slot isn’t enough

The rejection fires on the same condition either way — you already carry this product. What it cannot know is which of two things the shop wants:

The shop is thinkingRight doorWhat the app can see
“40 more of these just arrived” Restock Exactly the same row. Same name, same SKU, same collision. The app has no signal that separates these two intentions — only the shop knows.
“I want these at the second branch” Move

So the app cannot pick. It has to ask — and only when there is something to ask about: a one‑store shop has no Move, and must keep seeing exactly one button.

3 · Which

A, and it isn’t close

The whole point of the Stock door is that a shop reads four hints and picks. Someone who has already reached Import and hit a rejection has narrowed it to two, and the app knows which two. Sending them back to a four‑row menu to re‑make a decision they have half‑made is the kind of politeness that reads as a runaround.

A also puts the choice where the shop meets the problem — on the rejection row itself, which is the placement csv-import-modal.tsx:36–39 already argues for in its own comment: “offered both up front and again on the rejection list, since that is where someone actually meets the problem.”

B’s real cost is the third sheet, not the extra tap

On a phone, Import is already reached through More → Stock → Import. Adding a door on top makes the Restock sheet the fourth. Every hop needs its own useStagedSheet, and this branch has already shipped one bug where the wrong staging flag would have left a dead button on a wide iPad. Four‑deep is a lot of rope.

Doing nothing is worse than it looks

A multi‑store shop reads “that’s a Move” and gets no button — so it closes Import, finds Stock, opens Move, and re‑does the work. That is the friction that sent people to Import in the first place. And moveFromImport stays wired but unreachable: a hook no test can reach, which drifts.

The one thing to get right in A

Two solid blue buttons stacked would read as a choice between equals. They are not: Restock is the common case — more of something arriving is ordinary, redistributing between branches is occasional, and a one‑store shop never sees the second at all. So Restock keeps the filled treatment and Move takes the quiet outline, exactly as drawn above.

Labels say the situation, not the tool — “More of something you already sell?” rather than “Restock instead”. Someone who picked the wrong door does not yet know the vocabulary; that is why they are here.

Scope: one type change in csv-import-modal.tsx, one render loop, one call site in inventory.tsx that already has both staged hooks (restockFromImport and the currently-inert moveFromImport) built and waiting. Roughly an hour, and it retires the inert hook rather than leaving it to rot.
