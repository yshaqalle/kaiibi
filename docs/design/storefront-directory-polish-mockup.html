A shop page worth forwarding, and a directory worth browsing

The storefront answers what is in today and nothing else. Everything a customer asks before they trust a stranger's link — who runs this, where is it, when is it open, do you deliver to me — is either crammed into one 40-word about line or missing outright. This puts the landing page's own section language on the shop page, splits the page into three tabs so depth costs no scroll, and gives /store the same vocabulary so the two read as one product.

01 · Direction

One language, two palettes

The landing page and the storefront are built from different systems and always will be — marketing-theme.ts sells, storefront-catalog.ts operates, and the storefront renders in one of six palettes for a stranger with no account. What travels between them is not colour. It is rhythm.

Borrowed from the landing pageWhat it becomes on a shop pageWhy it survives the palette swap
Tag pill + heading + lede (SectionHead) Every band on the shop page opens the same way — ABOUT / “Ten years on the same corner” It is a type ramp, not a colour. gray100/gray700 becomes soft/muted.
Generous vertical bands, alternating tone Product grid on soft, About on ground, FAQ back on soft The storefront already owns both tones. Today it just never alternates them.
Feature card — tinted icon tile, title, body (LandingFeatures) “Why shop here” — three claims the shop writes itself The icon tint is the only colour, and it derives from the palette's accent.
Accordion (LandingFaq) Shop FAQ, mostly generated from settings the shop has already filled in Pure structure. No brand colour anywhere in it.
Ink footer band (LandingFooter) Shop footer — name, contact, “Powered by Kaiibi” The storefront already has exactly one ink surface. This is its second, and it closes the page.
What is NOT borrowed: the landing page's bordered, shadowed white cards. The storefront is bento — borderless 26px cards floating on a page tone — and that is the app's system, shared with Dashboard and Accounting. Putting a 1px #E8E8EE border on a Palm-palette card would import a marketing token into a page that renders six ways. The cards stay bento; only the rhythm around them changes.

Where a line is allowed

Bento's rule is that separation is the page tone, and cards therefore carry no border. That rule is right about cards and wrong about everything else. On the ink palette ground is #ffffff and soft is #f4f4f5 — 3% apart. A card is a large filled shape and reads at 3%. A search field is a white bar on a near-white page, and at 3% it has no edge at all: it does not look like something you can type in, it looks like a gap. Across 1,280px, band boundaries have the same problem.

So this adds a seventh derived token — hairline, ink blended 0.88 toward ground, on exactly the pattern mutedInk and onDarkMutedInk already follow in storefront-catalog.ts. It is computed per palette, so it is never a hex literal at a call site and never a marketing token on a public page.

Gets a lineWhy
Search field, category chips, the sort control A thing you can act on must show where it begins and ends. This is the defect in the screenshot, and it is a usability bug rather than a style preference.
Between two bands that share a tone Padding alone reads as a gap. A rule makes the same gap read as a boundary.
Under a section head — “What's in today”, “8 shops in Hargeisa” It is the only thing standing between a filter bar and eight identical rectangles.
Inside a card, between rows of facts / hours / areas These were already ruled — in soft, which on a ground card is the same invisible 3%. Same line, now visible.
Gets no line, deliberatelyWhy
Any card — product tile, shop card, fact card, directory card Bento's actual rule, and the cards are large enough for the tone to carry it. Bordering them is what makes a bento page read as boxes ruled onto a background.
Between bands where the tone already changes A rule drawn where the background already changed is a second separator doing the first one's job.
The anchor card and the footer They are ink. Nothing needs help telling them from the page.

Tabs are real. The panels below are the same markup, called out one at a time.

Why tabs rather than one long page. The shop page's job on arrival is unchanged — whose shop, what is in it, what does it cost — and a customer who came from a forwarded WhatsApp link must not have to scroll past a founder's story to reach a price. Tabs put About and Visit one tap away and zero scroll away from the goods. They also mean the About tab can be genuinely generous, because nothing on it is in anyone's way.

A shop that has filled in nothing extra renders two tabs, not three, and a shop with neither renders none — the rail disappears and the page is exactly what ships today.

04 · Storefront

Visit & deliver, and the one thing it needs

Shown in the live frame above — switch to the third tab. It is the tab with the least new design and the most new value: opening hours already exist in 20260805000000_store_opening_hours.sql, per location, split shifts and all. Nothing reads them publicly.

“Open now” is the highest-value string on this whole page. It is the first thing a customer checks before walking anywhere, and today the answer is nowhere on the storefront, the directory, or the forwarded link. The data is sitting in the database. The work is a column on the public RPC and a “is it open” helper — src/lib/scheduling.ts already models split shifts, so the hard part is done.

Delivery areas deserve better than “From $1.00”

The CollectingCard reduces every priced area to the cheapest one, because on a phone card there is room for one line. On its own tab the full list fits, sorted by fee, and a customer can see whether their own neighbourhood is on it — which is the actual question. The cheapest-area line stays on the Shop tab; it is a summary now rather than the whole truth.

Two decisions worth arguing about.

1 — Open/closed on the card. It is the single most useful thing a directory card can carry and it needs the same hours work the Visit tab needs. Without hours the card falls back to the item count alone, which is what the earlier directory mockup shows. With them, the sort order becomes meaningful: open shops first.

2 — The featured card. A double-width ink card breaks a flat grid of eight identical rectangles and gives the page a centre of gravity, exactly as the anchor card does on a shop page. But somebody has to choose the shop, and “shop of the week” is an editorial job nobody owns yet. If that is unpalatable, it can be picked deterministically — most recently published, or most items listed — and the label changes to match.

What this costs

Ships with no new data at all

The tab rail, the band rhythm, the section heads, the ink footer, the generated FAQ, the stat strip minus “trading since”, the directory hero and its filters, the featured card picked deterministically, the skeleton and the empty state. This is a theme-shared.tsx and storefront-view.tsx change plus one new route — and it is most of what makes the page look considered.

Needs the public RPC widened

ThingWhere it already livesWork
Opening hours + “open now”store_opening_hours, modelled in scheduling.ts Add to get_public_storefront and the directory listing. Highest value on the list.
Full delivery-area list with feesAlready fetched by the routeRender it; no query change.
Category + item counts on a directory cardDerivableCount in the listing RPC.

Needs new columns or tables

ThingShapeCan the page live without it?
Long aboutWiden storefronts.about, or a second story column Yes — falls back to the line the anchor card already shows.
Gallery imagesstorefront_images(storefront_id, url, sort) Yes — story goes full width.
“Why shop here” highlightsstorefront_highlights(icon, title, body, sort), max 3 Yes — the band does not render.
Trading sinceOne smallint on storefronts Yes — the stat strip drops that cell.
Extra contacts (call, Instagram)Two nullable columns Yes — WhatsApp alone is what ships today.
Every new field is optional and every block degrades to nothing. That is the rule the storefront already follows — headline, about, heroImageUrl and the whole flyer band are all nullable and all render nothing rather than a placeholder. A shop that has filled in none of this gets today's page with better spacing and a footer. A shop that has filled it all in gets something worth forwarding.

Open questions

QuestionMy answer, if you want one
Do the tabs change the URL? Yes — /store/<slug>/about. A shop should be able to send someone straight to its story, and a tab with no address cannot be forwarded.
Do all three themes get tabs, including Counter? Yes. Tabs are chrome, not layout — Counter keeps its price list on the Shop tab and gains the same About and Visit.
Who writes the About copy? The shop, in the storefront editor, with the placeholder text doing real work: an empty field must read as “this is what a good one looks like”, not as a blank box.
Does the directory need search on day one? No. City + category filters cover eight shops. Search earns its place at fifty.
What happens to marketplace-coming-soon.tsx? It is what /store replaces.
