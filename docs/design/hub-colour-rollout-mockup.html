Design exploration · after the Accounting hubs

Where the hub colour goes next

The hub scheme is two separable ideas, not one: a band mark that says what kind of thing a card is, and one blue that says a thing is pressable. They generalise very differently. The blue raises a question across the whole app that has to be answered either way. The marks fit exactly one screen outside Accounting — and actively misfit three others.

What the survey found. Icon tiles exist in two files in the codebase — the two hubs. Every other screen is icon-light, so “colour the tiles like the hubs” has almost nowhere to land. What the other screens do carry is status, and status already has a solved palette. Badge and CategoryChip both already have bento variants, so that red flag in the skill is stale — but only three of the four screens on its “not yet converted” list are. Settings is still cream: zero bento tokens, all hex literals. That turns out to decide the whole plan below.
Status: 1, 3 and 4 are built and verified on branch accounting-hub-colour. 2 is blocked — see Settings. The screens pictured under “the fork” are the shipped state; the Settings frames are still a proposal.

Settings — the one screen the marks genuinely fit

Eighteen rows, five groups, an Ionicon already on every one of them, rendered flat grey. This is the shape the hub scheme was built for, and it is the only other place in the app that has it. It is also the one thing here I did not build.

Blocked, and worth being blocked. Settings has not been converted to bento — it reads #F3F4F6, #6B7280, #111111 throughout, with zero bento tokens. Dropping bentoBandAccount tiles into it is precisely the “half-applying tokens to an unconverted screen” the design system tells you not to do, and it is the same rule that kept the blue out of the modals. Settings needs converting first, which per the system's own process starts with its own mockup. The frames below stay a proposal until then.

Three of the five marks carry their hub meaning over unchanged

GroupMarkSame as the hubs?
SalesBlueYes — the shop's core trading work
CatalogTealYes — things the shop holds
BooksGraphiteYes — the formal record
BusinessAmberNo — here it means the shop's own setup, not “has a clock on it”
AccountSlate (new)No — the one group that is about the person, not the shop
The finding that matters. Two of five marks change meaning between screens. So this is not one global colour semantic — it is a per-screen category language drawing on one shared, CVD-validated hue pool. Pretending otherwise is how amber ends up meaning “overdue” on one tab and “shop setup” on the next with nothing to tell them apart. Written down, it is a rule somebody can follow; left implicit, it is a trap.

Slate is the only new hue. It was picked by test, not taste: purple and pink both collapse against the existing set for colour-blind viewers (worst-pair ΔE 10.7 and 13.7), crimson reads as an error state, and slate clears at ΔE 23.7 with a 6.18:1 white glyph.

Built, but not as drawn. The premise here was wrong: the wide table already printed “not an operating cost”. Three real gaps turned up underneath it, and those are what shipped instead of a stripe — no new colour was needed. (1) The phone card view omitted the qualifier entirely, so on a phone there was no way to tell which lines move net profit. (2) A ternary printed the provenance note or the qualifier, so every bill-generated restock — the commonest row in the list — lost it. (3) The tag was #B5793A, a cream literal reading 3.65:1 on white, under the 4.5 bar at 10px; bentoWarn fails too at 4.0, so it now takes the amber already solved dark enough to carry small text at 5.92:1. Provenance dropped to the label grey, because “from a bill” asks nothing of the reader.
