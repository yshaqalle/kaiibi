Scanner · Search · Touch — implementation reference

One till, three ways in

A shop runs on whatever devices it has. A couple of phones, a tablet on the counter, maybe one till with a barcode scanner wired to it. Scanning is the fast path on that one device — it must never become the price the other twenty pay, and it must never take typing away from the device that has it.

The rule this is built on

Search and touch always work, on every device, whatever else is attached. If a scanner happens to be there, it is added on top — never traded against.

What the device is asked, and what follows

One question, answered per device rather than per store: is a physical keyboard attached right now? A HID barcode scanner is a keyboard as far as the OS is concerned — which is also why iOS hides its on-screen keyboard on that device, and why the till that scans is the one till that cannot type without help from us.

Identical component, identical rules. POS is where the speed argument bites hardest — a cashier mid-sale can scan the next item without closing the keypad or losing what they have typed.

Keypad anatomy

Five rows. Digits sit on top rather than behind a mode switch, because barcodes get typed here as often as names. Short rows are centred so a key keeps the same width on every row and the hand can trust it.

Not included, deliberately: shift, symbols, emoji, autocorrect, the predictive bar, language switching. Product search is case-insensitive and matches name, SKU, brand, category, tag and barcode — every one of those would be weight with nothing on the other end. Keys measure ~34px on a 440pt phone, under the 44px touch target: acceptable for search, and it would not be for anything that moved money.
