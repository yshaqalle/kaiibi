Kaiibi · People · Customers & Team

Fitting a whole person on one screen

Both People tabs have the same problem: the detail pane needs far more height than it gets, so every selected person arrives pre-scrolled. The fix is one pair of moves applied twice — compress the chrome above the panes, and lay the detail out in two columns — so the answer fits without hiding anything behind a tap.

Two tabs, one disease

Measured off the stylesheet in real CSS pixels: at a 900px-tall window Customers gives its detail pane ~340px for ~830px of content. Team gives ~450px for ~700px — better than Customers, not worse as an earlier version of this page claimed, because Team has no segment chips and its search sits inside the list pane rather than in the chrome. Neither fits. The same two moves fix both, which is why they share a spec.

Customers — where the screen goes today

The page shell is flex: 1 and deliberately does not scroll (people.tsx:183) — the two panes scroll independently under fixed chrome. That is the right structure. The problem is how much the chrome takes before the panes get a say.

B or C: what the 86px actually costs

C wins 86px more than B by collapsing four per-figure hints into one. Those hints qualify the numbers — “in today: 3” means something different once you know it counts anyone who clocked in at any point rather than who is on the floor now. B keeps all four and still wins 65px, mostly by dropping the card’s own title, which is redundant beside labels that already read CUSTOMERS / VIPS / AT RISK. Compare frames 2 and 3 below.

Team — the same two moves

Team’s chrome is lighter than Customers’ — 336 CSS px against 446. It has no segment chips, and its search field sits inside the list pane (people.tsx:747) rather than in the fixed chrome, so it costs the detail pane nothing. That is not a virtue: it means the search scrolls away as you go down the roster, the opposite of Customers, where it stays put. The glance strip can also carry a Caveat (“Hours are hidden — you don’t have timesheet access”) which adds 62px when shown.

So Team’s detail pane gets ~450px for ~700px of content. Less starved than Customers, still short by a third.

What changes, concretely

Shared — both tabs, one implementation each:

  • Glance strip keeps its tiles, at reduced scale. Value 30px → 21px, tile padding 16 → 9, and the card’s own title drops — it is redundant beside labels that already read CUSTOMERS / VIPS / ON THE TEAM. 215px → 150px, and all four hints survive, which is the point: “in today: 3” without “clocked in at some point” is a floor count that isn’t one. Both tabs render the identical shape, so this is one shared component, not two.
  • The blurb hides once someone is selected. It orients a reader arriving at the screen; it has nothing to say to a reader looking at a person.
  • Detail identity collapses to one row. Name, status badge, the meta line, and the action buttons share a single line instead of three or four stacked bands.
  • The detail becomes two columns, falling back to one below the two-pane breakpoint, where the detail is already a full-height sheet and has the room.
  • Long lists get a bounded body. max-height with internal scroll rather than a “See all” modal — no click to reach a row, and no card that grows without limit for someone with 200 orders or 60 shifts.

Customers only:

  • Search and segment chips share a row. The field flexes, the chips sit beside it and keep their horizontal scroll for narrow windows.
  • Two columns: left is identity + tiles, Notes, Usually shops at; right is Purchase history and Points history, both bounded.

Team only:

  • Search moves out of the list pane into the fixed chrome, matching Customers. Today it scrolls away with the roster (people.tsx:747). The time-off requests panel stays in the list pane — it is a queue you work through, not a control.
  • Two columns: left is identity + tiles, Payroll, Access & permissions; right is Recent shifts, bounded. Permissions gains room to render as a proper grid instead of a squeezed span={6} half-cell, and the shift list stops being the thing you never see.
  • The timesheet-access caveat keeps its full height. It explains why two of the four figures are blank; shrinking an explanation to win pixels is how you end up with numbers nobody trusts.
Net, with option B

Customers: chrome 40% → 24% of the window, panes ~630 → ~845 units for a tallest detail column of ~680. Team: chrome 30–35% → 21–25%, panes ~470 → ~615 for a tallest column of ~470. Both fit, with headroom for a long name wrapping the identity row. Option C would add another 86 units of slack on top — slack that buys nothing once it already fits.

The open question

Whether two columns hold up at the narrow end of “wide”: at a 1024px window the detail pane is ~660px, so each column is ~320px and the stat tiles want to wrap 2×2. Below the two-pane breakpoint the fallback is one column, unchanged. Whatever the glance strip ends up as, it applies to both tabs — two People tabs with different strip treatments is worse than either choice on its own.

Not in this change

The Customers Notes field still only saves on blur, loses an in-progress edit when you switch customer, and renders for staff who cannot edit. Separate fix, tracked apart from this layout work. Nothing here touches the Schedule or Me tabs.
