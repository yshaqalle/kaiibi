Design mockup · Marketing → Poster

One promotion, four templates, three shapes

The offer already exists as data — a value, a scope, a window, a shop name and a branch address. That is a poster's entire content, so the shop should never open a design app to make one. Pick a template, and Kaiibi renders the same promotion as a square for a feed, a story for WhatsApp status, and an A4 sheet for the door. It is also the only thing in this whole feature that reaches Facebook and TikTok without an API.

Why this is cheap to build

The app already turns structured data into a rendered document: receipt.ts builds an HTML string and hands it to expo-print's printToFileAsync on native, or a hidden print iframe on web. A poster is the same pipeline with different HTML and a different page size. expo-print is already a dependency; so is expo-file-system for saving and expo-sharing for the share sheet.

PDF costs nothing new

A4 out of printToFileAsync, exactly as receipts do it. Print it for the door, or send it on WhatsApp — WhatsApp accepts a PDF as a document attachment, so a shop can push a flyer to a customer with no image tooling at all.

Images are a phone capability, and the docs are blunt about it

Checked against the SDK 57 pages rather than assumed. react-native-view-shot is Expo-supported and bundled in Expo Go — Android and iOS only, no web. And expo-print's printToFileAsync on web "opens the print dialog" instead of returning a file. So saving a poster is something the app does on a phone; the browser gets Print and no Save button, rather than a Save button that quietly does nothing.

Templates, not a design tool

Four layouts and a colour, and every one of them is legible at arm's length. A canvas the shop can drag things around on guarantees posters with clipped text and a logo on top of the price — and guarantees support requests about both.

The Kaiibi mark follows a rule that already exists

receipt_branding_removal already governs whether receipts carry "Powered by Kaiibi", and kaiibi-mark.ts already resolves it. The poster reads the same module rather than inventing a second answer to the same question — and a poster in a shop window is better distribution than a receipt in a bin.

Any colour they like, still readable

A shop should be able to put its own colour on its own poster, and the presets will not contain it. So the picker is open — a wheel and three sliders on the swatch row, plus a hex field for a shop that knows its brand. What is not open is the text colour.

The Market template makes the picked colour the entire ground. Left free, a bright brand yellow gets white type on it, on a sheet meant to be read from across a street. So Kaiibi computes the colour's relative luminance and chooses white or near-black ink to sit on it — about ten lines, no dependency, and it removes the only way this can go wrong. The same colour also has to work as accent text on the Bold template's dark ground, where a deep navy would vanish; it is stepped lighter or darker until it clears, exactly as theme.ts already documents doing for its own tokens.

One consequence: the colour belongs on shops, not on each poster. Shops store a logoUrl today and no colour at all, so this is a new nullable hex column — set once in Settings, defaulting every poster, and available later for the receipt accent and the sidebar. Per-poster colour would have an owner re-picking their own brand every time they run a sale.

The picker never blocks and never scolds. If a chosen colour had to be stepped for the accent role, the chip says so quietly; the poster is correct either way.

Every word on the poster, and where it comes from

The point of generating this from the promotion rather than from a form is that a poster can never contradict the till. If the offer says 20% and ends Saturday, so does the paper on the door.

The numberdiscount_value and discount_type — "20%" or "$2 off". Never typed.
What it applies toscope and scope_value — "Everything in store", "All shoes", "Anything by Somtel".
The datesstarts_at and ends_at. No window set, and the line simply does not print — better than a poster claiming "while stocks last" that the shop never agreed to.
Shop name and logoThe receipt branding already configured in Settings. Nothing new to upload.
Branch, address, hoursThe location record and store hours. Optional per poster — a shop with one branch usually wants it, a shop with six does not.
LanguageSomali, English, or both stacked. The headline is a fixed phrase per language, not a translation of free text, so it is always idiomatic.
Headline overrideThe one free-text field, and it is capped short. "Ciid wanaagsan" belongs on the Eid poster and cannot be derived from a discount row.

What this deliberately does not do

  • It does not post anywhere. "Share" hands the file to the phone's share sheet and the owner picks the app. Posting on their behalf means a Facebook Page connected to Kaiibi and Meta's app review — a later thing, and the poster is the reason it can wait.
  • It does not track. A poster on a door produces no clicks and no attribution. If a campaign needs measuring, that is the customer prompt at the till, not this.
  • It has no photo slot in the first cut. Product photography is the single fastest way to make a generated poster look bad, and the templates are built to work without one. It is an obvious second step once shops ask.
  • It is not a designer. Template, colour, language, and which optional lines show. That is the whole surface, on purpose.
