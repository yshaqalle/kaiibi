Accounting · Transactions · design proposal

An opened sale you can read at a glance

Today every section is the same grey capital label over a label on the far left and a value on the far right, often 1,900px apart. Nothing stands out, so the eye has to hunt. This version puts the four numbers people check first in tiles, keeps each label close to its number, and uses the blue pill buttons from The books.

Wide gapsOn a wide screen "Profit" and "$3.71" sit on opposite edges. Each card here is a third of the width, so they sit together.
Everything looks alikeItems, payment and profit all share one style. Here each is its own card, and the key figures get tiles.
A total that doesn't add up$12.00 + $0.29 tax ≠ $12.00. The tax was included in the price, and the summary must say so rather than add it.
Grey buttonsReceipt, Edit and Refund become light blue pills, and Delete a red one. That's the rule from The books: blue means press this.

Decisions for you

Actions at the top?

Recommended: yes. Today Receipt, Edit and Refund sit under a long profit section, so you scroll past everything to reach them. At the top they're the first thing beside the customer.

Refund: light or solid blue?

Recommended: light, like Receipt and Edit. It opens a sheet where the refund is confirmed, and that sheet's confirm button is the solid one. Delete is red so it never looks like the other three.

The profit bar

A thin green bar showing margin. It's small but helps compare sales as you open several. Easy to leave out.

Tax included vs added

Not stored on the sale. It's read from the numbers: subtotal + tax = total means added, subtotal = total means included. This also fixes today's summary, which shows $12.00 + $0.29 = $12.00.
