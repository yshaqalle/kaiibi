The hero is a control, and it had a bug

Three interactions, and the one thing the reference got wrong.

The reference hero carries three controls: a payment filter that cycles All methods → Cash only → Mobile money, a Money in / Money out segment, and the range pill. The takings figure eases between values over 650ms rather than snapping — kept here, and suppressed under prefers-reduced-motion.

The bug. In the reference, the payment filter changes the takings figure but renderPayments() ignores state.payFilter entirely, and the money in/out row below is never filtered either. Set it to Mobile money and you get takings of $0.00 sitting directly above Money out $1,000.00 — two numbers in one card describing different populations, with nothing saying so. That is how a dashboard loses trust.

The fix here. A filter scopes the whole card or it does not exist. Filtering now dims the non-matching rows in Payment methods, and the Money in figure narrows to the same methods. Money out cannot be filtered — kaiibi does not record which wallet an expense was paid from — so selecting it clears the filter and says why, rather than showing a filtered heading over an unfiltered number. Percentages stay measured against full takings, because a filter that changes the series count must not repaint the survivors.

Three decisions the reference got wrong, and what is here instead

A tooltip cannot be the only way to read a number. The reference puts four charts behind a hover, which is nothing at all on a phone — the device most of these shop owners use — and nothing to a keyboard. Every chart here prints the hovered figures into a readout line under it, which also carries a resting sentence when nothing is hovered. The tooltip is the fast path, never the only one.

The tooltip is bound per element, not by id. The reference's attachTooltip(hostId, …) takes a string and calls getElementById. Each card here is cloned into three frames, so an id-based hook wires up the first copy and silently leaves the other two dead. In React Native the same bug wears a different hat: a chart that reads a module-level singleton instead of its own props breaks the moment two of them are on screen.

Top movers deliberately ignores the range pill. A "biggest move" is a comparison between two recorded windows. Scaling both by a range multiplier leaves the percentage identical while turning the money figure beside it into fiction, so the card states its own scope — vs. week before — and does not scale. This is the same rule that hides the delta badges outside the recorded range, applied to a card rather than a badge.

One dark card became three

Revision 2 said the Dashboard has exactly one dark card. The reference has seven, and copying that would leave a screen with no ground. The line drawn here: dark is for the hero card and for full-width bands, nothing else. A band runs all twelve columns, so it reads as a rule between zones rather than a tile competing with the hero — the way a section break reads, not a headline. Best sellers and Open hours qualify; the three Top movers do not, and stayed white. That is a rule you can check, which is the only kind worth writing down.

The reference's purple gradient promo tile is gone. Purple is not in the palette, and a gradient advert sitting in a ledger reads as something to dismiss. Its three figures survive on an ordinary card.

The fan is a flourish, and it is allowed to be

Best sellers keeps the reference's fan of curves, where stroke width encodes share. Width is read poorly — but it is not asked to carry anything. Every figure sits in the rows beside it, sorted, labelled and to the cent. Delete the fan and the card still works; that is the test a decorative mark has to pass. It is dropped entirely on the phone, where a stacked fan is a smear.

What the reference HTML actually contributed

Taken

Zone labels. Six uppercase rules group thirteen cards into six questions. The biggest improvement here, and it costs nothing.

The dark takings hero. One card, and only one. It answers what the Dashboard never could: how much money actually came through the till. Takings is not revenue — the gap is sales tax that was never yours. Both numbers already exist, and dashboard.tsx:196 notes the payment mix costs "no extra round trip", so the hero is free.

Delta badges, on four KPIs rather than eight, and the interactions above.

The whole Overview row, at the reference's own proportions — takings 3, income/paid 3, margin gauge 2, the costed+goal stack 2, revenue spark 2. Every one is backed by a figure kaiibi already computes: total income and paid are revenue and operating expenses, the gauge is net profit over revenue, the dot grid is cogs.uncostedItemCount against products sold, the ring is GoalMeter, and the spark is the same daily series the trend chart draws.

Adapted

The KPI strip lost three tiles. Revenue, Expenses and Net margin are now stated at full size in the Overview row, so repeating them below would print the same three numbers twice on one screen. The strip keeps the six figures nothing else states — gross profit, net profit, orders, average sale, sales tax held, team on today — and fits one clean row of six. Restoring all eight is a one-line change if you disagree.

Charts keep height:auto instead of the reference's preserveAspectRatio="none" with a fixed height. Non-uniform scaling turns every circular marker into an ellipse and distorts stroke width — visible on the reference's own line charts.

Left behind

The mood tracker, "Wallet Verification", the Activity manager, and the concentric-bubble chart — none has a data source in kaiibi, and the bubbles encode magnitude as circle area, the least accurately read encoding, for a comparison the P&L already states to the cent.

Cash on hand, budget vs actual, recurring bills, sales tax and the expense donut stay in Accounting, as revision 1 decided. The reference's own data block proves the point: its cashAccounts, recurringBills and budgets are all empty arrays — cards drawn for a shape of business the app has never had data for.

The entity tab strip — Inventory / Customers / Team on one card — is navigation wearing a dashboard's clothes. kaiibi has those tabs already, with search, filters and editing. A three-row preview of each is a worse version of all three.

Range cycling on the customers card. Lifetime spend does not have a range, so its scope pill is deliberately inert and says All time. Two identical avatar strips measuring different windows is the single easiest way to mislead on this screen — the sister card beside it follows the range, and the difference has to be visible without reading the code.

Four tokens in theme.ts were failing — now fixed

These were live on Dashboard and Accounting; this mockup did not introduce them, it found them. All four are now applied in src/constants/theme.ts — they were the only four values changed, none of the hexes was duplicated anywhere else in src, and typecheck, lint and 721 tests pass unchanged.

Each was re-solved in OKLab against bentoSoft #f6f6f7, not white: the KPI tiles and the P&L total sit on the soft grey, so white is the flattering surface and the wrong one to test on. Hue and chroma held; only lightness moved, and the two muted steps stay 1.33:1 apart so the hierarchy between a label and an axis tick survives.

The green is the interesting one. #0f9d58 clears 3:1, so it is legal on the 19px bold net-profit total and illegal on the 15px bold row above it — the same token passing and failing in the same card.

Profit green and loss red still sit at ΔE 4.0 for deutan viewers — the classic red/green trap. Legal only because every mark carrying them also carries a sign or an arrow: each waterfall bar is labelled, each P&L figure shows its minus sign, each delta badge has a chevron. That labelling is load-bearing, not decoration.

Type is the system UI stack rather than a webfont, deliberately: React Native renders system faces on device. Every figure is set in tabular numerals so columns align — which is also why the eased takings figure does not jitter as it counts.

Implementation notes

Delta badges need data that does not exist yet. Every query in reload() takes the one { since, until } pair from dateRange; nothing fetches the window before it, and profitAndLoss is a pure function over a single period. Cheapest honest version: one extra getSalesAndRefundsInRange over the prior window plus listExpensesInRange for the net-profit badge — two round trips, wrapped in the existing attempt() helper so a failure degrades to no badge. Because that prior window only exists for the recorded range, the badges here disappear when you cycle to another range rather than quoting a comparison that was never fetched.

Range cycling is a demo convenience, not a proposal. The shipped screen gets its range from BentoControlBar, which refetches. Here the other ranges scale the recorded figures and say so in the chart footnote, exactly as the reference does — never let that estimate ship as though it were measured.

The hero needs no new colour token — its ground is bentoInk, and white on it reads 19.7:1. It does need a Card variant="bentoInk" so the treatment is declared once, and a rule that the Dashboard has exactly one.

DataTable needs a narrow column set, not just a scroll. Low stock exists to compare units left against the reorder level, and at minWidth={420} that second column sits off the right edge of a 380px screen. Horizontal scroll is the right fallback for a wide table, but not when the hidden column is the point. Both tables here fit 323px without scrolling.

StatTile, Badge and CategoryChip still hardcode the cream palette and each need a real bento variant — the standing red flag in the building-bento-screens skill, unchanged by this revision.

New in revision 3

The tooltip is a component, not a per-chart feature. One element, one attachTip(host, labels, series, readout), four charts. In React Native it is a Pressable overlay plus an absolutely positioned view, and the readout line is what it degrades to — so the readout is the primary implementation and the tooltip is the enhancement, not the other way round.

Open hours needs one query kaiibi does not run yet — sales bucketed by hour rather than by day. bucketDailyTotals is the template; the same pass over the same Sale[] keyed on getHours() costs no extra round trip, because the sales are already in memory for the P&L. The axis bounds come from OpeningHours, which means the chart stops where the shutter does instead of drawing twelve empty hours either side.

Closed days are a state, not a gap. A Sunday with no sales and a Sunday the shop was shut are the same zero and completely different facts. The strip disables closed days and says so on click, rather than plotting a flat line that reads as a catastrophic trading day.

Each band chart picks its own viewBox from its frame width. One aspect ratio cannot serve a 1244px band and a 344px phone: hold the ratio and the phone gets a 90px-tall chart with 4px axis labels. frameMode() here stands in for useWindowDimensions() on device — three sizes, three viewBoxes, uniform scaling in all of them.

The pale bar had to clear 3:1 too. The reference draws non-peak bars as rgba(47,107,255,.18) — 1.3:1 on white, a mark you can lose entirely on a sunlit phone. The light blue here is #5f86ff at 3.31:1, which is a different colour rather than a faded one.
