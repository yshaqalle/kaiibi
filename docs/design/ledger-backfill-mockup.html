A door for the ledger backfill

backfill_shop_ledger(uuid) exists, ties to the cent, is idempotent and takes a per‑shop advisory lock. Nothing calls it. Until something does, a shop's ledger holds only what has happened since this branch shipped, and years of real trading sit outside the books. This is the screen that lets someone press it — and, more importantly, the screen that says what pressing it will do before they press.

1 · The card, in place

The hub's Ledger & journals group holds four launcher cards today. This adds a fifth. Every card states its default period in the footer — “As of today”, “Manual entry”, “7 days” — and carries exactly one action. This one's period is live: it reads the number of unposted rows, the way Chart of Accounts already reads its account count.

The fifth card lands on the second row at four‑across and alone — which is exactly why BentoCell span={3} exists rather than a flex‑wrap row: a wrapping row gave the lone card flexGrow and stretched it across the whole band, which is how Audit Log came to render 1344px wide beside four 327px siblings.

Why the filled button, and why the plus.

The hub's two button styles carry a real distinction, pinned by a test: a card that writes to the ledger gets the solid button and a leading +; a card you only read gets the quiet one. Post History writes — potentially thousands of entries — so it is the second card in this group to be solid, beside General Journal Entry. Reading a report and writing to the books must not look like the same act.

Why the count is in the footer and not a badge.

Because it is the card's period, and every other card in the group states one. “3,973 unposted” answers the same question “7 days” does — what am I about to be shown. A badge would answer “is something wrong”, and nothing is: a shop that has never backfilled is in a normal state, not an alarming one. It is coloured with the warn ink rather than the muted grey only while non‑zero, which is the one honest use of emphasis here.

2 · Before anything happens

This is the state the screen is in when it opens, and the one that matters most. Before anyone presses anything it must say how many rows are unposted and of what kind. A single number — “3,973 entries” — is not enough: a shopkeeper who sees 3,973 has no way to tell a correct replay from a mapping that has silently swept up every payroll expense twice.

Eight rows, named in the shop's words.

The database calls them sale, refund, settlement, receipt, count, invoice_payment, payroll, expense. The screen calls them what the person pressing the button calls them. The mapping is one table in ledger-backfill.ts and it is the only place the two vocabularies meet.

A zero row still renders.

All eight kinds are listed even when one is zero, greyed rather than dropped. A list that hides its empty rows changes shape between shops and between runs, and a reader who saw seven rows last time cannot tell whether the eighth is zero or whether the door forgot to look.

The caveat is context, and carries no action.

Nothing here is wrong. The reader is being told why their reports are about to move, which is the definition of context. A wrong tone would demand a fix for a state that is not a defect — and the house rule is that a wrong with no action trains people to skip the whole family.

Nothing to post
The eight zeroes are the message, not the padding.

“Nothing to do” on its own is indistinguishable from “this screen failed to load”. Eight named rows each reading 0 is a positive statement: it looked in eight places and all eight are clear. That is worth eight lines.

A handful, rather than none.

A shop that runs this on Monday and comes back on Friday sees a small number — the week's trading is already posted, but a bill entered against a closed month, or a row written by an older client, can still land here. The screen makes no distinction between “years of history” and “three stragglers”: the copy is the same and so is the arithmetic, because both are the same act.

6 · Without ledger.close

backfill_shop_ledger gates on ledger.close, not ledger.post, and its own comment says why: “rewriting a shop's entire history is the heaviest thing anyone can do to these books”. The door must gate on the same permission the RPC does, or it offers a button that raises.

Posting past trading into the ledger needs permission to close an accounting period, which your role does not carry. Ask an owner.

The view is a URL parameter, so the screen is reachable by typing it and cannot rely on the hub having hidden the card. partial is the exact tone: the figure is incomplete through permissions, not through a defect, and there is nothing this reader can do.

The UI gate is not the gate.

Hiding the card stops an honest reader reaching a button that raises. It stops nothing else: backfill_shop_ledger is granted to authenticated and anyone can call it over PostgREST. The real gate is the has_shop_permission(p_shop_id, 'ledger.close') check at the top of the function, and the counts function carries the same check so the two cannot drift into disagreeing about who may look.

7 · Where the counts come from

The screen has to answer “how many, of what kind” before the RPC runs. The obvious way to get that is eight queries in TypeScript. It is also the wrong way, and the reason is specific: backfill_shop_ledger's idea of “unposted” is not journal_entry_id is null. It is that, and eight per‑kind money predicates that are anything but obvious.

KindUnposted means…The part TypeScript would get wrong
Salejournal_entry_id is null and it carries money“Carries money” is a six‑term disjunction over sale_items and sale_payments. A zero‑value sale — free samples on account — is deliberately never posted.
Settlementis_settlement is the filterA till payment's journal_entry_id stays null for ever: complete_sale folds it into the sale's own entry. Counting on the column alone over‑reports by every payment the shop has ever taken.
Refundreached through salesrefunds has no shop_id. Neither does invoice_payments.
Deliverycosted lines onlyAn uncosted line is excluded, not zeroed.
Stock countnet variance ≠ 0A count that found what it expected is not an accounting event.
Pay runstatus = 'posted'A draft has paid nobody, and an unposted run has had its pointer cleared on purpose.
Expensefour exclusionsPayroll‑derived, count‑derived, and the inventory_purchase half of a bill are all excluded — two of them stay unposted permanently by design.

Reimplementing that in the client gives a door and an RPC that disagree about the word the whole screen is built on. The number would be plausible and wrong — the failure this branch has spent nine tasks avoiding.

So: one view, and one function over it.

public.unposted_ledger_sources is a view yielding one row per source row the backfill would replay — (shop_id, source_kind, source_id, on_date) — carrying the eight predicates and nothing else. public.unposted_ledger_counts(p_shop_id) groups it, gates on ledger.close exactly as the RPC does, and is what the screen calls.

The client does no counting at all. It maps eight source_kind values to eight English labels, orders them, and sums.

And they are pinned to agree, in verify-backfill.sql.

Two checks, which together are tight in both directions. Before the replay: the view's row count equals what backfill_shop_ledger returns. After it: the view returns zero rows, for every kind. A view that over‑counts fails the first; one that under‑counts fails the first the other way; one that goes stale fails the second. The fixture's permanently‑unposted rows — the zero‑valued sale, the count‑derived expense — are what makes the pair sharp rather than a tautology, because a view that forgot their exclusions would over‑count by exactly those.

8 · Six decisions

A hub card, not a Settings item and not an automatic migration.

Automatic was considered and rejected: a backfill that runs itself on deploy gives a shop no moment at which to see 3,973 entries appear and no chance to read what they are. It also cannot be gated on a permission, because a migration has no user. The books belong to the shop and pressing the button is an accounting act, so it lives with the other accounting acts.

The confirmation is a state of the card, not a dialog.

Three reasons, in order of how much they cost: lib/confirm.ts raises a real window.confirm on web, which a Playwright check cannot dismiss; a sheet opened from within the Accounting tab is a stacked modal and iOS drops it; and a dialog can only fit one sentence, where the honest explanation needs three.

No per‑kind or per‑date selection.

“Post the sales but not the expenses” produces a ledger that ties to nothing, and “post from January” produces receivables with no origin. backfill_shop_ledger takes one argument for the same reason. A partial replay is not a smaller version of a correct one; it is a different and wrong thing.

No progress bar.

The RPC is one transaction and returns one integer — there is nothing to report progress against, and a bar that moves on a timer rather than on work is a lie. “Posting…” with the button disabled is the whole truth available.

The counts are read live, and again after the run.

Not cached and not derived from the number the RPC returned. The second read is what turns “it says it wrote 3,973” into “there are 0 left”, which is the claim a reader actually wants — and it is the same query the empty state uses, so there is one code path, not two.

What this still does not do.

It does not tell you what the entries will say — no preview of the accounts and amounts before writing. That would mean running the mapping twice, once to show and once to write, and two copies of the mapping is the exact failure mode Task 1 created the account‑map functions to prevent. The Journals list after the fact is the preview, and it is the real thing rather than a promise about it.

It also cannot be undone from here. Reversing 3,973 entries is a different feature with a different permission, and offering half of it would be worse than offering none.
