The current sale — one panel, or one sheet

Today the money is behind a door. A cashier taps Checkout, and a sheet slides up carrying the cashier chips, the customer, the points and the payment — four decisions that were invisible while the basket was being built. On a counter tablet this redesign brings all four into the sale panel itself. On a phone the sheet stays, because a handset cannot hold a basket and a payment at once — it is redrawn, not removed. Both then gain the four things a counter in Hargeisa keeps asking for: hold this one, they'll pay the rest tomorrow, they're here to settle up, and what is that in shillings.

Everything in the frames below is drawn in the tokens from src/constants/theme.ts — grey page, borderless 26px cards, cool-grey ink ramp — so this is the POS in the same language Dashboard and Accounting already speak. The frames are live and share one sale: add a product, take a payment in two parts, hold an order and resume it, attach the customer who owes money, or tap Checkout on the phone to open the sheet.

The counter — 1180px

Browse on the left, the whole sale on the right. Nothing about this sale is one tap further away than anything else about it.

The category row scrolls sideways here too. pos.tsx passes horizontal: true in both branches of categoryListProps — the wide layout only differs in its padding — so a shop with sixteen categories scrolls on a counter tablet just as it does on a phone. Drag it, and note that it stays where you left it when you add a product: a row that jumps back to All on every tap makes a cashier re-find their category all shift. A fade marks whichever end still has categories behind it, since the scrollbar is hidden; with four categories there is no fade, because there is nothing more to see.

The phone — 380px

Same state, cart above browse — which is already how pos.tsx stacks the two panes under TABLET_BREAKPOINT. The phone keeps the sheet. Discount and payment do not go inline here: there is no room to hold a basket and a payment on screen at once, and inlining them would put the money below however many items the customer brought. So the panel is the sale — items, total, Checkout · $84.74 — and everything about taking the money lives behind that button, in the modal it already uses. Tap it in the frame below, or read it standing open, beside today's, in the next section.

What changes is the sheet's design, not its existence: the same blocks in the same order as the tablet's panel, on grey rather than white. That is deliberate and not new — checkout-panel.tsx already notes that the sheet is the page and the blocks on it are the cards, because a white sheet flattens them into it. Header pinned, charge button pinned, the blocks scroll between them.

Three rules keep the small screen honest, and every one of them is about giving the space back to what is being sold:

  • The foot stays short. One Checkout button at full size, then Hold and who is serving share a single line under it — about 110px instead of 200. Every pixel it takes is a pixel of basket the cashier cannot see.
  • The product grid is two rows, then it scrolls. Measured off the first tile rather than guessed, which is exactly what pos.tsx does today with compactTileHeight * 2 + 8 — a phone that shows six products and no basket is a browsing app, not a till.
  • The categories are one row that scrolls sideways, never a wrapping block — and that is true at both sizes, not just here. See the note under the counter frame.

Paying off an older balance

Pay later only works if there is a way to pay. A balance is created at this counter, so it has to be clearable at this counter — not in Accounting, and not by a shop owner editing a figure later.

Attach a customer who owes something — Farah Hassan in the frames above, $34.74 unpaid since 12 Aug — and the debt appears under their name the moment they are attached, with the one control that clears it. Collect it adds the balance to what this counter is asking for, and the totals then name both debts rather than quietly summing them: This sale, Older balance, To collect. The payment side does not change at all — the same methods, the same amounts, the same split.

With an empty basket it still works. A customer walking in only to settle up is a real transaction: attach them, tap Collect it, take the money, and the button reads Take $34.74 off the balance. The receipt prints as a payment against the older sale and says what they owe afterwards.

Renamed: "Put it on account" is now "Pay later"

One name for one idea, and it is the customer's word rather than the bookkeeper's. It reads the same on the button (Save as unpaid → the sale is owed), on the receipt (BALANCE DUE) and on the customer row (Owes $34.74).

This is the piece that needs the server

A balance has to exist somewhere before it can be shown or settled: a sale status, an amount owed, the customer it belongs to, payments recorded against it afterwards, and a place in Accounting's receivables so a shop owner can see who owes what. Settling is a second write on top of that — a payment that belongs to an older sale rather than to this one. Everything else in this document is a screen change; this is a data change, and it should be planned on its own before either is built.

What is new, and what only moved

1 · A button that says what it will do

"Checkout" is a door, not a commitment. The button that takes the money — the panel's on a tablet, the sheet's on a phone — now reads Charge $84.74 · Cash, or Take $50.00 now · $34.74 owed, or Save as unpaid · $84.74 owed, or Take $34.74 off the balance. When it cannot fire it says which decision is missing — "Take a payment", "Attach a customer to carry the balance" — instead of sitting greyed out with no reason. The phone keeps a plain Checkout · $84.74 on the till itself, because at that point the decision it opens has not been made yet.

2 · Held orders

A customer goes back for one more thing and the queue behind them is four deep. Hold for later parks the whole sale — basket, customer, discounts, part-payment — behind the stack icon by the title, and Resume brings it back exactly as it was. The badge counts what is parked, so nothing can be silently forgotten at the end of a shift.

3 · Every payment carries its own amount

A split is not an even divide. Each method opens an amount — defaulted to what is still owed, which is the amount in nearly every sale — and what is entered is what is taken. Cash can be entered in USD or shillings, with the note buttons a cashier is actually handed ($5, $10, $20, $50), and anything over the balance shows as change due rather than being quietly swallowed. ZAAD and e-Dahab keep the optional name and number that go on the receipt. This is the model PaymentMethodPicker already implements; what changes is where it sits — inline on a tablet, first-class in the sheet on a phone — and that the running Still to pay figure now has a permanent place instead of appearing mid-flow.

4 · Part now, the rest later — said out loud

Take $50 of an $84.74 basket and $34.74 is unaccounted for. Neither surface leaves that implicit: Collect it now (take another payment) or Pay later (the customer owes it). Nothing is collected at all? That is the same choice with no payments against it, and the button reads Save as unpaid. Both require a customer — a balance with nobody's name against it is a loss, not a debt — and the amount owed rides the button, the totals and the receipt, in both currencies. The other end of it — how that customer pays the balance off — has its own section above.

5 · Clear all, still one tap and one question

Unchanged in spirit from today's clearSale: it sits by the item count, and it asks once before emptying a basket someone is standing at.

6 · Charging hands over the receipt — the one that already ships

The button completes the sale and the receipt comes up over the till, and the receipt is not part of this redesign: what the frames show is receipt-modal.tsx as it stands — the monospace paper, the dashed rules, the shop block, ITEM / QTY / PRICE / TOTAL, the QR and its short code, THANK YOU FOR YOUR PURCHASE!, and the three black actions (a fourth, Share, appears on native). It earns exactly one new line: a part-paid sale prints BALANCE DUE and whose it is, in the same idiom as everything above it. The till clears when the receipt is dismissed, not when the money is taken — on iOS the receipt cannot even be presented until the sheet before it has finished dismissing, which is why pos.tsx stages it on onDismiss today.

7 · Shillings under every figure

The price a customer hears is rarely the price on the tile. Every money figure carries a second line in the shop's local currency: line totals, the grand total, the balance due. The rate comes from the shop's own currency row (currencies.rateToUsd), which the shop already maintains for cash drawer counts — never a constant in the client.

8 · Discounts as chips

Both discounts — the line and the order — are now 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 and a custom field, gated on discounts.manual exactly as the editor is today. An offer that is already running still shows as its own green tag on the line, and cannot be tapped away by accident.

9 · The counter is a fixed frame

Nothing on this screen is allowed to grow the window. The receipt already works this way and keeps doing so: its card takes a concrete height, so the paper inside can be flex:1 and scroll while the header and the three actions never move — and the comment in receipt-modal.tsx explains why it is height and not maxHeight (against a content-sized parent the scroller resolves to zero and scrolls nothing).

The sale panel now takes the same shape. The title, the item count and Clear all are pinned at the top; the total, the charge button and Hold for later are pinned at the bottom; and everything that grows with the sale — the customer, the items, the discount, the payment and the itemised breakdown — scrolls between them. Only the two figures a cashier reads aloud hold still. A twenty-line basket changes what is in the scroller and nothing else: the total and the charge button stay exactly where the cashier's thumb left them. The product grid on the left scrolls in its own pane for the same reason, which is what pos.tsx already does with two independent ScrollViews on a wide screen.

The phone is deliberately different, and matches what ships today: the page itself scrolls, because the cart renders above the browse pane there, so the total is near the top already. The item list caps at about four lines and scrolls inside itself, so a long basket cannot push Checkout off the screen. The sheet then repeats the same shape one level down — pinned header, pinned charge button, blocks scrolling between them — which is why the payment can never end up below a basket on the small screen.

Kept from the source design

The inline panel (on a tablet), the customer row with its search-and-create dropdown, the held-order queue, pay later, and the speaking checkout button. All of it survives contact with kaiibi's data.

Not kept: the even split. The source divided the total across the selected methods and showed a share on each chip. Real counters take $50 in cash and the rest on ZAAD, so each payment carries the amount that was actually handed over — which is also what PaymentMethodPicker already does.

Changed: the line discount tag no longer cycles

In the source, tapping a line's discount tag steps 5 → 10 → 15 → 20 → off. At a counter that is a price change with no confirmation and no undo, one mis-tap wide — and the cashier who overshoots has to tap four more times to get back. Here the tag opens the same chip row the order discount uses.

Restored: what the source design dropped

The source panel has no tax, no loyalty points, no promotion tags, no register, no stock state and no cashier. Every one of those is live in pos.tsx today, and three of them change the total. They are all back in the frames above; the table below says where each one landed.
