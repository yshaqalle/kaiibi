Five across at 100%, not at 85%

Nothing about the band is wrong — it is 17 points too wide. A bento card may never be narrower than 240pt, and on a 1508pt window a sixth of the grid is a 197pt card, so the three small cells step up to a third and two of them wrap onto a second row. Zooming out to 85% buys the 43pt back, which is the whole reason it looks right there. The fix is to give the tiles their own floor: a ring, a dot field and a sparkline stay readable well below the width a card of headings and captions needs.

Why 240 is right for a card and wrong for a tile

240 is where a bento card stops holding a heading, a figure and a caption on one line each — earned on Accounting, where a 7/12 card beside a 5/12 one has real sentences in it. The three small Overview cells hold no sentences: a ring with a percentage inside it, three dots, and a 44pt sparkline with two words under it. They are tiles, and their floor is the widest thing drawn in them, not the narrowest line of prose.

The change

  • BentoCell takes an optional minCard, defaulting to the 240 every cell uses today. The rule itself does not change — only the number it is asked about.
  • The three span-2 Overview cells pass minCard={184}: the margin gauge, the costed-products / goal stack, and the revenue spark.
  • The margin ring becomes fluid — maxWidth: 150 with a square aspect ratio, the way the goal ring already is — so at the floor it shrinks instead of pressing on the card's own padding.

184 is not a device measurement either: it is the goal ring plus its caption, the widest of the three tiles at rest. It puts the step-up at a 1438pt window, so every laptop from a 1440 upward gets the band in one row and anything narrower degrades exactly as it does today.

What does not move

The two span-3 cards keep the 240 floor and their quarter of the grid. Accounting is untouched: no cell there passes minCard, so every fraction it resolves today it resolves after. Phones and portrait tablets are untouched — one column and thirds respectively, both already far below where this number applies.
