Phase 1 shipped: promotions know when they run, and every sale remembers which offer discounted it. That was the foundation and it bought no reach at all — nothing yet tells a customer an offer exists. The three phases below are the reach. Each is a separate branch with its own review and its own deploy, because a twenty-task branch gets one review over twenty tasks of drift.

Phases 2 and 3 do not touch each other, so they can run as two branches at once and merge separately — that is the only real compression available. Phase 4 genuinely waits: there is nothing to prompt a cashier about until campaigns exist to be redeemed.

The honest sizing

Calibrated against Phase 1, which was 5 tasks and produced 19 commits, about 20 review findings, and one long working session including reviews, fixes and verification on a real device.

PhaseTasksWhere the time actually goes
2 · Poster~6 Four templates across three shapes that must not clip at any value — "$1,250 off" and "5%" in the same layout. Plus the contrast library, and a web build that has to offer Print and not a Save button that silently does nothing.
3 · Campaigns~9 Two tables with their RLS, the audience filter, recipient top-up that stays idempotent, the four-step composer, the send queue with its return-from-WhatsApp question, and metrics that only ever count something the app watched happen.
4 · Till offers~5 Both sale procedures change signature this time, so it is another careful verbatim reproduction. Then the picker sheet, the customer prompt, and the product tile badge.

Roughly three to four times Phase 1's build. The variance is not the code — it is the findings. Phase 1's implementation went quickly; what took the time was a critical bug where new shops would have shipped cashiers unable to discount, a permission bypass through the sale editor, a forged-promotion hole, and a till that got stuck unrecoverably when an offer expired mid-sale. Phase 3 touches money and messaging both, so budget for the same.

The one input that changes the shape rather than the size

How many customers does a typical shop message in one campaign?
At tens, the one-tap WhatsApp queue above is the right product: free, no Meta account, no approval, ships in days. At hundreds, it is twenty-five minutes of tapping and a real risk of WhatsApp limiting a personal number — and Phase 3 stops being a nine-task app feature. It becomes a Meta Business account, a dedicated number, a server to send from, and Meta approving your message wording before a single one goes out. That is calendar time you cannot compress by working harder.

Absent an answer I will build for tens, with the sender kept swappable so the campaign, audience and message survive the upgrade untouched.

The detailed mockups behind each phase

  • Phase 2 — the poster: four templates, three output shapes, the colour picker and why the text colour is not the shop's to choose.
  • Phase 3 — the campaigns tab: the audience step, the message with placeholders, the send queue, and where every number on the screen comes from.
  • Phase 4 — offers at the till: the auto-applied line, the customer prompt, the picker, and what the finished sale remembers. Its first and last views are already shipped as part of Phase 1.
