Storefront · checkout bar & polish

The checkout bar is a phone control that never learned about desktop

It is the last thing a customer touches before committing money, and right now it is a 1,972-pixel-wide slab that says four words. Below: what is actually wrong with it (three of the four are verifiable in the source, not matters of taste), three directions for replacing it, and five other storefront improvements found while looking.

1 · What the bar does today

From theme-shared.tsx: absolutely positioned, left: 14, right: 14, bottom: 14, a 999-radius pill with one centred string.

It escapes the reading column

Every theme centres its scroller at SHOP_MAX_WIDTH (1080). CheckoutBar is rendered as a sibling of that scroller, inside the theme's full-width root — so left:14 / right:14 anchors to the window, not the column. On a 2,000px screen the goods sit in 1,080px and the button spans 1,972px. It is the one element on the page that is not in the column.

2 · Three directions

Rendered in Palm, the palette the shop in question actually runs.

Today — one zone, one claim

Checkout · $34.99

A · In the column — the one-line fix, same control

Move CheckoutBar inside the max-width wrapper, or give it the same maxWidth: SHOP_MAX_WIDTH, alignSelf: 'center'. Fixes the escape and nothing else. Worth doing on its own merits even if you take neither of the others.

B · The slip — evidence left, action right — recommended

The container becomes ground — a slip docked at the bottom — and the accent goes back to being a proportionate button rather than a 1,000px field of colour. That is the substantive change: on the current bar the accent is the background, so it stops reading as a thing you press. Here the thumbnails answer "what am I buying", the count answers "how much of it", and the fulfilment word ("collection") sets the expectation before the form does.

C · Docked on desktop — B on phone, a corner card on wide

$112.973 items
Checkout

On a wide screen a full-width bar is a phone idiom — the cart is a persistent side concern, not a banner. Docking it bottom-right frees the column entirely and removes the need for the clearance padding on desktop. The cost is a second layout to hold in your head, and it only pays off if desktop traffic is real.

Recommendation: A now, B next, C only if the numbers justify it. A is a one-line correctness fix with no design debate attached. B is where the actual improvement is, and it works identically on both widths, so it does not fork the layout. C is genuinely better on desktop and genuinely more to maintain — worth it only if desktop is more than a rounding error in this shop's traffic, which is a question about Hargeisa, not about CSS.

What B changes in code

  • CheckoutBar gains products (or just the first three cart lines' image URLs) alongside itemCount and subtotalCents.
  • The container fills with colors.ground and takes a shadow; the inner button keeps colors.accent with ground type — so the existing accent-carries-white gate is still the one that matters, unchanged.
  • Thumbnails fall back to colors.soft when a product has no photo — the same no-photo fallback the tiles already use, so a photoless shop degrades to a count and a total.
  • CHECKOUT_BAR_CLEARANCE should stop being a constant. Measure the bar with onLayout and feed the height into the scroller's padding, which removes the re-measure trap the comment warns about.
  • Apply the padding unconditionally so the first Add does not reflow the grid.

3 · Five other improvements

Found while reading the storefront for the bar. Ranked by what a customer would actually notice, and honest about which are defects versus judgement calls.

1 · Palm paints "Buy" and "Message us" the same green

Defect
Observed
Palm's accent #1F6B45 against the fixed WhatsApp button #1F7A4D — ΔE 7.51, half the codebase's own ΔE 15 floor.
Why
Two different promises in one signal, on the shop's two most important controls. Palm is the only palette with it; the rest sit 49–88 away.
Fix
One value. #14543A gives ΔE 19.07 and improves white-text contrast from 6.47:1 to 8.90:1.

2 · The shop name renders over a grey shape, and the tagline renders twice

Defect
Observed
In the live shop: a grey angular block behind "Clothing Store", and "You're Gonna Like the Way You Look, I Guarantee It" printed twice — bold over the shape, then again as plain text.
Why
This is the anchor card, the first thing a stranger reads. A duplicated line reads as broken software, which is expensive on a page whose whole job is looking trustworthy enough to walk to.
Fix
Not yet diagnosed — I have not traced it. Likely a headline/tagline field rendered by two components, plus a cover-photo scrim with no photo behind it.

3 · The cart is in two places that disagree

Polish
Observed
Visit panel: Cart · 1. Bottom bar: Checkout · $34.99. One shows the count, the other the money; neither shows both.
Why
Two controls for one object teaches the customer that neither is authoritative.
Fix
Falls out of direction B — once the bar shows count, total and thumbnails, the panel's Cart button is redundant and can go, or become a plain "Review cart" link.

4 · Nothing tells the customer the shop is closed until checkout

Worth checking
Observed
isOpenAt is called in exactly two places: visit-panel.tsx's hours card, and shop-directory-card.tsx, which prints Open now on the listing. So a customer sees the shop's open state in the directory, and loses it the moment they walk into the shop — the Shop tab, where the buying happens, never computes it.
Why
A collection-only shop that is closed cannot hand anything over. Someone ordering at 11pm should learn that beside the Checkout button, not on a tab they may never open.
Fix
Surface the same computed state in the slip — "collection · opens 8am" instead of "collection". Zero new data; isOpenAt already exists.

5 · The accent is spent on decoration as well as action

Polish
Observed
On the shop page the accent fills the checkout bar, the active category pill, the Add buttons, the Open now pill and the nine stock dots.
Why
The stock dots are a status readout, not something you press. When the same colour means "tap me" five times and "just so you know" once, the tap signal gets diluted — the same argument storefront-catalog.ts already makes for having no stockOk token at all.
Fix
Draw in-stock dots in ink rather than accent, matching the rule the catalogue applies to in-stock text. Out-of-stock keeps its stockOut ring, so the distinction survives.
Deliberately not on this list: the empty middle of the "In the shop" card. It looks like a rhythm problem and is not — stockFoot takes marginTop: 'auto' on purpose, with a comment explaining that on a wide layout the card is stretched to the anchor's height and the alternative is dead air at the bottom. I went looking for a bug there and found a decision.

4 · Suggested order

  1. Bar into the column (§2A) and Palm's accent (§3.1). Two one-line changes, both pure fixes, neither needs a design decision.
  2. Trace the duplicated tagline (§3.2). It is the only item here a customer would call broken, and it is on the first card they read.
  3. Build the slip (§2B), taking the clearance measurement and the unconditional padding with it. This is the real piece of work, and it absorbs §3.3 and §3.4 for free.
  4. Stock dots to ink (§3.5) — small, and best done while the accent's job is already under discussion.
  5. Desktop dock (§2C) only if desktop traffic warrants a second layout.

Nothing here touches the palette system, the six palettes, or the bento surfaces — the slip is built from ground, accent and soft, so it recolours six ways like everything else on the page.
