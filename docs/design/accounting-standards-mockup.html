Design mockup · Accounting

A real general ledger under kaiibi

Every item on the request list below the line — trial balance, balance sheet, cash flow, journals, fixed assets, audit log — is impossible to produce correctly from the tables kaiibi has today. They all need one thing that does not exist yet: a double-entry general ledger. This document proposes it, shows the screens it makes possible, and lists exactly what changes.

What's wrong today

Not bugs — structural gaps. Each one is a place the current model gives an answer an accountant would reject.

There is no ledger, so nothing has to balance

Money facts live in seven unrelated tables — sales, expenses, invoices, payroll_runs, cash_accounts, stock_receipts, refunds. Reports are assembled by summing them. Nothing forces the sums to agree, and nothing can detect it when they don't.

Cash is typed in by hand, not derived

cash_accounts.balance_cents is a manual snapshot — the migration says so outright. So the shop's cash position and the shop's transaction history are two independent claims that are never reconciled. A balance sheet built on a typed number is a guess with a total on it.

Three things in the expense enum are not operating expenses

inventory_purchase buys an asset. owner_draw reduces equity. NON_OPERATING_CATEGORIES in expense-reporting.ts reaches the right net profit by excluding both — the right answer arrived at the wrong way.

And now stock_loss, added by the Count door. It correctly reaches the P&L, but as an operating expense — below gross profit. Shrinkage is the cost of goods that left without selling, so gross margin should carry it. A chart of accounts fixes all three structurally: 1200, 3100 and 5100 respectively.

Posted records are silently editable

expenses and invoices both carry updated_at / updated_by and can be changed in place. Under any recognised standard, a posted entry is corrected by a reversing entry, never overwritten. Today there is no shop-level audit trail at all — platform_audit_log is operator-side only.

Accrual is half-applied

Credit sales do reach revenue (via sale_balances), and sales tax is correctly treated as owed rather than earned. But a credit bill only reaches the P&L through a sync_invoice_expense trigger, and accrued wages are computed on the fly in the report rather than posted. Two of the three cases work; none of them is enforced.

What's already right, and worth keeping

Cost of goods sold is captured per line at sale time (backfill_sale_item_costs) — that is the matching principle, already done. Sales tax is already a liability, not income. Refunds return goods rather than cash-by-default. Everything money-moving already goes through an RPC rather than a raw insert, which is exactly the seam a posting layer needs.

The principles, and how the design meets each

"Common accounting principles" is not one rule. These are the ones that actually bind a retail ledger.

PrincipleWhat it demandsHow this design satisfies it
Double entry Every transaction touches at least two accounts; debits equal credits. A journal_lines table with a deferred constraint that each entry sums to zero. It is impossible to write an unbalanced entry, including through a bug.
Accrual basis Revenue when earned, expense when incurred — not when cash moves. Credit sales debit Accounts Receivable at sale time. Credit bills credit Accounts Payable at issue date. Payment is a second, separate entry that only moves cash against the receivable/payable.
Matching Cost of a sale lands in the same period as the sale. Already true — sale_items.cost_cents is frozen at sale time. The sale's journal entry carries the COGS/Inventory pair in the same entry, not a separate month-end job.
Revenue recognition Money collected on someone else's behalf is not revenue. Sales tax credits 2100 Sales Tax Payable. Loyalty points earned credit 2300 Loyalty Liability and only become revenue (or a discount) when redeemed.
Historical cost Assets carried at what was paid, reduced systematically over their life. The Fixed Asset register holds acquisition cost and a depreciation method. Depreciation posts monthly to 6800 Depreciation against 1590 Accumulated Depreciation, a contra-asset — the cost figure is never edited down.
Consistency The same account means the same thing every period. One chart of accounts per shop, seeded from a fixed default. An account with postings can be renamed or archived but never re-typed or deleted.
Audit trail & immutability A posted entry is never altered; corrections are visible as corrections. Journals are append-only. "Void" writes a mirror-image reversing entry linked to the original. Every posting, reversal, period lock and chart change writes to accounting_audit_log with actor, timestamp and before/after.
Periodicity & closing Results are cut into periods, and a closed period stops moving. Months close automatically, 10 days after they end — long enough for late bills to arrive. kaiibi posts the four adjusting entries it can compute itself (depreciation, accrued wages, loyalty liability, the roll to 3900 Retained Earnings) and shows a checklist for the three only a human can answer. Posting into a closed period is refused at the database, not the UI.
Entity separation The owner's money is not the business's money. owner_draw stops being an expense category and becomes 3100 Owner's Draw, a contra-equity account. It leaves the P&L and appears on the balance sheet, which is where it belongs.
Prudence Don't carry an asset at more than it will realise. Inventory shrinkage and write-offs post to 5100 rather than quietly adjusting a quantity. Receivables aging (below) is the input a shop needs before writing a debt off.

On the scope of the claim. This design produces statements that are internally consistent, balanced and auditable — what an accountant means by "proper books". It is not a certification against a named framework. If the target is IFRS for SMEs or a specific tax authority's filing format, the statement layouts and a handful of disclosures would need pinning to that framework; the ledger underneath is the same either way.

A hub is a grid of launcher cards, not a second pill row. Each card states what period it defaults to — "7 days", "As of today", "This month", "Updated live" — because a balance sheet as of a date range is meaningless and a sales report as of one instant is useless. Tapping a card pushes a screen with a breadcrumb back to the hub, so the phone shows one thing at a time and the shell keeps owning the range and store filter exactly as it does now.

Why "Owed to you" disappears. It answered one question — who owes us — with a table that Aging Receivables answers better, and it sat in the pill row competing with seven other tabs. Nothing is lost; the receivables group in Reports is strictly more than the tab was.

FIFO — the decided approach

Stock is valued first-in-first-out. This is the largest and riskiest piece of the project, and the only one that touches the POS's hottest transaction.

Today kaiibi keeps one cost_cents per product, overwritten as stock arrives — a weighted average. FIFO instead remembers every delivery as its own layer, and a sale drains the oldest open layer first. The stock on the shelf is then valued at what was most recently paid for it, and COGS reflects what was actually paid for the units that left.

Two new tables

TableHoldsWritten by
inventory_cost_layersproduct, location, unit cost, quantity received, quantity remaining, source and its id, received_at — plus nullable expires_on and batch_number, which the accounting never readsreceive_stock, opening-balance migration, positive stock adjustments, customer returns
inventory_cost_consumptionwhich layers a given sale_item drew from, and how many units at what costcomplete_sale — and read by refund_sale_items to put units back at the price they left at
A stock-take is the fourth writer. save_stock_count sets stock where receive_stock adds, so a count down consumes layers oldest-first exactly as a sale does, and a count up creates a layer at the frozen unit_cost_cents. Where that cost is null — an uncosted product — the variance is recorded with no value, because null and zero are different answers.

Layers are per location, not per shop. kaiibi already tracks stock and reports profit per location, and a shop-wide layer pool would let a sale in Bakaara consume a delivery that physically sits in Hodan. The cost of that choice is that a stock transfer has to move layer quantity between locations carrying its cost — otherwise moving stock silently reprices it.

The four risks, named

Concurrency is the real danger

Two registers selling the same product at the same moment both reach for the oldest layer. Without row-level locking one of them over-consumes it; with careless locking they deadlock and the POS stops taking money.

Layers must be selected FOR UPDATE in a consistent order (received_at, then id) so two concurrent sales queue rather than deadlock. This needs a concurrency test that runs real parallel sales, not a unit test.

Selling stock that has no layer

kaiibi lets a sale go through when stock is zero or unknown. Under FIFO there is then no layer to consume and no cost to record. Silently using zero would overstate profit on exactly the sales nobody is watching.

The fix: consume what layers exist, then create a provisional layer at the product's last known cost for the shortfall, flagged. When stock next arrives, the provisional layer is trued up and the difference posts to 5100. The Inventory Valuation report shows how much of the value is provisional.

It slows the hottest path

A 20-line sale might touch 40+ layer rows, all inside the transaction that already writes the sale, its items, its payments and now its journal entry. Needs an index on (shop_id, product_id, location_id, received_at) where quantity_remaining > 0 and a measured before/after on a realistic basket.

Layers also fix a bug in a feature that already shipped

products.expiry_date has been there since 0001_init — one expiry per product, overwritten by the next delivery. Two batches of milk with different dates cannot both be represented, so the second silently replaces the first and the alert fires on the wrong day. That is the same defect products.cost_cents has, and a cost layer is a delivery.

So layers carry a nullable expires_on and batch_number. The accounting never reads them; getExpiringProducts() is left alone and keeps working. Consumption order stays FIFO, not FEFO — first-expiring-first would change COGS semantics, and the table supports it later by changing one ORDER BY.

FIFO starts at the switch date — history is not restated

Existing stock has no delivery history to draw layers from, so the migration writes one opening layer per product per location at today's cost_cents. Everything before that date keeps its weighted-average COGS.

This is correct and normal — a change in valuation method is disclosed, not backdated — but it has to be stated on the report rather than discovered. Hence "FIFO since 1 Sep 2026" on the Inventory Valuation screen.

Sequencing consequence. FIFO has to land before complete_sale starts posting COGS to the ledger. Do it the other way round and the ledger records weighted-average COGS for a few weeks and FIFO after, with a discontinuity nothing explains. That moves FIFO from the tail of the project into phase 2, and it is the reason phase 2 needs its own spec.

Permissions

Three new permissions, following the existing has_shop_permission pattern: ledger.view (see the books and statements), ledger.post (write manual journal entries and bills), and ledger.close (lock a period — owner by default). Staff who can already record a sale keep working exactly as before; the ledger posts underneath them and needs no new grant.

Phase 2 is the risky one and deserves saying so plainly. Adding a posting side to complete_sale means the POS's hottest path now writes more rows inside the same transaction, and the backfill has to reproduce years of history correctly enough that the first trial balance ties. It wants its own spec, its own tests, and a verification script that replays a shop's history and asserts the ledger agrees with the existing report totals to the cent.

Four things I'd push back on

Purchase Orders is a feature wearing a report's clothes

"Open and received purchase orders, by vendor" implies a whole procurement flow that doesn't exist: raising an order, approving it, sending it, receiving against it partially, closing it. That is weeks of work with its own screens, and none of it is accounting — a PO commits nothing to the ledger until goods arrive.

Putting it in this project means either shipping a stub that lists nothing, or letting procurement quietly become the critical path for the balance sheet. It should be its own spec, after this one.

Build the layers, ship both, default to weighted average

The cost-layer table is the expensive, risky part — and it produces both figures. Weighted average is just the layers averaged. So the method stops being an architecture commitment and becomes a per-shop setting, switchable later without a rewrite.

Default to weighted average: nobody's numbers move on migration day, it taxes less while costs rise, and it's the model a shop owner already holds. FIFO is one tap away for the shops that want the more current balance sheet. Design and risks here.

What doesn't change either way: the switch is never retroactive. It takes effect at a month boundary, history keeps the basis it was recorded under, and every statement says which basis produced it.

Keeping P&L and Income Statement as separate cards is fine — building them separately isn't

They're two names for one statement, but the descriptions distinguish them usefully: a summary for the owner, full account detail for the accountant. So both cards stay, and both run one query with a detail flag. Built as two reports they would eventually disagree, and the day they do, nobody will know which is right.

Manual journal entries are a foot-gun for a shop owner

A free-form debit/credit form is the one screen here that can put the books into a state nobody can explain later. The design keeps it — an accountant will need it — but gates it behind ledger.post, defaults that permission to the owner only, requires a description, and leads with the four common entries as templates so the blank form is the exception rather than the starting point.
