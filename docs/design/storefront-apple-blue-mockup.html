Storefront · matching Accounting

The Accounting tab is not blue — and the storefront already matches it

Rechecked against what is actually shipped in src/app/(admin)/(tabs)/accounting.tsx and theme.ts, not against the proposal mockup. Two things came back that change the brief: Accounting's action colour is near-black, not blue — and the storefront was already deliberately converted to Accounting's surface system, down to identical hex values and identical radii.

1 · What the Accounting tab actually is

Bento is a surface system, not a colour scheme: a grey page, borderless 26px white cards floating on it, one inverted anchor card, and a cool-grey ink ramp. Colour is rationed — and the blue in it is not a button.

There is no blue button in Accounting. Verified in the source: tab-pills.tsx paints the active pill bentoInk, range-menu.tsx paints Apply bentoInk, bento-band.tsx fills the anchor bentoInk. bentoSeries1 appears only in charts (bar-chart, waterfall-chart, payment-mix-chart, ranking-chart) and bentoAccentInk only as badge text (badge.tsx info). So "match Accounting's colour" means near-black actions with blue rationed to charts and one badge — the opposite of the all-blue-buttons proposal.

2 · The storefront was already converted

theme-shared.tsx says so in its own header comment — "The app's own system, recoloured… Bento is a SURFACE system rather than a colour scheme, which is exactly why it survives being recoloured six ways." The mapping is deliberate, and on the default ink palette the values come out identical.

The three "near" rows are near because they are derived rather than picked. Accounting hand-tunes each step in OKLab; the storefront computes muted and hairline from ink and ground by a fixed blend, because the same maths has to hold for six palettes at once. Chasing them to identical values would mean hardcoding ink's results and breaking the other five. These gaps are the system working, not drift.

So the honest answer to "make the storefront match Accounting" is: on the ink palette, it already does. Same page tone, same card fill, same radii, same borderless treatment, same inverted anchor, same near-black actions. What differs is only what should differ — the storefront can be recoloured five other ways, because it is the shop's page and not kaiibi's.

Palm is the only palette with this problem, and it is worth fixing on its own terms regardless of what happens with blue — a shop on Palm currently paints "Buy" and "Message us" in what a customer reads as one colour. The cheap fix is one value: stepping Palm's accent to #14543a takes it to ΔE 19.07 from the WhatsApp green — clear of the 15 floor — while improving its white-text contrast from 6.47:1 to 8.90:1, so it passes the accent gate more comfortably than it does today. Adopting blue would mask this, not solve it: the next shop that picks Palm brings it straight back.

5 · If you want Accounting's blue specifically

It cannot be a storefront accent, and the margin is almost comic. storefront-catalog.test.ts requires contrastRatio(accent, '#ffffff') >= 4.5 for every palette, because accent is a fill that carries white label text.

#2F6BFF misses by 0.0012. White on it is 4.4988:1 against a 4.5:1 floor. That is not bad luck — it is the token doing its job. bentoSeries1 was validated as a chart mark, where the bar is 3:1 against the ground and the number is written beside it in ink. It was never asked to carry white type, so it does not. bentoSeriesSoft fails harder for the same reason.
The one bento blue that ports is bentoAccentInk #1B47B8, at 7.95:1 — because it was already chosen as ink, a colour meant to be read. And it arrives with its own wash, #E6EDFF, so the storefront could take the whole info badge pair intact and use it exactly where Accounting does: a neutral emphasis chip beside a figure. That is a real, faithful port of Accounting's blue — and note it is a badge, not a button.

What stays out of it either way

  • WhatsApp green. A recognised affordance, not a brand colour — pinned by a test that no palette contains it.
  • Errors red, "Ask us" amber. stockOut is explicitly "never the accent"; there is a regression test named for the time saffron broke that.
  • Status needs a glyph. Accounting's rule — green/red is ΔE 4.0 for deutan viewers — applies to anything ported across.

6 · The call

Given the storefront already matches, "match Accounting" is really a choice about how far to go.

Recommended · smallest

A · Ship it — close the three near-misses only if they bother you

The surfaces, radii and actions already agree. Optionally align ink's three derived steps toward the bento ramp. Cost is near zero; risk is that changing a derivation constant moves all six palettes, so this is a "look at it, then decide" item, not an obvious win.

Faithful port

B · Take Accounting's badge blue

Add the #E6EDFF / #1B47B8 pair as a neutral emphasis chip on the shop page, matching badge.tsx's info. Keeps actions near-black, which is what Accounting does. This is the only port that is genuinely "the same blue".

A departure

C · Apple blue as a seventh palette

#0071E3 passes every gate and drops in as one row in COLORS. But be clear that it makes the storefront less like Accounting, not more — Accounting has no blue button to match.

My read: you asked to match Accounting, and the measurement says the match already exists. The thing that made it look mismatched is the proposal mockup, which paints every button blue — and that is not what the shipped Accounting tab does. If what you actually want is the app to go blue, that is a live and reasonable idea, but it is a change to Accounting first, and the storefront should follow it rather than lead it.
