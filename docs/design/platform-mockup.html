The operator console, in the bento language

Dashboard and Accounting are converted. The platform portal is a separate shell with its own sidebar, six tabs and about forty hardcoded hex values — it never got the pass at all. This is that proposal: the bento palette, card shape and control vocabulary applied to the console as it is laid out today. The grid appears only where an operator glances. Flip through the six tabs; the drawer and my read are below.

The shell: sidebar kept, surfaces swapped

Six destinations and a second identity — that is a console, not a tab bar.

The tempting move is to delete the sidebar and paste in accounting.tsx's shell wholesale, so both look identical. That would be the wrong read. Bento is a surface system; the skill's own line is that most screens take the surfaces and keep the layout they already have. Six destinations, three of which an operator ping-pongs between while working a single shop, is exactly what a persistent sidebar is for.

So the sidebar stays and changes materially: no borderRight, because bento has no borders. It simply sits on bentoPage grey, and the active item is the only white thing in it — a bentoSurface pill at radius 14. That inverts today's logic, where the active item is grey on white, which is why nothing in the current nav reads as selected from across a desk.

Below the breakpoint the sidebar becomes the horizontal pill row, which is already what accounting.tsx does and what the portal half-does today. The header row above the body is then literally the accounting recipe: eyebrow, 26px title, blurb, controls right. Every tab gets a real title instead of the current mix — some tabs render an h1, Overview renders a hero sentence, Shops renders nothing at all.

One relocation: WhoAmI stops floating over the top-right of the scroll area and moves into the sidebar, below the nav — pinned to the rail's foot in the real screen, where the sidebar is full height. Today it collides with whatever the tab puts in the header, and it vanishes entirely on a phone except for a "Sign out" link. In the rail it is where every console puts identity, it stops competing with the tab's own controls, and it frees the header's right edge for search.

Where the grid stops, and why

Glance gets the grid. Scan gets one full-width card.

Six tabs, and the split is clean — which is unusual and worth saying, because it means no tab needs a compromise layout.

The one place I would not use a DataTable: the phone rendering of Shops. Seven columns in a horizontal scroller means an operator swipes sideways to answer "when does this trial end", which is the question they opened the tab for. The current per-shop card is the right call and survives the conversion unchanged apart from its surfaces — see the phone frame. That is a deliberate departure from "DataTable already scrolls horizontally, don't fight it", and it is the only one.

Five statuses, two tokens

The gap this conversion actually exposes.

Bento ships exactly two status colours — bentoProfit and bentoLoss — and rules the four series colours out of status use entirely. The portal has five subscription statuses, and today paints them with five loose hexes (#1B4FA8, #1E7A3C, #9A6412, #999999, #B03535) that appear nowhere else in the codebase.

Three of the five map onto what exists. grace does not: it is the status that means paid but not yet recorded, still fully usable — neither profit nor loss, and rendering it grey would flatten the single most operationally interesting state on the screen. So this proposes one new token, bentoWarn at #b07206, which Caveat's wrong tone is already reaching for via theme.warning and a hardcoded #8A5A05. It earns its place twice over.

Every status carries its glyph, in every rendering, at both widths — the skill's rule about colour never being the only signal is not optional here, because two of these five are red and green.

The charts have to give up their palette

platform-charts.tsx ships its own seven colours.

CHART_COLORS is violet for signups, green for revenue, amber for Pro, slate for Free — a second, older palette that predates bento and collides with it in two places. Its green is a hair off bentoProfit, so a green bar and a green figure on the same screen disagree by an amount you can see but not name. And its comment ("green always means money") is exactly the semantic overload the skill bans: green is status, not series.

The mapping is mechanical and preserves the property the comment actually cared about — a series keeps its colour everywhere:

signups #7C5CFC bentoSeries1 New shops per week
revenue #1E9E5A bentoSeries2 Money collected per month. Deliberately no longer green — money is the subject of the whole screen, so colouring it "money" says nothing.

The 5px accent stripe on each plan card goes too. Bento cards do not have edge decorations, and a 5px bar is the sort of thing that reads as a border at a glance. The tier's colour survives as the dot beside its name and the colour of its headline shop-count — enough to match a slice in the donut, which is all it was for.

The shop drawer

The densest surface in the product, and the one that can do the most damage.

Radius 26 rather than 16, bentoSoft panels instead of hairline boxes, section labels in bentoMuted. Two things do not get flattened into the calm palette:

The reason field. It gates every button below it, so while it is empty it stays amber — not decoration, a precondition made visible. That is Caveat tone="wrong" logic applied to an input, and it is honest here because there genuinely is a fix and the fix is "type the reason".

The danger zone. Deleting a shop destroys its catalogue, every sale, its books and its payroll, with no undo anywhere. The bento treatment makes it a bentoLoss-tinted panel rather than a bordered box, but it keeps all three pieces of friction — collapsed by default, the counted list of what dies, and the retyped name. Nothing about the visual pass touches that.

What this needs before it can be built

Named honestly, because two of these are shared and will affect other screens.

1. A bentoWarn token

New. Consumed here by grace and the reason field, and immediately by Caveat's wrong tone, which currently hardcodes #FFFBEB and #8A5A05 beside a theme.warning that belongs to the cream palette.

2. StatTile needs its bento variant — still

The skill already flags this as live on Dashboard and Accounting: cream-bordered tiles on white bento cards. Overview alone puts eighteen tiles on screen, so the portal makes the existing defect three times more visible rather than introducing a new one. Worth fixing in the component, not per-screen.

3. A status pill component

Five statuses rendered in four places (wide table, phone card, drawer header, attention rows). Today that is a STATUS_COLOR record and a STATUS_DOT record applied by hand at each site. It should be one <SubscriptionStatus /> so the glyph can never be dropped by accident.

4. Everything else already exists

BentoGrid, BentoCell, BentoCard, DataTable, NameCell, ValueCell, Caveat, TabPills. No new layout primitive. BentoControlBar is the one shared control the portal does not want — there is no date range or store filter here, and the search box is the header's right-hand slot instead.

My read

Real gain: Plans becomes comparable

Three tiers side by side at span={4}, each showing what it includes and what it withholds, is a different tool from three stacked rows. This is the tab where the grid is not cosmetic.

Real gain: forty hexes become tokens

The portal is the last screen carrying its own private palette — five status colours, seven chart colours, and roughly thirty greys. After this, changing the ink ramp changes the portal too, which is not true today.

Real gain: the active nav item is finally legible

Grey-on-white becomes white-on-grey. Small change, and the first thing an operator will notice.

Neutral: Audit and Operators

They are already lists of rows with hairlines. They will look calmer and read the same. Convert them for consistency, not for gain.

The cost, named: Overview gets taller

26px cards with 18px padding and a 12px gutter spend more vertical space than today's 12px panels. On the desktop frame the Overview grows by roughly a screen-and-a-half of scroll. The pairing at span={6} claws most of it back, but not all — and if that is unacceptable, the honest lever is dropping the "Movement" strip into the same card as "Money", not shrinking the radius.

If this reads right, the build order that keeps each step shippable is: shell and nav → Shops (proves the table + status pill) → Plans (proves the grid) → Overview (the largest, and the one that wants StatTile fixed first) → Requests, Audit, Operators → drawer. Then drop Platform from the still-cream list in the theme.ts comment.
