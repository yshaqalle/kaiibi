Design · what is still open

The decisions still open

Five things are waiting on a call rather than on work. Each one below is a question, the options drawn, and what I would pick. Nothing here has been built.

Figures are illustrative — the test shop has almost no receivables, so the tables use plausible numbers to show shape rather than data.

Decision 1

Is “Aging Receivables” a new screen, or a view of a tab you already have?

This is the biggest one, because it decides whether the next block of work is four screens or two. Owed to you already answers most of what an aging report is for.

It already has who, how much, since when, and how long — and it already goes amber past thirty days. So an aging report is not new information. What it adds is one thing: totals per bucket, which is the number you take to a conversation about cash, and which no screen computes today.

Option A

A separate Aging Receivables report

A card on the Reports hub, its own screen, buckets down the page. It is what the original plan listed, and it matches the other reports.

The cost: a second screen listing the same customers as Owed to you, with the same amounts, sorted differently. Two places to look for one question, and the day they disagree, both stop being believed.

Option B · recommended

A bucket strip on the tab that already exists

Put the four totals above the table it already has, and let each one filter the rows beneath it. The report becomes a lens on the tab rather than a copy of it.

The Reports hub still gets its card — it opens Owed to you with a bucket selected, exactly as the three statement cards already open the ledger's screens on the Accounting tab. That pattern is shipped and understood.

Decision 3

Should cards in a row be the same height?

A shipped defect with two honest answers. Both hub files carry a comment claiming the cards line up via rowAlign="stretch" + fill; on web they do not, and have not for as long as the hubs have existed.

7 days

Every account's debit and credit balance, side by side.

As of today

As it ships — 193 / 174 / 212 / 193px, ragged along the bottom.

Option A · recommended

Make the claim true — equalise the heights

A row of cards is a set of peers, and a ragged bottom edge reads as one card being unfinished. It is also what the code already says it does, so the comment stops lying.

The risk is real and is why this is a decision: BentoGrid is rendered by Dashboard as well, and the file's own comments record a previous attempt at percentage heights that made every card a viewport tall on iPhone. It needs its own verified change on both platforms, not a one-line edit.

Option B

Delete the claim instead

Drop rowAlign="stretch" and fill, correct both comments, and accept ragged bottoms. Cheap, honest, and no risk to Dashboard.

Defensible — the cards are still readable — but it settles for the thing that looks like a bug.

Decision 4

Does a balance sheet as a CSV mean anything?

The eight ledger screens export nothing. Five are tables and are easy. The three statements are not tables, and that is the question.

ScreenShapeCSVPDF
Trial Balance, Journals, Chart of Accounts, Audit Log, Fixed AssetsA DataTableYes — add text to the columns, same as the seven reportsYes
Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash FlowStatementRow lists — indented sections, subtotals, a total that must reconcileNoYes

A statement is a document, not a dataset. Its meaning is in the hierarchy — which lines roll into which subtotal — and a flat CSV either loses that or fakes it with an indentation column nothing can compute on. An accountant asked for "the balance sheet" wants the balance sheet, and that is the PDF.

So: CSV and PDF for the five tables, PDF only for the three statements. This is the one place in the app where a screen would deliberately offer one format and not the other, so the button row simply shows one button rather than explaining itself.

Decision 5

Should the header-actions contract be able to loop at all?

Not a visual decision, but it is the one that already cost a bug report, so it belongs on the list. useHeaderActions publishes a React element into shell state; that is a circuit, and it is safe only while every caller hands it stable dependencies.

Option A · what we did

Fix the caller, keep the contract

const openNewBill = useCallback(
  () => setEditingBill('new'), []);

Done, tested, shipped. But the trap stays armed: the next tab that passes an inline arrow gets the same runaway, and nothing catches it until somebody opens that tab and reads the console.

Option B · recommended next

Remove the circuit

Have the shell hold a render function rather than an element, published once on mount through a ref. Then a tab re-rendering cannot write to shell state at all, and the dependency list stops being load-bearing.

// the tab registers once, and re-renders
// cannot feed back into the shell
useHeaderActions(setActions,
  () => <>…buttons…</>, [])

Six call sites, all mechanical. Worth it because the current contract asks every future caller to know a rule that nothing enforces.

What I would do, in order

  • 1 · Bucket strips on Owed to you and Bills (Decision 1B). One bucketing function, two placements, two hub cards that open them. This is the whole of "aging receivables and payables" and it adds no screens.
  • 2 · The vendor roll-up (Decision 2). The only one of the original four that shows something no screen shows. Drop Customer Balance Summary — it is the Owed to you tab.
  • 3 · Ledger exports for the five tables (Decision 4). Mechanical now that text and ReportExport exist. PDF only for the three statements.
  • 4 · Remove the header-actions circuit (Decision 5). Before another tab is added, not after.
  • 5 · Card heights (Decision 3), as its own change verified on web and iPhone, because BentoGrid carries Dashboard too.

Deliberately not on this list: Discounts & Refunds and Budget vs Actual, which are real gaps but need no decision — they are ordinary reports over data that exists. And purchase orders, which is not a reporting question at all: kaiibi has no PO model, and whether it should is a product call to make on its own.
