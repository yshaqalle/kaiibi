Design mockup · POS

POS, at the counter

Four screens are converted and all four arrived at the same rule: grid for glancing, flow for scanning. POS is neither. It is the one screen that is operated — under time pressure, with a customer waiting, sometimes one-handed. So the question here is not which layout the design prefers. It is what the palette and the card shape can do without costing a tap.

The third case

Unchanged: the two panes, and the phone's order

Browse left, cart right. On a phone the cart renders above the grid — already true in the code, and it is the right call: mid-sale the basket is what you are checking, and burying it under a product grid means scrolling past everything you just added. Nothing about bento improves on that, so nothing about bento touches it.

Changed: the total is the loudest thing on screen

30px, tabular, above a 2px black rule. Everything else in the summary is 13px grey. This is the number the customer is told out loud and the one that gets a sale wrong if it is misread — it should not be the same size as "Subtotal".

Changed carefully: tile radius is 18px, not 26

BENTO_RADIUS is 26 and that is right for a panel. On a 150px product tile it eats the corners and the tile starts reading as a pill. This is the first place the system needs a second radius, and I would rather name it than quietly break the rule.

The risk worth arguing about: contrast at the counter

Every other converted screen is read at a desk. This one is read in shop lighting, at arm's length, often in a hurry. Cool grey on white is a quieter palette than the cream one it replaces — and the product tile carries the most information per pixel of anything in the app.

So the tiles here keep a bentoLine border rather than floating borderless like the panels do. If it still reads soft on a real counter, the honest fix is a firmer tile border, not more colour.

Touch targets are the real constraint, not styling. The quantity stepper is 30px circles with a 12px gap — bigger than the 26px the Inventory table uses, because Inventory is adjusted occasionally at a desk and this is pressed repeatedly at a counter. The "Complete sale" button is 54px tall and full-width. Those numbers are the design; the palette is just what they are painted in.

What this needs: a BENTO_RADIUS_TILE (18) beside the existing 26, and nothing else new. Search, chips, cards, the black primary action and the sheet all already exist from the last four conversions.

What I have deliberately not designed here: the receipt modal and the barcode scanner overlay. Both are full-screen takeovers with their own rules, and both deserve their own look rather than being swept into a palette pass.
