The shareable link — what works, what doesn’t

There are two links in this product and only one of them exists. The link a shop sends to bring customers in works, as of today. The link a customer would use to follow the order they placed has never been built.

Link A — the shop’s address

What a shop sends to a customer to say “this is my shop”. Fixed today. Until this morning the app displayed and copied an address that could not resolve, so a shop that published, pressed Share and sent the link watched it fail.

  1. The shop publishes its page

    works

    Editor, theme, flyers, delivery areas. One shop of eleven has done this.

  2. The shop copies its link

    fixed today

    Seven places built this address; they now route through one function, so changing its form later is a single edit.

    Copy link · Share on WhatsApp
    yusefshop.kaiibi.com
    kaiibi.com/store/yusefshop

  3. The customer opens it

    verified live

    Loaded in a real browser against production: the shop renders with its products, prices, stock, basket and WhatsApp button.

  4. The customer places an order

    works

    Delivery or store pick-up. Pick-up used to be invisible when it was the only option — a customer was never told the order was a collection, or where to collect. Fixed today.

    (no fulfilment choice shown at all)

  5. The shop is told

    works

    A count rides the ☰ button, so it reaches whoever is standing at the till rather than only someone already looking at the orders screen.

  6. The shop fulfils it

    unblocked today

    A shop whose till requires an open register could not complete any online order. A driver collecting cash across town has no drawer, so requiring one refused the sale and protected nothing.

  7. The customer follows their order

    not built

    There is no route, no token, and no way to read an order back. Once an order is placed the customer is blind to it, and every “where is my order?” is a phone call.

    Would be
    kaiibi.com/o/<token>

  8. The shop amends it

    not built

    A shop short on stock has exactly one move: cancel the whole order. There is no way to reduce it to what is on the shelf, change a quantity, or fix a mistyped phone number. Not a missing screen — a missing capability.

    Why it is not a small change. Signed-in users have no write privilege on orders at all, completion is all-or-nothing, and one order may produce only one sale. So “send some now, the rest Thursday” cannot be two sales against one order.

Link B — the customer’s order

Steps 7 and 8 are one feature, not three asks: partial fulfilment is amending, and the link is what makes amending safe — it is how the customer sees what changed without a phone call. It is designed and specified; none of it is built.

Left to doSizeWhy it is not done
The order link itself not built Needs a token that cannot be guessed — order numbers are sequential per shop, so #1042 proves #1041 exists and no public address may use them.
Amending an order not built Every change must go through a privileged function, and prices must be recomputed at today’s prices — an amend that trusts the frozen snapshot builds an order that can never be completed.
The Orders screen redesign not built Today it is a chip row and one bare table; a shop cannot tell which of seven orders is urgent without opening seven sheets.
Which address form is canonical deferred Deliberately parked. Cheap now that the address shown actually works — it is a preference about how the link reads, not a defect.
Bulk “list online” half a day Four products across eleven shops. A customer cannot order what is not listed, and the per-product toggle is the likeliest reason so little is.
Native beyond the ☰ menu now cheap The lapse flow, the editor and order fulfilment have never run on a phone. The port problem that blocked this is fixed.
Every state above was checked against the repo, the database or a running app today — not recalled. Link A step 3 was loaded against production in a real browser; steps 7 and 8 were confirmed absent by searching for the route, the token and the read function.
