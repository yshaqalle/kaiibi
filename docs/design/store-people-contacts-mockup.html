Kaiibi · Platform console · store identity & contacts

Who is this store, and who do I call?

The console knows what every store pays and what it uses. It does not know who runs it. Today an operator can see “Hooyo Market · Standard · trial ends Tuesday” and still have no name to greet, no address to write to, and one switchboard number that may or may not reach a person. This adds the people.

The whole change in one line: a store stops being a billing row and becomes a business with an owner who has a name, an email, and a number you can tap to open WhatsApp — plus the rest of the team behind them.

1 · What we hold today, and what we are missing

Nothing here needs new data collected. Every field below already exists in the database — the console simply never reads it. That matters: this is a permissions and surfacing job, not a “please fill in your profile” campaign aimed at stores.

FieldOn the console todayWhere it already lives
Store nameYes shops.name
Store phoneYes shop_locations.contact_phone of the primary branch — the number on their receipts
CityRead, never shown shop_locations.city of the primary branch — already on PlatformShopRow, fetched on every load, drawn in exactly zero places
Every branch, and whereNo — filtered out shop_locations name + city + phone. The operator policy already covers all rows; listPlatformShops throws them away with .eq('is_primary', true)
Owner’s nameNo shop_members.full_name — every shop has an owner row (backfilled 20260823), kept in step with profiles.full_name by trigger
Owner’s emailNo shop_members.email, denormalised from auth.users at provision time
Owner’s personal phoneNo shop_members.phone (20260819000200) — often null, and that is fine; the store number is the fallback
The rest of the teamNo shop_members × roles — name, email, phone, role name, active flag

The one blocker. shop_members has a single policy — admin manages shop_members using owns_shop(shop_id). An operator is not the owner of anyone’s shop, so today the console reads exactly nothing from it. That is the migration this design needs, and section 6 keeps it as narrow as the support-author fix was.

Three rules the table follows

  1. The owner’s name goes in the Store cell, not a column of its own. It is an attribute of “which store is this”, and the existing NameCell already carries a meta line. A fourth identity column would push Ends and Products off the edge on a 13″ laptop.
  2. Contact replaces Joined, which moves to the drawer. A signup date is read once a quarter; a number is read every time the row is opened. Joined survives as created in the drawer’s header line, where it already is.
  3. Search widens to people and places. shops-tab.tsx’s filter gains owner name, email, phone and every branch city, so typing “faadumo”, “burco” or the last four digits of a number finds the store. Operators think in people and towns, and the search box currently refuses both.
  4. The city is the primary branch’s, with a count when there are more. “Burco +2” rather than three towns competing with the owner’s name for one line. The full list is in the drawer.

What the People section decides

  1. The owner is in full; the team is one row. The owner is the person who can say yes to a plan change, so their contact details are the thing the drawer owes you at a glance. Everyone else is a question you sometimes have — so it gets a row that answers it in a tap, and section 4 is where the answer lives.
  2. “4 of 11 seats” is the section scope, not a Usage row. The count and the list are the same fact; showing them apart is how “Staff 4 / 11” ended up meaningless.
  3. The summary line names names. “3 others” is a number. “Maxamed, Sahra, Nasra · 1 who has left” is often the whole answer, and saves the tap entirely.
  4. No actions on a person, anywhere. The console cannot deactivate a member, change their role, or reset a password. This is a directory, and section 6 keeps it one at the database level too.

Why this shapeand what each part is for

One modal, two views

The drawer already owns a full-screen sheet on a tablet and a bottom sheet on a phone. Pushing the team inside it means the back arrow returns you to the store with your reason field, your plan chips and your scroll position intact — which a second modal on top of the first would not.

Grouped by whether they still work there

Two headings, not a status column. Working here is who you can talk to; No longer here answers “I spoke to Cabdi in June, what happened?” and is deliberately contact-free — WhatsApping an ex-employee about their former employer’s billing is not something the UI should make easy.

Tap a person, get the rest

The row carries what you scan for — name, role, email, phone. The expanded panel carries what you occasionally need: when they were signed up, and what their role is actually allowed to do, which is the question behind “can Maxamed change the plan himself?” (he cannot; only the owner can).

What it never says

shop_members also holds pay_type, pay_rate_cents, hire_date and photo_url. None of them are returned — not hidden in the UI, absent from the function. Kaiibi operators have no business knowing what a shop pays its cashier, and the only way to guarantee that is to never send it.

Which branch each person can work at

A chip on the row: Hooyo Koodbuur for someone tied to one branch, Both branches for someone who is not. It answers the question a multi-branch store’s owner actually asks us — “why can’t Nasra see the Koodbuur till?” — without the console being able to change a single assignment.

⚠
Empty means everywhere, not nowhere. can_access_location() gives a member with no rows in shop_member_locations access to every branch. A UI that renders an empty array as “no branches” states the exact opposite of what the database enforces — so the label is computed from the array’s length, and the owner is hard-coded to “every branch” because their access comes from owns_shop() and not from an assignment row at all.
⚠
“Signed up by the owner” is nearly true, so we don’t say it. Nothing records who added a member, and the write policy is staff.manage — which a Manager can hold. So the header says “signed up by the store”, and each row gives the date it can actually prove.

Which number it dials, in order

  1. The owner’s personal phone (shop_members.phone) — the person who can say yes to a plan change.
  2. The store’s number (shop_locations.contact_phone) — today’s behaviour, kept as the fallback, not removed.
  3. Neither → the WhatsApp glyph is not drawn at all and the mail glyph takes its place, because every owner row has an email by construction. An outline glyph with a dashed edge is used only where the layout needs the slot held (the Stores table), never as a dead button you can press.

The prefilled first line does not change — whatsappLink() in src/lib/whatsapp.ts already normalises the number and carries the message. What changes is that it is now addressed to Faadumo rather than to “Hooyo Market”, so the greeting can use her name.

Accessibility. The glyph is a button with aria-label="WhatsApp Faadumo Cabdi" and a 44 pt hit target, so the word that was removed from the screen is still there for a screen reader. Colour is never the only signal: an unavailable action is absent, not greyed.

6 · Where the data comes from, and what it is not allowed to become

One migration, one function, no new table and no new column. It follows support_author_profiles() exactly — the fix that was already made once for the same class of problem.

-- Operators read the roster of any shop: names, work contact, role. -- A function rather than a policy, for 20260825000400's reason exactly: the -- table-wide select grant on shop_members means a row-scoped policy would -- ALSO hand back pay_type, pay_rate_cents, hire_date and photo_url -- the -- exact columns src/lib/staff.ts already routes around for the shop's own -- managers. This returns the nine the console draws and nothing else. create or replace function public.platform_shop_people(p_shop_ids uuid[]) returns table (shop_id uuid, user_id uuid, full_name text, email text, phone text, role_name text, role_permissions text[], is_owner boolean, active boolean, joined_at timestamptz, branch_names text[]) language sql stable security definer set search_path = public as $$ select m.shop_id, m.user_id, m.full_name, m.email, m.phone, r.name, r.permissions, (s.owner_id = m.user_id), m.active, m.created_at, -- Empty array = every branch. NOT "no branches" -- can_access_location() -- (20260814000000) treats a member with no assignment rows as having -- access to all of them, so the label is computed from length, and -- getting it backwards would tell an operator the opposite of the truth. coalesce((select array_agg(l.name order by l.is_primary desc, l.name) from public.shop_member_locations ml join public.shop_locations l on l.id = ml.location_id where ml.shop_member_id = m.id), '{}') from public.shop_members m join public.roles r on r.id = m.role_id join public.shops s on s.id = m.shop_id where public.is_platform_admin() -- aal2 enforced inside and m.shop_id = any(p_shop_ids); $$; -- PUBLIC gets execute on every new function, which on a definer function -- means anon. Revoked first, so the grant is the whole list of who can call it. revoke execute on function public.platform_shop_people(uuid[]) from public; grant execute on function public.platform_shop_people(uuid[]) to authenticated;

Four properties this holds

  1. Read-only, forever. shop_members gains no operator write policy and platform-admin gains no action that touches it. An operator cannot add, remove, rename or deactivate anybody’s staff. If we ever want that, it is a separate decision with its own audit-log entry, not a side effect of a directory.
  2. MFA still gates it. is_platform_admin() demands aal2, so a stolen operator password alone reads nothing — the same bar every other console read sits behind.
  3. It is work contact, not private life. Everything returned is what the shop typed into its own Staff screen for its own team: the name on the receipt, the address we already email invoices to, the number the shop itself printed. Pay is absent from the return type, along with hire date and photo — not filtered in the client, not selected in the first place.
  4. The privacy line in the drawer stays true. “This portal cannot open this store’s products, sales, books, or schedule” is unchanged — a roster is not trade. The line in the drawer gains one clause: …and it shows who works here so we can reach them, nothing they have recorded.
⚠
Batched, not per row. The console loads once and holds every store in memory, so this is one call passing every shop id — the same shape listSupportThreads() uses for author profiles. A per-drawer fetch would be N+1 against the busiest screen in the portal and would make opening a store feel slow for no reason.

One index. shop_members_shop_id_idx already exists (0017), which is exactly what shop_id = any(...) needs. Nothing to add.

8 · What this is, in build order

  1. Migration — platform_shop_people(), revoke, grant. No schema change.
  2. Data layer — listShopPeople(shopIds) in src/lib/platform.ts, returning ShopPerson[] grouped by shop; PlatformShopRow gains owner: ShopPerson | null so the table and Overview read one field rather than searching a list.
  3. Branches — drop .eq('is_primary', true) from the shop_locations read and keep the rows as branches: Branch[], primary first. No migration: the operator policy already covers every row, and city is already being fetched. This is a filter coming off, not access going on.
  4. WhatsApp glyph — one small <WhatsAppButton phone message label /> component wrapping the existing whatsappLink(), replacing the text pill on Overview and used unchanged everywhere else.
  5. Stores tab — owner + city in the Store cell, Contact column in place of Joined, search widened to people.
  6. Store drawer — People above Usage (owner in full, team as one row, seat count as the scope), then Where they trade listing each branch with its city and phone. The Staff and Branches rows come out of Usage, since both sections now say it with names.
  7. Team view — a second view inside the same modal, with the back arrow, the two groups, the branch chip on every row, and the tap-to-expand detail.
  8. Overview — Needs-attention rows name the owner and use the glyph.

Steps 4–7 are independent of each other once the data layer lands, so each screen can be reviewed on its own rather than as one large diff.
