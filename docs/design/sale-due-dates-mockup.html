A credit sale says when it comes back

Today a sale on account has an age and no deadline. The shop can see that Amina has owed $1,240 for thirty-four days, but not whether thirty-four days is late — because nothing ever said when it was due. This adds the missing half: a due date, defaulted from a shop-wide term, stamped by the database, and shown everywhere the debt already is.

The whole design turns on one restraint: the common case is zero taps. A cashier ringing up a credit sale should not be asked for a date. The shop already has a policy — thirty days — so the control states the answer and offers to change it.

Change — an inline expansion, not a sheet

Named terms rather than a calendar. They exist because "in two weeks" is how a shopkeeper actually thinks about a variation, and making them count the days on a calendar to express it is the tax the shop-wide default was meant to remove.

Changed during implementation, on purpose. This was drawn as a bottom sheet. On a phone the pay-later control already sits inside the checkout sheet, and a sheet opened from a sheet is dropped on iOS — this repo's own documented trap, the one that looks like a dead button. So the options expand inline within the card instead. Same four choices, no second surface, and no useStagedSheet plumbing for a control this small. The shop default is labelled as the shop default. A cashier who opens this sheet and sees the chosen row say shop default learns the policy exists and that they are about to depart from it — which is most of what stops a per-sale override from quietly becoming the norm.

The override is per sale and does not stick. The next sale is 30 days again. A sticky override is a setting that nobody knows they changed.

On collection is today's date rather than a null. A due date that is sometimes absent puts the aging column back in the position this work exists to get it out of — two different measurements in one bucket.

2 · The shop-wide term

One field in Settings. It is the only place a shop states its credit policy.

Selling on account

Payment term
How long a customer has to settle a credit sale. Applied to every new sale on account; any one sale can be given a different date at the till.
30 days

A stepper rather than a free number field: the realistic range is 7 to 90 in weekly-ish steps, and a text input invites 0, 3650 and an empty string, each of which needs a rule nobody wants to write or read. Changing it affects sales made after the change — existing due dates are already stamped and are not recomputed, which is the point of stamping them.

The two badges

Late is amber-washed — a task, not a loss, the same reading the 90+ tile already takes. Due soon is grey and means within seven days; it is the one that lets a shop ring ahead instead of chasing afterwards. A row comfortably ahead of its date gets no badge at all, because a badge on every row is a badge on none. Both carry a word, so neither depends on its colour.

4 · The meaning that changes under someone's feet

This is the part to say out loud when it ships, not to bury in a changelog.

The aging strip stops measuring age and starts measuring lateness. The four buckets, their boundaries and their reconciliation are untouched — src/lib/aging.ts takes a days accessor and never knew where the number came from, so receivables simply pass a different one. But every figure in the strip will move on the day this lands, and a customer who was in 60 – 89 days yesterday can be in Current today without paying a shilling. That is the improvement. It is also a number somebody may already be reading weekly.

One thing the brief and the code disagree about

The intent was recorded as: "Current" stops meaning "under 30 days old" and starts meaning "not yet due". With the bucketing left alone that is not quite what happens. bucketForDays puts everything under 30 into current, and daysSince clamps negatives to zero — so a sale not yet due and a sale twenty days late both land in Current.

Leave the bucketing alone
Current means under 30 days past due. The hint under the tile has to say that, not "not yet due". A shop chasing a two-week-late debt won't find it in a bucket, but it is on the list with a Late badge and a Past due figure, so nothing is hidden — it is just not bucketed separately.
Split the first bucket
Current means not yet due, and a new 1 – 29 days bucket holds the freshly late. Truer to the words, and five tiles still fit the 2×2 wrap badly. This is a change to AGING_BUCKETS and its reconciliation test, which the brief ruled out.

Drawn as the first. The strip above says under 30 days past due and the tab is titled How late the money is. It respects the "don't touch the bucketing" constraint, and the honest hint costs nothing. Worth a look before I build it, since the wording in the brief implies the second.

5 · Underneath

Migration 20261026000000. Nothing here is visible; all of it is why the screens can be simple.

PieceShapeWhy this way
shops.credit_term_days int not null default 30 The policy, in one place. A check constraint keeps it in a sane range so the stepper's bounds are not the only thing enforcing them.
sales.due_on date, nullable in the column, never null in practice Nullable so the trigger has something to fill. due_on is already the name this concept carries on invoices, so the two sides of the ledger agree on their vocabulary.
Trigger before insert on public.sales, fills due_on only when null Not a change to complete_sale. That function has been reproduced verbatim nineteen times and the repo has already lost an edit that way. A trigger is a write path that cannot be forgotten, and filling only a null leaves the per-sale override working. sales already carries two before-insert triggers, so this is the established pattern rather than a new one.
The date it counts from shop_local_date() + credit_term_days Not now()::date. Somalia is UTC+3, so an evening sale would otherwise come due a day early. shop_local_date() already exists for exactly this and its own comments explain why there is no shops.timezone.
Backfill Every unsettled sale gets created_at + 30 So the aging column measures one thing from day one. The alternative — aging from the due date where there is one and the sale date otherwise — is two measurements in one bucket, the exact failure the aging work avoided. The context caveat on the tab says the historic dates were derived.
customer_balances Gains due_on The view is already per unsettled sale, which is the right grain — the earliest due date per customer is then a grouping concern, not a query one.

One client-side consequence. groupByCustomer in src/lib/receivables.ts collapses sales to one row per person and currently tracks oldestAt. It gains earliestDueOn alongside it, tracked the same way. That is the accessor the aging strip is handed — aging.ts itself is untouched.

6 · Deliberately not in this

Not doingWhy not
The useHeaderActions circuit Stays dropped. The live instance is fixed and the trap is pinned by header-actions-loop.test.tsx. Redraw it when an Accounting tab is actually being added — the earlier recommendation for it was wrong and it should not be built as previously written.
Touching AGING_BUCKETS or the reconciliation test Out of scope by decision. See §4 for the one place that decision is visible.
Overdue on the Bills side Bills already carry due_on and already flag overdue. This brings receivables level with payables, not past them.
Reminders, interest, statements A due date is the prerequisite for all three and none of them is this change.
Recomputing existing due dates when the term changes A stamped date is a commitment made to a customer. Editing history because a setting moved is how a ledger stops being one.
