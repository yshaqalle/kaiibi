Count, and who is allowed to

The fourth door, disabled since the Stock sheet shipped. Its one‑line description — “replaces the count with what you actually found — 11 becomes 8, and the app records the −3” — is not enough to build from, because the interesting part is not the 8. It is what the −3 means, where it goes, and who is allowed to write it.

1 · Why Count is the dangerous door

The four doors are not peers in risk, and the disabled one is the sharp one:

DoorWhat it does to valueReversible by
RestockAdds units, against an invoicea corrective Count
MoveRelocates. Shop total unchangedmoving it back
ImportCreates catalogue rowsdeleting the product
CountDestroys units, against nothingnothing — the stock is gone

A Restock overstating by 40 is caught by the invoice. A Count writing 11 down to 8 has no counterparty and no paperwork — it is one person's word that three units are not there. That is why it is the door that most needs a reason attached, a record kept, and a permission of its own.

And today the app already loses this money silently

Per‑product stock can already be edited inline on the Inventory list — that is a count, one product at a time, with no reason, no record and no P&L effect. Cost only reaches the P&L through sale_items.unit_cost_cents at sale time, so stock that vanishes is never costed at all. It leaves Stock at cost and never appears as an expense. Gross profit reads higher than it is, by exactly the cost of everything that walked out.

Building Count is therefore not only adding a door. It is the first time the shop gets told what its shrinkage costs.

Difference 1 — the field is pre‑filled with what the app believes

Restock's quantity starts empty, because zero received is the honest default. Count's starts at the current figure, because a stock‑take mostly confirms. A row left untouched means “I looked, it matched” — which is real information and is why the footer counts 3 counted while only 2 change anything.

Difference 2 — the variance is a column, not a footnote

−3 in red next to the number you typed, computed live. The thing a person doing a stock‑take needs to see is not the 8 — they just counted it, they know. It is how far off the app was, because that is the number they will be asked about.

“Save counts”, never “Adjust”

Save is what a person doing a stock‑take thinks they are doing. “Adjust stock” is the system's word for it, and it is the vocabulary that makes people reach for the wrong door in the first place.

4 · Reasons, and the honest default

Five reasons, per line, optional: Damaged, Expired, Theft or loss, Miscount, Other.

Optional, and the gap is reported rather than filled

Requiring a reason on every one of 16 variances is how a 300‑line stock‑take stops getting done, or gets done with sixteen Miscounts that mean nothing. So a blank reason is allowed — and the preview says “9 with no reason” out loud, because unexplained shrinkage is itself the finding. A shop that sees that pill every month knows something the app cannot tell it.

What is not allowed is defaulting the blank ones to Miscount. That is a precise‑looking answer to a question nobody asked — the same instinct migration 20260804000000 followed when it refused to backfill historical costs.

Per line, not per count

One stock‑take finds different causes on different shelves: sun cream expired, toner walked. A single reason on the whole count would be wrong on almost every line it covered. The count carries a free‑text note; the lines carry reasons.

A positive variance is not a reason to celebrate

+2 found means the app was wrong too, and it is usually a missed Restock or a mis‑scanned sale. It takes the same reason list. Shown in green because it is arithmetically the other direction — but the footer nets them (−1 unit, not “3 lost, 2 found”) so nobody reads a good day into it.

5 · Where the loss goes

This is the part that is currently silently wrong, and it is worth being precise about the mechanism.

Stock that vanishes is never costed

COGS is built from sale_items.unit_cost_cents — frozen at sale time. A unit that is stolen, breaks, or expires is never sold, so its cost never enters COGS by any path. It simply leaves Stock at cost and is gone.

The shop paid $142.80 for stock it no longer has, and the P&L never hears about it. Gross profit reads higher than it is by exactly that amount, every month, invisibly.

The proposal: an unticked checkbox, exactly like Restock's inventory‑purchase one — “Also log $142.80 of shortfall as stock loss” — writing one expense per store under a new category. expense-reporting.ts has inventory_purchase (“Inventory restock”) but nothing for shrinkage; this needs stock_loss adding.

Unticked, for the same reason Restock's is — but the argument is weaker here

Restock's is unticked because a shop entering supplier invoices in Accounting separately would double‑count. There is no equivalent double‑count risk for shrinkage: nothing else in the app or in a shop's paperwork records it. So the case for defaulting this one on is real, and the case against is only that a silent write is a surprise. Worth your call; drawn unticked to match its sibling.

Only when the value is knowable

Shortfall is valued at cost — and cost is currently latest‑wins and shop‑wide (see inventory-costing-mockup.html). So $142.80 is as approximate as Stock at cost is. And any line whose product is uncosted contributes nothing, so a count full of uncosted products would offer to log a shortfall far smaller than the real one. The checkbox must hide, not lie, when the count contains uncosted lines — and say why.

6 · Who is allowed

Today all four doors sit behind one permission, inventory.edit. So anyone who can receive a delivery can also write stock off, move it between branches, and rewrite the catalogue. Those are different levels of trust, and Count is the one that destroys value.

Already true and worth knowing: a Cashier is already read‑only. The seeded role is ['pos.access', 'inventory.view'] (0020_default_roles.sql), the Stock button is gated on inventory.edit, so a cashier never sees this sheet at all. The gap is not read‑only — it is the all‑or‑nothing inside edit.

Two new permissions, not four

inventory.count and inventory.transfer, both nested under inventory.edit and both defaulting on for every role that already has it — so no existing shop's staff loses anything on the day this ships. Restock stays the base meaning of inventory.edit, because receiving is the ordinary case and a permission everyone turns on is not a permission.

Catalogue editing (+ Add product, Import, price and cost) wants separating too, but that is a wider change than this door — drawn here to show where it sits, not proposed for this pass.

Nesting has to be real, not visual

Turning Change stock off must turn both children off and disable them, and the RPC must check the child permission itself — not merely rely on the UI. has_shop_permission already does exactly this shape for every other gate; the sheet must not be the only thing standing between a cashier and a write‑off.

7 · Open questions

Four decisions, then I can write the plan

1. The stock-loss expense — unticked as drawn, ticked by default, or not built this pass? There is no double‑count risk, which is the argument for defaulting it on.

2. Partial counts. A stock‑take of one shelf leaves 200 products uncounted. Drawn as: uncounted products are simply not in the count and keep their numbers. The alternative — treating a whole‑store count as authoritative and zeroing anything absent — is a foot‑gun I would not build.

3. Does a count need approving? Drawn as: no. One person counts and saves. A shop that wants a second pair of eyes gets it by not granting inventory.count widely — which is what §6 is for. Approval workflow is a bigger feature and I would let a shop ask for it.

4. Permissions in this pass, or after? They are separable, but shipping Count without inventory.count means every shop that has ever granted inventory.edit gets write‑off on the same day — and taking it back later is a conversation.

Independent of those: this needs a stock_counts / stock_count_items pair and a save_stock_count RPC, mirroring stock_receipts and receive_stock. A count that only writes numbers leaves “who said these three were gone, and when?” unanswerable — which is the whole reason to build the door rather than keep editing counts inline.
