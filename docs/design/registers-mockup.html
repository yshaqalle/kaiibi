Registers: who is on the register, and does the drawer add up

A sale currently records a branch, an auth user, and cashier_name — a frozen string copied from a list that has no link to any account. So the shop cannot answer the one question that matters at handover: who was on Register 2 this afternoon, what did they start with, what did they hand over, and is it short? This adds a register (a named counter) and a register session (one open→close cycle, with a count at each end).

Not called a shift. shifts already means the People rota — who is scheduled to come in — and time_entries already means clocked hours. Reusing either word for a cash boundary would collide with a live, different meaning in the same app. It is a session.

The three ways POS can behave with no session, and which one ships

BehaviourWhat the cashier seesVerdict
Hard block, always POS refuses to sell until a register is opened. No. Every existing shop is mid-trade. Shipping this on by default breaks a working counter to introduce a feature nobody asked that shop for.
Nudge (default) A dashed bar offering Open register. Sales work; they simply carry a null register_session_id. Ships on. The bar is an invitation. A shop adopts registers when it wants them, not when we decide.
Hard block, opt-in Same refusal, behind shops.require_open_register, default false. Ships off. The shop that wants accountability needs this to be enforceable, or staff will simply skip the count on a busy day.

The decisions worth arguing about

Count before reveal

The close sheet takes the counted cash before it shows what the register expected. If the expected figure is on screen first, a tired cashier at the end of a shift types it back in and the variance is structurally always zero — the feature then costs a minute a day and detects nothing.

They can still step back and change the number after the reveal. The design does not prevent that; it just refuses to lead with the answer. And a non-zero variance requires a note, so an unexplained gap cannot be silently accepted at 9pm and puzzled over on Friday.

A register is durable; a session is not. That is the whole distinction

registers.id is a stable uuid that outlives every session opened on it. register_sessions.id is one open→close cycle. Collapse the two — create a register when a session starts and drop it at close — and every session becomes its own island: "is Register 2 short three days running", "which register takes the most on a Friday", "this phone seller's variances are all in one direction" all stop being answerable. The durable id is the reporting.

It is not necessarily physical. A register is "a durable named place a sale is rung from" — usually a counter with a drawer, sometimes a person's phone. Both are the same row with a kind of 'counter' or 'mobile', and a mobile one is auto-provisioned the first time someone opens a session with no counter free, named after them and reused thereafter. What differs is how much of the close sheet applies, not the shape of the data.

Location stays not null on both. A phone seller is still selling somewhere, and stock, takings and staff access in this app are all scoped by branch — a register with no branch would be a hole in every one of those.

The sale records the session, and deliberately not the register

sales.register_session_id alone. The register is one join away through the session, and unlike the things this schema does freeze — cashier_name, unit_cost_cents, exchange_rate — register_id is an immutable FK: a session can never move to a different register. Those snapshots exist because the value drifts; copying a stable id just creates a second place for the truth to live and a way for the two to disagree.

The cost is one join on "takings by register", answered with an index on register_sessions(register_id) rather than a column on the busiest table in the app. If that ever measures badly on real data, denormalise then — with a number to point at.

The session carries the employee — there is no separate assignment table

"Assign Amina to Register 2" and "Amina opened Register 2 with $120" are the same event. Storing them separately means they can disagree, and then neither one can be trusted for the money. Whoever opens a register is the person on it; a manager holding registers.manage can open it for someone else, which is the real-world case (the supervisor sets the float, the cashier works the counter).

Planning ahead — "Amina works Tuesday morning" — is what shifts already does. Building a second rota here would be two answers to one question.

Handover is close-and-reopen, not reassign-in-place

Reassigning an open session in place leaves one drawer count spanning two people, so neither can be held to it. The point of the count is the boundary.

But making that two separate flows (close, then find the open sheet, then re-count the same money) is the kind of friction that gets skipped. So Handover is one sheet: count once, that figure closes the outgoing session and becomes the incoming float, and both names are on it.

The float defaults to the last close, and that is a claim

Pre-filling the opening float with the previous session's counted figure is right almost always — it is physically the same money in the same drawer overnight. It is wrong the day someone banks the takings, and then the pre-fill is a number the opener has to notice and correct.

Mitigated by showing its provenance under the field ("Register 1 closed at $118.50 yesterday 21:04") rather than presenting it as a bare default. If it still causes wrong floats in practice, the honest fix is to default to empty, not to hide the provenance.

The risk: mobile money is not in the drawer, and the close sheet says so twice

ZAAD and e-Dahab are most of the takings in a lot of these shops, and a close sheet that shows a big "total sales" figure next to a small counted-cash figure invites exactly the wrong subtraction. So the reveal keeps the two apart structurally: the expected-cash arithmetic contains only cash lines, and the mobile-money totals appear separately as chips under the heading Not in the drawer.

Related, and worth stating because it is a real trap in this codebase: sale_payments.tendered_cents is only written when change was given, so it is null for exact-tender cash and cannot be summed. Expected cash sums amount_cents on cash lines, minus cash refunds against the session.

Note by note is the count that actually happens

Nobody counts a drawer by arriving at one number. They count a stack of twenties, then the tens, and hold a running sum in their head — which is where the errors come from at 9pm. So the tally lets them enter what they are physically holding ($20 × 4) and does the arithmetic. The running total is the counted figure; there is no separate confirm-the-number step to get out of step with the rows above it.

It is a toggle, not a replacement. A kiosk with six notes in the drawer does not want seven rows, and forcing the tally on them would make the fast path slower. The choice is remembered per device, the way kaiibi.activeLocationId already is.

The last row is the one that makes it usable: a plain amount for coins, shillings and anything that is not one of the shop's notes. Without it the tally can disagree with the drawer and the cashier has no way to say so — and a count you cannot reconcile is worse than a count you had to do in your head.

Enter amount + currency; compare per currency; convert only the variance

A Hargeisa drawer holds dollars and shillings at once, so the count is a list of amount + currency lines — add as many as the drawer holds — at open and at close alike. One combined figure is shown, because the person closing wants to know whether the drawer is right, not to do two separate reconciliations.

The order of operations is what makes that safe. Each currency is differenced against its own expected figure first, with no rate involved; only the resulting variances are converted and summed. Convert the balances instead and difference those, and a rate drifting 115 → 118 reports about $80 of variance on a 355,000 Sl Sh drawer where every note stayed put — a fabricated accusation against whoever was on the register. Converting a −$5 variance at 115 or at 118 changes it by pennies.

It also fixes a bug in the first draft of this plan: expected cash cannot be "sum amount_cents where method='cash'". On a shilling payment amount_cents is the dollar equivalent applied to the sale, while the money that entered the drawer is foreign_amount_cents. Dollars expected must therefore exclude cash lines carrying a currency_code, and shillings expected is foreign_amount_cents − foreign_change_cents over that currency's lines.

Two rate snapshots per session, and the FX movement is reported separately

The rate is captured at open and again at close, on the session itself, rather than read live whenever a report is run. Without that, reopening last Tuesday's close next month re-converts it at today's rate and the signed-off figure quietly changes — the same reason sale_payments.exchange_rate and sale_items.unit_cost_cents are already snapshots in this schema.

Having both ends also buys something the shop cannot currently see at all: the drift between them is the day's exchange exposure on the float. 355,000 Sl Sh held across a 115 → 118 move lost about $78 of value with every note still in the drawer. That belongs on the close sheet — it is real money — but in its own card, never folded into the variance. Mixing them tells a cashier they are $83 short when they are $5 short.

Rates default from shop_currencies.rate_to_usd and are editable at the point of counting, because the street rate moves faster than anyone updates Settings.

The note list is a starting point, and the drawer can always overrule it

$1/5/10/20/50/100 is right for a dollar drawer and meaningless for shillings, where the notes run 100 to 10,000. So the denominations are data, not a constant: shops.cash_denominations as a jsonb map keyed by currency code, seeded {"USD":[…],"SLSH":[…]} and editable in Settings → Registers.

But a seeded list will always be wrong somewhere, and the moment it is, the cashier is holding a note the app refuses to acknowledge — so it goes in the catch-all, the breakdown stops meaning anything, and the one artefact that solves a variance next week is gone. + Add a note fixes that where the problem actually happens: in the drawer, with the note in your hand. It takes effect immediately for this count, and offers to remember it — only to someone with settings.access, since remembering it edits a shop setting.

This needs no schema: closing_denominations is a jsonb map keyed by note value, so an unseeded 10,000 is simply another key. The stored count is a record of what was in the drawer, never a foreign key into a list that may have changed since.

Every currency also gets the plain-amount Other row, dollars and shillings alike — coins, a torn note, a foreign scrap. It is what lets the tally always reconcile to the drawer instead of quietly disagreeing with it.

A jsonb map rather than a column on shop_currencies because USD is deliberately not a row in that table — migration 0015 makes it the implicit default when currency_code is null. Splitting the list across a shop column for dollars and a currency column for everything else would be two homes for one setting.

Colour never carries the variance on its own

Every variance shows a sign, a word (Short / Over / Balances) and a glyph as well as bentoLoss/bentoProfit. That is the bento skill's rule 4, and it matters more here than on a chart: this is the number that decides whether a conversation with an employee happens.

Implementation notes

Almost nothing new in the design system. The bar, the sheet blocks, the black primary action, the pill chips and the avatar all exist. Every field is still a plain TextInput keyboardType="number-pad" plus toCents(), the control every money field in the app already uses — the tally is rows of those, not a custom keypad. The one genuinely new primitive is the segmented One figure / Note by note switch, and SegmentedControl in src/components/segmented-control.tsx already is that.

Radii: the sheet is the page (--d-pg, 24) and the blocks inside it are cards (--d-sf, 16), matching the checkout sheet in pos-mockup.html. The register bar and the register chooser rows take BENTO_RADIUS_TILE (18/16), not 26 — same reason product tiles do.

Settings → Registers is drawn in the grayscale Settings palette on purpose. Settings is not a bento screen yet, and this panel should look like the Locations panel it sits beside, not like the POS.

Deliberately not designed here: mid-session pay-ins, pay-outs and cash drops; a denomination breakdown; a printed Z-report; auto-close at midnight. Each is a real request and each is its own screen.
